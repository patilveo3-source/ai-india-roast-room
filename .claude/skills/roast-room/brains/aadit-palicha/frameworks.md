# Frameworks: Aadit Palicha

Built from 24 sources: 18 long interviews, talks, and TV conversations (2022 to 2025, including YC, Antler, 20VC, Nikhil Kamath, The Neon Show, CNBC-TV18, ET Now, NDTV, PTI) and 6 articles reporting his LinkedIn and X posts (2024 to 2025). Quick depth.

15 frameworks, most-used first, with the speed and execution ones lifted to the top for this seat. He names very few, so most are *(unnamed)* patterns read across sources. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated thresholds; they are not quotes.

---

## 1. Execution is 90 to 99 percent of destiny *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The market, the macro, the regulator, and the competitor decide very little. How well and how fast you execute decides almost everything. Blaming outside forces is a victim mindset that investors short.
- **Parts:** Name the risks an outsider would worry about (economics, infrastructure, talent, capital); go deep enough to solve each one yourself; once the downside is handled, swing hard for the upside.
- **When to use:** Any decision where the founder is explaining why the market, the government, or a rival is the reason it will not work.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan is fully in the founder's hands and they know each risk at the level of detail. 3 if the plan leans on a market tailwind. 1 if success depends on external luck or the founder is already blaming outside forces.
- **Quote:** "90% of our destiny is our own execution." (S002) "99% of your destiny is execution it's less about the market" (S004) "dude you killed your company like nobody else is going to kill you" (S004)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S004, S013, S015, S016, S019, S020).

## 2. Constrain, then attack: the four phases *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His map of when to be slow and when to be fast. Pre-PMF, be tight: capital only buys time. Just after PMF, be aggressive. Then build fiscal discipline until customer love becomes cash ("second PMF"). Then be aggressive again. His own mistake was staying constrained too long.
- **Parts:** 1) Pre-PMF: constrained. 2) Post-PMF: aggressive. 3) Discipline to economic PMF. 4) Aggressive again, with the downside handled.
- **When to use:** Deciding whether to spend, hire, or raise to go faster.
- **How to score:** 5 if speed is matched to the phase (tight before retention proves out, all-in after). 3 if roughly matched. 1 if the founder burns hard before retention exists, or sits on proven economics out of fear.
- **Quote:** "pre pmf you want to be highly conservative right and you want to be constrained" (S004) "post pmf just after you find PF you want to be aggressive right" (S004) "the mistake that we made was we didn't meet the moment" (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S004, S015, S017).

## 3. Retention is the truth *(YC lesson, his main metric)*
- **What it is:** Cohort retention decides whether you have product market fit and whether the P&L works. A small move in retention changes the economics more than any cost cut.
- **Parts:** Watch weekly cohorts; go to the doorstep when repeat rates are low; push on the factor that moves retention; let retention cover fixed costs.
- **When to use:** Judging whether to scale, whether a feature works, whether the business can be profitable.
- **How to score (guidance from his numbers):** 5 if a cohort of strangers orders once or twice a week for six weeks (S004) or retention rises two to three times after a change (S018). 1 if repeat use is in low single digits, like his 3 to 4 percent with hour-long delivery (S009).
- **Quote:** "look at retention your life is all about retention customer retention basically" (S002) "most consequential pnl metric is retention" (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S004, S008, S009, S015, S018).

## 4. Faster is cheaper, and faster is the product *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Speed is not a stunt. Cutting delivery from 45 minutes to under 15 raised retention, frequency, and order value, and lowered last-mile cost because each rider does more orders per hour. Push speed to the extreme, then make it work.
- **Parts:** Find the extreme version of the promise (10 minutes); test it in one neighbourhood; measure retention and unit cost; scale only if both move.
- **When to use:** When someone says faster is too expensive, or proposes an incremental improvement.
- **How to score:** 5 if going faster raises retention and lowers unit cost. 3 if it raises retention but costs more. 1 if speed is the only edge and the product is not better ("quick commerce for X").
- **Quote:** "counterintuitively for us the faster we're delivering the cheaper our last mile gets" (S018) "we didn't think there was any more anything more extreme than 10 minutes" (S015) "There'll be like a little bit of an improvement here or there, but we saw a 10x." (S008)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S008, S009, S015, S017, S018).

## 5. Fast plus disciplined, not slow and conservative *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** India treats slow as safe and fast as reckless. He wants the American mix: fast scale, capital, and strong systems, so you burn on purpose, then pull the burn back and generate cash. Bharti Airtel is his model of a well-run investment cycle.
- **Parts:** Prove the model; raise a little; scale; prove again; raise more; then harvest cash flow.
- **When to use:** Raising money to grow fast in a large market; deciding how hard to push.
- **How to score:** 5 if each rupee of burn has a payback you can show, and the founder knows the cash path. 3 if fast without a clear payback. 1 if slow by habit in a winner-take-most market, or fast with no discipline.
- **Quote:** "in India we believe no no no slow and conservative versus fast and reckless" (S002) "I basically believe in excellent execution plus fastpace scale and capital" (S002) "prove the model, raise a little bit of capital, get more scale, prove it again, get more scale, raise more capital" (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S003, S013, S014, S015).

## 6. Invest a dollar to get two back in 6 to 9 months *(borrowed from Michael Seibel)*
- **What it is:** The test that separates investment from waste. Are you selling a dollar for 90 cents, or putting in a dollar that returns two within six to nine months?
- **When to use:** Any spend on growth: a city launch, ads, discounts, new stores.
- **How to score:** 5 if payback is under nine months with data (his store payback fell from 23 months to 8, S014). 3 if payback is 12 to 24 months. 1 if there is no payback, only volume.
- **Quote:** "selling a dollar for 90 or are you actually investing a dollar to get $2 back in 6 to 9 months" (S004)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S004, S014).

## 7. Customer at the doorstep, cut the noise *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Early on, market sizing, competitor maps, and investor views are noise. The only truth is whether the customer in front of you is happy. Talk to them yourself, deliver yourself, iterate on what they say. Insights already in big data are too late.
- **Parts:** Serve the first 30 people near you; ask what failed; fix one thing; repeat; do it yourself before hiring.
- **When to use:** Pre-PMF decisions; a founder writing strategy decks instead of talking to buyers.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has spoken to and served real customers this week. 1 if the plan rests on reports and search data.
- **Quote:** "when you're early stage it's all just like intellectual nonsense right like just what really matters is that you're sitting in there's like a customer in the doorstep and is he or she happy or not" (S004) "how do we get the 30 people around us to order from us?" (S008) "if there's an insight that is very well established in my data to be honest with you it's probably already too late" (S002)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S004, S008, S009, S012, S017).

## 8. Speed, quality, selection, price: push all four *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** What customers want does not change, as Bezos says. Raise the bar on all four together, not one at a time. Incremental fixes gave little; changing the model (dark stores) gave a 10x.
- **When to use:** Product strategy; checking a "we are just faster" pitch.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan beats the alternative on all four. 1 if it wins on one and loses on the rest.
- **Quote:** "At the end of the day, they care about speed, quality, selection, and price, right?" (S008)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S004, S008, S013, S018).

## 9. Growth, customer experience, unit economics as one equilibrium *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** No fixed order of priority. Trade points between the three. Giving up ten points of unit economics for one point of customer experience is a bad trade; one point of experience for two of economics (a delivery fee) can be good.
- **When to use:** Fees, discounts, free delivery, pricing changes.
- **Quote:** "For us it's like it's growth customer experience unit economics and it's all the same level." (S001) "If I'm trading you know 10 points of unit economics I'm like losing that for one point of customer experience then it's not worth it." (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S003).

## 10. Go deep enough to be dangerous *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Founders must learn the parts they do not know (supply chain, finance, pricing) until they can govern them, so the company never depends on hires. Big bold calls are safe only after this.
- **Quote:** "your startup is never dependent on the kindness of Changers" (S004) "then the upside is the only thing that that I see on the table because I've been able to execute and mitigate the risk then I can really swing for gold" (S004)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S015).

## 11. Hire senior people early, then step back to controls *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Hire executives who have seen a zero to one journey before you scale (COO and CMO before launch), so mistakes do not multiply. Once they build teams, founders focus on controls, compliance, and unit economics.
- **Quote:** "if we make a mistake today and we're going to be 150x in 12 months then that problem is going to be 150x bigger" (S003) "we in fact we hired a chief operating officer, chief marketing officer pre-launch." (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S003, S004, S012).

## 12. Put a number on the crazy goal, then plan backwards *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Name the ambitious target, build a detailed plan and capital plan to reach it, then execute by the plan. In September 2021 he knew the April 2022 order count and which round was needed when.
- **Quote:** "Sounds crazy, but let's put a number to it. And now let's actually build a very detailed plan that that talks about how to do it." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S002).

## 13. Test and control at scale *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** At scale, an experimentation engine with millions on each side decides, not opinion. Most ideas fail, and that is fine because the result is objective.
- **Quote:** "90% of the time you're going to be wrong but the it's very objective because you can see the retention" (S004)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S004, S015).

## 14. High margin is low TAM *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Low-margin consumer businesses have the biggest markets. There, the founder's job is getting to profit, not adding bets. Focus is the advantage.
- **Quote:** "The lower the margin, the higher the TAM." (S001) "our biggest advantage is pure vertical focus" (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S013, S016, S003).

## 15. Fully loaded profitability, no Excel jugaad *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Count every cost line before calling a unit profitable. No moving costs to corporate to make a store look good.
- **Quote:** "you can do all that Excel jugard but today we're in a position we're fully loaded" (S003) "we don't have creative definitions of that" (S007)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S007, S014).
