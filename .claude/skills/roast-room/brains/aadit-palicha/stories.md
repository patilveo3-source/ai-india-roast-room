# Stories: Aadit Palicha

Built from 24 sources (2022 to 2025). 14 stories he tells, most-used first, with the lesson. Accounts by others are marked.

1. **The WhatsApp group for the neighbour.** In the 2020 lockdown, an elderly neighbour could not get groceries; the two founders delivered by bicycle, she added friends, the group hit 276 people, then they built an app (Kirana Cart). Lesson: start by solving one real person's problem (S004, S009, S012, S015).

2. **Nine months of delivering themselves.** For about nine months the founders did most deliveries, asked at each door what went wrong, and slept by a 24/7 support phone, answering calls at 3 or 4 am. Lesson: do the unscalable thing yourself; the doorstep is the truth (S004, S009, S012).

3. **The 72-hour prototype and no launch moment.** The first app took about three days; there was "no like launch moment" (S009). Lesson: ship crude and iterate (S005, S009).

4. **3 to 4 percent repeat, then the dark store.** With hour-long delivery, only 3 to 4 percent reordered the next week, and customers said they would leave after lockdown. Inventory from Metro Cash and Carry in KV's room became the first dark store; that neighbourhood did three to four times the volume. Lesson: incremental fixes do little; change the model for a 10x (S003, S008, S009, S017).

5. **Under 15 minutes: two to three times the retention.** Moving from 45 minutes to under 15 lifted retention two to three times and NPS above 85, and lowered last-mile cost (S018). Lesson: speed is the product and a cost lever (S007, S015, S018).

6. **Living in the hub.** In July 2021 they lived in a rickety hub in a slum area for about two months to make the model work (S004).

7. **The investor who rode a bike.** Suvir Sujan of Nexus delivered orders himself and talked to about 40 customers before investing, then told them to go now. Lesson: even investors should care only about customers (S002, S004).

8. **Three rounds in three months.** Nexus, Glade Brook, and YC Continuity ($100M) between July and December 2021; $160M in five months; four years of building compressed into six months (S001, S003). Lesson: when PMF is real, move all in.

9. **2022: written off.** A nine-month-old company in the worst capital market since 2008, against two rivals with over $1B cash. They out-executed and built fiscal discipline (S003, S004).

10. **Silicon Valley Bank.** In 2023, $38M stuck at SVB the week a term sheet arrived; he did not sleep for four days; YC's growth fund shut down the next day. Lesson: treat failure like death and you find a way (S004).

11. **Cold-pitching Amazon and Flipkart veterans as teenagers.** With a script he wrote, he pitched senior executives for months, interviewing up to 13 or 14 hours a day, once past midnight on a Saturday (S003, S012). Lesson: senior talent early keeps mistakes from multiplying.

12. **The equity split.** The board offered the founders' pool 70/30 in his favour; he made it 50/50 without telling KV. Lesson: co-founder trust is letting go of things (S004).

13. **Nashik in six weeks.** New Tier-2 stores hit 1,000 orders a day in six weeks, faster than metros (S010).

14. **2025 under pressure (reported by others and his posts).** He posted EBITDA up 2,000 bps and burn down about 65 percent while accusing a rival CFO of a smear campaign (S019, S020). Reported by others: fees rolled back over dark patterns, about 200 cafes shut, 500 layoffs, the IPO slipped (S024). Lesson for the seat: speed creates debts in trust and cash that come due.
