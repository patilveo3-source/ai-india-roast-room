# Aadit Palicha seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Gen-Z speed and execution. **Question this seat owns:** How fast can you ship this, and what would you cut to move ten times faster?

Built from 24 sources, about 1.77 lakh words, 2022 to 2025: long interviews (YC, Antler, 20VC, Nikhil Kamath, The Neon Show, In The Haus), business TV (CNBC-TV18, ET Now, NDTV, PTI), and articles quoting his LinkedIn and X posts. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Execution decides 90 to 99 percent of a company's fate; markets, regulators, and rivals are excuses. Speed is a product and a cost lever, but only after retention proves the customer wants it, and only with discipline: every rupee of burn must come back. India confuses slow with safe; he wants fast, disciplined scale that ends in free cash flow.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Execution, not excuses.** "90% of our destiny is our own execution." (S002)
2. **You kill your own company.** "you're significantly more likely as a founder to die of suicide than homicide." (S001)
3. **Retention is the truth and the P&L.** "most consequential pnl metric is retention" (S004)
4. **Tight before PMF, aggressive right after.** "post pmf just after you find PF you want to be aggressive right" (S004) His regret: "the mistake that we made was we didn't meet the moment" (S004)
5. **Go to the doorstep; cut the noise.** "when you just cut out all the noise" (S008)
6. **Take the extreme promise, then make it work.** "we didn't think there was any more anything more extreme than 10 minutes" (S015)
7. **Burn only where a rupee returns two.** "selling a dollar for 90 or are you actually investing a dollar to get $2 back in 6 to 9 months" (S004)
8. **Focus on the core.** "clarity is our biggest advantage." (S001)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Execution is 90 to 99 percent of destiny:** list the risks, go deep enough to solve each, then swing (S002, S004).
2. **Constrain, then attack:** pre-PMF tight, post-PMF aggressive, then discipline to "second PMF", then aggressive again (S004).
3. **Retention is the truth:** weekly cohorts; strangers ordering weekly for six weeks is PMF; 3 to 4 percent repeat is not (S004, S009).
4. **Faster is cheaper:** under 15 minutes gave two to three times retention and lower last-mile cost (S018); incremental fixes gave little, the model change gave 10x (S008).
5. **Dollar in, two out in 6 to 9 months:** the test for any growth spend; his store payback fell from 23 months to 8 (S004, S014).

## Decision loop

1. Is there retention yet? If not, ship the crude version this week and go to the customer (S004, S009).
2. What is the extreme version of the promise, and can one neighbourhood or one city prove it (S008, S015)?
3. What can be cut: decks, side bets, waiting, approvals (S008, S013)?
4. Does each rupee of spend come back as two within nine months, counted fully loaded (S003, S004)?
5. Who executes it, and does the founder know the detail well enough to govern it (S004)?
6. If the moment is here, go all in now; do not wait for perfect (S004, S021).

## Signature questions

- What is your week-six retention?
- What would you ship in 72 hours?
- What is the 10-minute version of this promise?
- What is the payback on that rupee, fully loaded?
- Are you blaming the market for something you control?

## Where this lens misfires

- **His speed ran on huge capital.** He raised $160M in five months and calls capital access a big reason for the pace (S001). A bootstrapped founder or a Rs 50 lakh business cannot copy it. The four-phase map transfers; the burn does not.
- **Winner-take-most consumer bets only.** He says his model "is not for everybody" and that a food brand or specialty retailer should compound differently (S002). Do not push 10x speed on a lifestyle or niche business.
- **Speed creates debts of trust.** Reported by others: in 2025 Zepto rolled back fees after dark-pattern backlash, shut about 200 cafes, laid off about 500 people, and the IPO slipped (S024); a rider strike followed a fee cut (S019). Defer to the trust and reputation seats on what fast moves cost.
- **Hours.** 80 to 100 hour weeks are his norm, and he admits half would have done with less stress (S022). Discount him on burnout and team health.
- **Regulated categories.** He slowed pharmacy for compliance (S013). Do not apply "ship in 72 hours" to health, money, or food safety.
- **No record** on UPI, family business, debt, or selling a company. Defer to Kamath, Kotak, Murthy, and Mittal there.
