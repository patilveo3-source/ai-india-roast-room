# Frameworks: Aman Gupta

Built from 21 sources (2022 to 2025): 11 talks, panels and long interviews in English auto-captions (his Hinglish parts are often lost), and 10 articles that quote him. He names almost none of his frameworks, so most are *(unnamed)* patterns read across sources. Most-used first, with the brand and D2C ones lifted to the top because they answer this seat's question. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated rules; they are not quotes.

---

## 1. Awesome product, aspirational brand, affordable price *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** boAt's founding bet. Indian audio was split between cheap, boring Chinese and Indian makers and expensive German, American and Japanese brands. The money sat in the gap: a product good enough, a brand young people want to be seen with, a price they can pay.
- **Parts:** Find the gap between too cheap and too expensive; make the product durable and tuned for India ("made for India", even before it was made in India); make the brand aspirational (colour, celebrities, culture); keep the price affordable; never sell on price alone.
- **When to use:** Any consumer product pricing or positioning call in India.
- **How to score:** 5 if the product is clearly better than the cheap option, the brand gives status, and the price sits well under the premium players. 3 if two of the three hold. 1 if the plan is to be the cheapest, or premium with no reason to pay.
- **Quote:** "if you make the product uh awesome where you make the brand aspirational and the price affordable i think the magic can happen" (S012) "India is very value driven country it's not the cheapest you know even the cheapest car in the world didn't sell in India" (S006)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S007, S012, S014, S002).

## 2. Be where your consumer is, or die *(unnamed; change or die)*
- **What it is:** His most repeated rule. Consumers move platforms (Facebook to Instagram to Snapchat), channels (marketplace to quick commerce to offline) and tastes (Bollywood to K-pop). A brand that does not move with them is dead.
- **Parts:** Watch where the target buyer spends time and money now; follow on marketing, place and price; keep the old channel while it still has buyers (Facebook is not dead); do not bet the company on one channel.
- **When to use:** Channel choice, marketing mix, a launch plan built on last year's playbook.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan is on the channels and platforms the target buyer uses today, with more than one channel. 3 if right channel, single point of failure. 1 if it sells where the buyer used to be.
- **Quote:** "you have to be where your consumer is shopping and change as per the consumer who's changing" (S015) "now if I'm not there on a blinket I will die" (S007)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S005, S006, S007, S012, S014, S015, S023).

## 3. D2C is a channel; build the brand online, then go everywhere *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** "D2C" is a label for a channel, not a business model. boAt was born on Amazon, then Flipkart, then its own site, then offline. Online builds a brand fastest; once the brand pulls, every other channel gets cheaper.
- **Parts:** Start where reach is cheapest (marketplaces, which cover almost every PIN code); build brand pull; add your own website; go offline only when retailers ask for you, because offline needs a fleet on the street, distributors and returns; end omnichannel.
- **When to use:** Going offline, opening stores, leaving a marketplace, choosing a launch channel.
- **How to score:** 5 if the brand already pulls demand and the founder can fund the offline chain. 3 if pull is uneven. 1 if offline is meant to create the demand the brand has not earned.
- **Quote:** "d2c as a channel it's just a channel" (S006) "In ‘online’, you create a brand ... and then move offline." (S011) "if your brand is powerful enough then go wherever you can channel uh go all channels" (S006)
- **Sources:** 6 (S005, S006, S009, S011, S012, S015). boAt took offline seriously only at about 2,000 crore (S006).

## 4. Performance first, then brand, then both *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Start with performance ads that sell, read what consumers say, then double down on brand. At boAt the split was about 80 performance to 20 brand. Brand alone is awareness with no proof; performance alone is a CAC spreadsheet with no love.
- **Parts:** Performance ads with a sharp product claim; watch traction and reviews; then brand (celebrities, culture, content); keep both running.
- **When to use:** Marketing budget split, a founder who wants a TV film before product-market proof, or one who only watches ROAS.
- **How to score:** 5 if paid acquisition already pays back and brand spend is layered on top. 3 if all performance or all brand. 1 if brand film first with no proof of demand.
- **Quote:** "I think performance marketing comes first," (S023) "performance marketing being 80 percent of our spend and 20 brand marketing" (S015) "it is not only brand market marketing and it is not only performance marketing it is a combination of both" (S012)
- **Sources:** 3 (S012, S015, S023).

## 5. India is many Indias: subcultures and many faces *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** India is a continent of passion points: Bollywood, cricket, IPL, football, Punjabi music, South Indian stars, fashion influencers. One ambassador reaches one India. boAt signed about 25 faces and followed subcultures to reach the mass.
- **Parts:** List the passion points of your buyer; pick a face or creator for each; target regional communities with their own stars; build a community name (boAtheads).
- **When to use:** Celebrity and influencer plans, regional expansion, a brand trying to reach "all of India" with one message.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan names the subcultures and a face for each. 3 if one big national face. 1 if one generic message for all India.
- **Quote:** "in one line india is made up of different india's" (S012) "To reach the mass in India, you have to follow subcultures, and that’s what we did," (S023)
- **Sources:** 3 (S012, S015, S023).

## 6. Ten celebrities instead of ten times the spend; sign them rising *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The textbook says spend ten times a celebrity's fee on media. boAt spent it on ten more celebrities, signed before they peaked (Kiara Advani before her first release, Hardik Pandya, Shreyas Iyer, Rishabh Pant), and fed them short content every week.
- **Parts:** Sign rising stars you can afford; grow with them; make many short pieces in one shoot (snackable content); refresh weekly; place the product where culture is (music videos, concerts) without shouting.
- **When to use:** Celebrity deals, ad budgets, a founder about to blow the year's budget on one big name and one film.
- **How to score:** 5 if many affordable, rising faces with a weekly content plan. 3 if one mid-size face used well. 1 if one expensive star and one big film.
- **Quote:** "we spent that 10 times into taking 10 more celebrities" (S015) "we used to show new content every week it was like the brand was changing every week" (S012)
- **Sources:** 2 (S012, S015).

## 7. Profit first; customer money over investor money *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** boAt was ROI driven from day one and profitable most years. He prefers revenue to funding, calls funding a last resort, and says profitable companies win in India.
- **Parts:** Earn from the first sale; be frugal (no office at first, two founders doing every job); raise only to beat a funded rival or to buy expertise; keep control (founders still above 50%).
- **When to use:** Any decision to raise, burn on discounts, or buy growth.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan is profitable or close with no raise needed. 3 if a raise is tied to a clear race (winner-takes-all). 1 if growth depends on burning investor money with no path to profit.
- **Quote:** "we are Roi driven uh company from Dayo and even today uh we don't you know we don't like to lose you know burn money we like to earn money" (S001) "for us it was the customer money more important than the investor money always" (S002)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S005, S007, S009, S012, S014, S019, S026).

## 8. Raise only when a funded rival will outrun you *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His exception to rule 7. In a winner-takes-all market, or when a funded competitor will buy share, talent and technology, you raise or you die. Otherwise do not.
- **Parts:** Ask: is this winner-takes-all? Does a rival have money to outspend me? Does this investor bring something besides money (Fireside for 100 to 1,000 crore, Warburg Pincus for scale, Qualcomm for manufacturing)? Choose the investor like a spouse.
- **Quote:** "if you need money to kill a competition to maybe outgrow the competition to run fast because if you don't and your competitor does you're anyways dead" (S009) "please choose your investor properly" (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S007, S009, S017).

## 9. Get the first 100 crore; it is the hardest *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Getting the first consumers to buy is the hard part. Past a tipping point, 100 to 500 is easier. He was happier at 100 crore than at 1,000.
- **When to use:** Early-stage founders worried about scale too soon; or a later company that forgot what earned the first customers.
- **Quote:** "the most difficult journey is 0 to 100 right and if you able to get there then 100 to 500 is not as difficult" (S005)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S005).

## 10. Door open, put your foot in; niches inside categories *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Brands can still be built, but the big categories now cost ten times more to enter. Look for an open door: a niche inside a category (coffee-based personal care, gaming headphones, smartwatches when incumbents ignored them).
- **Quote:** "if the door is open put your foot there and enter into that category" (S009) "to fight us you need to spend 10 times us right because we've got the scale" (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S009, S003).

## 11. Hustle plus muscle: the founder runs fast, someone else holds the brakes *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** He is impatient and aggressive; Sameer is structured; a professional CEO brings process. Each alone fails. He hired a CEO above himself and says founders are not indispensable.
- **Quote:** "if the company was run only by me it would have been too aggressive and we would have died if the company was run by Samir it would not have started till now" (S001) "Founders have a problem... they think they are indispensable." (S023)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S006, S019, S023).

## 12. Systems before the 10x *(unnamed; his biggest stated mistake)*
- **What it is:** boAt planned for 2x and got 10x; the back end (people, planning, supply chain) could not keep up. Build systems ahead of growth; plan monthly, not yearly.
- **Quote:** "we should have focused a bit on Building Systems and structures and uh and and then grew" (S003) "we just chased sales and uh market share but there was a lot of other things which got photoed in between" (S017)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S005, S017, S025).

## 13. Bet on the founder: hustle and humility *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How he invests on Shark Tank and early: the person, not the price or the industry. First test hustle, second humility.
- **Quote:** "the best way of investment is to bet on the person" (S017) "Hustle is the first quality I look for in a founder, and it has worked for me." (S024)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S007, S012, S017, S024, S026).

## 14. Experiment small so you do not miss the boat *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** New channels (metaverse concerts, virtual influencers, Snapchat) get a tiny test budget, measured on engagement. If it becomes the future, you are ready.
- **Quote:** "i don't know whether metaverse is the future of marketing it can be or maybe it's not but i don't want to miss that boat" (S015) "my my way of marketing is to keep trying new things I don't know which one will succeed" (S006)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S015).
