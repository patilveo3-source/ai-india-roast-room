# Stories: Aman Gupta

Built from 21 sources (2022 to 2025): talks, panels, long interviews and articles quoting him. The stories he reuses, one or two lines each, with the lesson he draws.

1. **Written off as "the next Micromax".** VCs said Indian electronics brands die and the Chinese would eat boAt; about 40 VCs and angels said no. Lesson: use their questions as the checklist, then prove them wrong. "I was written off before I was born" (S003) (S001, S005, S017, S024)

2. **"You missed the boAt."** The investors who passed later came back; he tells them they missed the boAt. Lesson: profit is the best answer to doubters. (S001, S017, S024)

3. **Kaval's 6 crore.** Kanwaljit Singh of Fireside invested 6 crore at a 100 crore valuation he was allowed to pick; he got about 100 crore back in secondaries and still held about 3%. Lesson: the right investor bets on the founders and brings vision, not just money. (S005, S007, S009)

4. **The arranged-marriage co-founders.** A mutual contact introduced him (brand, Delhi) to Sameer Mehta (money and back end, Mumbai); 50/50 from day one, despite his family's history of failed partnerships. Lesson: complementary skills beat friendship. (S001, S012, S014, S019)

5. **15 lakh at 36, two years from his wife.** After Citibank, a family distribution business, ISB, KPMG and JBL, he started with 15 lakh in the bank and a two-year deadline. Lesson: starting late is fine; one success is enough. "I only had 15 lakhs in bank at 36." (S002) (S001, S015)

6. **The distribution business that vanished.** His family business sold so well that the global brands opened their own India offices and cut him out. Lesson: a distributor without a brand is at the mercy of others. (S002)

7. **Five failures, then one success.** He failed five times before boAt. "you can have 100 failures but you need one success" (S003) (S002, S017)

8. **Ten celebrities, rising stars.** Instead of spending ten times one celebrity fee on media, boAt signed ten more faces, catching Kiara Advani, Hardik Pandya, Shreyas Iyer and Rishabh Pant before they peaked. Lesson: grow with the stars you can afford. (S012, S015)

9. **Thirty ads in a day.** The first campaign was 30 to 40 ten-to-fifteen-second films shot in one day, later called snackable content. Lesson: the impatient consumer wants new content every week. (S012)

10. **From 7 crore to 4,000 crore.** About 7 crore in year one, then 30, 100, 700, 2,000, 4,000 crore by FY23, target 10,000. He was happier at 100 than at 1,000. Lesson: 0 to 100 is the hardest. (S001, S005, S018)

11. **Grew faster than its people.** Planned 2x, got 10x; the back end and the old team could not keep up; a loss year came from hiring more than they could handle. Lesson: build systems before growth. (S001, S003, S005, S017)

12. **Hired a boss.** The founders brought in Vivek Gambhir from Godrej as CEO and reported to him. Lesson: founders are replaceable. (S001, S003, S019)

13. **Shark Tank as free TV.** Shark Tank put his face on national TV; boAt's organic traffic rose two to three times and fans bought boAt because of him. Lesson: a founder face is a marketing channel. (S001, S012)

14. **Make in India in three years.** From 100% imported before 2020 to 70 to 85% made in India, with one crore units a year. Lesson: government support plus consumer pride make it possible. (S005, S014, S019)

15. **Flatheads and the banana chips founder.** On Shark Tank he backed a struggling Flatheads founder because he saw himself in him, and a quiet banana chips founder who grew from 20 lakh to two crore a month. Lesson: bet on hustle and humility. (S003, S017)

16. **First product on Amazon.** boAt's first product sold on Amazon.in in 2014, the year of the first Great Indian Festival. Lesson: born on marketplaces, then moved to every channel. (S021, S009)
