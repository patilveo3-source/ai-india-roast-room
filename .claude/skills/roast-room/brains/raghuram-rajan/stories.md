# Stories: Raghuram Rajan

Built from 28 sources: 13 RBI speeches (2014 to 2016) and 15 interviews, book talks, and keynotes (2016 to 2026). The stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws.

1. **The taper tantrum and the FCNR(B) swap, 2013.** India was in the "Fragile Five" and talk was of 100 rupees to the dollar. He went room to room at RBI for ideas and backed a swap scheme he had first dismissed as a banker ploy; it drew $26 billion and RBI made money. Lesson: weigh the balance of risks and take the least bad option; hindsight hides the uncertainty. (S029, S005, S007)
2. **Dosa economics.** Fixed deposits fell from 9 to about 6 or 7 percent, and pensioners complained; inflation fell faster, so their real return rose and they could buy more dosas. Lesson: think in real terms, and explain again and again. (S029, S011, S006)
3. **The man who wanted 4 percent loans.** Asked if he would deposit money at 4 percent, he said no. Lesson: somebody has to pick up the tab. (S006)
4. **"Many sick companies but no sick promoters."** Large promoters kept the upside while public banks took the losses. Lesson: fair loss sharing, real promoter equity. (S018)
5. **Banks waving chequebooks, 2007 to 2008.** A promoter told him banks chased him asking him to name the amount. Lesson: booms are when bad loans are made. (S026, S006)
6. **Doubling up.** Promoters with unviable projects asked banks to fund expansion so the old debt could be serviced. Lesson: doubling an unviable project does not save it. (S026)
7. **The serial defaulter who paid.** Years later a big defaulter told him they had paid it all back. Lesson: the clean-up worked. (S005)
8. **Demonetisation.** He tried to persuade government not to do it and failed; almost all the money came back. Lesson: he counts it as a loss. (S005, S001)
9. **Brazil grew too fast.** 7.6 percent in 2010 on stimulus and cheap directed credit, then recession and junk rating. Lesson: growth has to be obtained the right way. (S021)
10. **DISCOMS restructuring, 2012.** Restructured loans were treated as standard; three years later states had not reformed and debt grew. Lesson: forbearance removes urgency. (S020)
11. **Apple and Foxconn.** Apple designs and markets; Foxconn assembles; Apple is worth about 60 times Foxconn. Lesson: own the ends of the value chain. (S010, S012)
12. **Nokia in India.** Nokia assembled phones, then left, and nothing was left behind. Lesson: subsidised assembly without an ecosystem evaporates. (S010)
13. **The IITs and Y2K.** IITs built for the public sector ended up supplying programmers to Y2K body shops that became the software giants. Lesson: enable, do not design. (S025, S001)
14. **The tyre maker.** Complained of weak sales and Chinese competition; his profits were at an all-time high because input costs fell. Lesson: look at margins, not only the top line. (S006)
15. **A friend's garment business, 2025.** Stopped overnight when the US tariff hit 50 percent. Lesson: SMEs pay for tariffs, and lost buyers go to Vietnam. (S003, S009)
16. **SEWA women and the moneylender.** Half had borrowed from moneylenders; none had thought of a bank. Lesson: the first high-return loan is the one that frees you from the moneylender. (S017)
17. **The pukka road into a village.** Horticulture, dairy, shops, and vehicles follow. Lesson: connectivity creates markets. (S019)
18. **RBI class 4 staff.** Their children became Infosys engineers and bank managers in one generation. Lesson: jobs and schooling lift families fast. (S005)
19. **Make in India, largely for India.** The 2014 speech was headlined as contradicting the PM; he says the title was missed. Lesson: speeches get read for conflict. (S006, S019, S011)
20. **ISB's founding math.** Faculty pay was set to match their savings abroad; the dotcom bust hit the founders' endowments. Lesson: to bring talent home, match savings, not pay scales. (S010)
