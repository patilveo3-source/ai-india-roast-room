# Frameworks: Raghuram Rajan

Built from 28 sources: 13 of his RBI speeches as governor (2014 to 2016) and 15 long interviews, book talks, and keynotes (2016 to 2026), about 137,000 words. Quick depth.

14 frameworks. He names some (Make in India, largely for India; flexibility not forbearance; Goldilocks); others are *(unnamed)* patterns read across sources. Most-used first. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated rules; they are not quotes.

---

## 1. Create the environment, do not pick the sector *(his recurring argument; "Make in India, largely for India" is one form)*
- **What it is:** Government should supply public goods (roads, logistics, skills, predictable rules, access to finance) and let entrepreneurs choose what to build. Subsidies, tariffs, and favoured sectors breed lobbying and inefficiency.
- **Parts:** Ask what public good a sector lacks; refuse sops and tariff walls; keep rules stable; let winners emerge.
- **When to use:** Any plan that depends on a PLI scheme, a subsidy, a tariff, or a sector being "in favour".
- **How to score:** 5 if the business works on its own economics in an open, competitive market. 3 if it gains from a scheme but would survive without it. 1 if the model only works while a subsidy or tariff lasts.
- **Quote:** "creating an environment where all sorts of enterprise can flourish, and then leaving entrepreneurs, of whom we have plenty, to choose what they want to do." (S019) "Learning from our past, I would say let us not encourage anything; that might be the surest way of killing it." (S025) "don't keep asking me for Sops don't ask me for tariffs those are two things I'm not going to do" (S010)
- **Sources:** 9 (S004, S010, S012, S013, S015, S019, S021, S023, S025).

## 2. Human capital first *(Breaking the Mould)*
- **What it is:** India's most important asset is its people. Growth, jobs, and even manufacturing depend on skills, health, and education, from nutrition to top universities.
- **Parts:** Can you find appropriately skilled workers; are you training them; are you building on skills India already has (services, engineering, the diaspora)?
- **When to use:** Hiring plans, location choices, any bet on cheap Indian labour.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan trains and upgrades its people and draws on India's skill strengths. 3 if it hires skilled people but invests little. 1 if it assumes cheap unskilled labour will be available and productive.
- **Quote:** "I think you start with saying that our most important asset is our human capital" (S014) "the point is not to pick sectors for us to you know through this scheme or that scheme but instead to think about how do we raise the level of our human capital across the board" (S004)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S004, S005, S007, S009, S010, S014, S015, S016).

## 3. Recognise early, flexibility not forbearance *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Name a problem the day you see it; hiding it ("extend and pretend") makes it bigger. Once it is recognised, be flexible in fixing it.
- **Parts:** Recognise (NPA as anaesthetic for surgery); separate viable from unviable; write down to economic value; finish viable projects; never throw good money after bad.
- **When to use:** A struggling product line, a bad loan, a customer who will not pay, a stalled project.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has named the problem, written it down, and has a fix with a deadline. 3 if recognised but the fix is drifting. 1 if the plan rests on waiting for growth to cure it.
- **Quote:** "Mutilating Shakespeare, an NPA by any other name smells as bad!" (S018) "Think therefore of the NPA classification as an anesthetic that allows the bank to perform extensive necessary surgery to set the project back on its feet." (S024) "Don’t throw good money after bad money simply because there is an unreliable promise that debt will become serviceable." (S026)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S018, S020, S021, S024, S026, S028).

## 4. Fair risk sharing: no super equity, genuine promoter equity *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The equity holder takes the first loss; debt is senior. Projects fail because they are built on tiny slivers of borrowed equity. But honest business failure must be protected by limited liability, or nobody takes risk.
- **Parts:** Real equity up front, more equity for more risk; personal guarantees mean personal assets; separate business failure from wilful default.
- **When to use:** Taking debt, structuring a project, personal guarantees, investor terms.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has real skin in the game and debt is sized to cash flows. 3 if equity is thin but cash flows are steady. 1 if the plan uses borrowed "equity" and expects lenders to share the downside.
- **Quote:** "Promoters should not try and finance mega projects with tiny slivers of equity." (S018) "The more the risks, the more the equity component should be (genuine promoter equity, not fake borrowed equity, of course), and the greater the flexibility in the debt structure." (S028) "the first point is equity should be junior to debt" (S006)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S018, S021, S026, S028).

## 5. No free lunch: who picks up the tab? *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Every cheap price is paid for by someone. Low loan rates need savers willing to accept low deposit rates; a weak rupee taxes consumers; a subsidy taxes the budget and future generations.
- **Parts:** For every benefit, name who pays and whether it can last.
- **When to use:** Pricing that depends on subsidised inputs, cheap credit, or a government scheme; any "free" offer.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can name who pays and why it is sustainable. 3 if the cost is hidden but small. 1 if the plan relies on someone else absorbing losses indefinitely.
- **Quote:** "There are no creative ways of extracting more money from the RBI – there is no free lunch!" (S029) "the idea is somebody is going to pick up the tab well we're always used to somebody picking up the tab" (S006) "undervaluation is a subsidy to domestic producers paid for by domestic consumers and savers." (S025)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S007, S020, S025, S029).

## 6. Growth the right way: stability first *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Fast growth bought with stimulus, cheap credit, or excess leverage ends in a bust. Steady, sustainable growth beats a spike.
- **Parts:** Is the growth funded by real demand or by cheap money; what happens when rates rise; are the buffers enough?
- **When to use:** Blitz-scaling, debt-funded expansion, raising at a peak.
- **How to score:** 5 if growth is funded by customers and margins with buffers. 3 if partly debt-funded with a clear payback. 1 if growth depends on cheap money continuing.
- **Quote:** "Growth has to be obtained in the right way." (S021) "Paradoxical as it may seem, Brazil tried to grow too fast." (S021) "Growth is good, but growth with stability is better, especially in a poor country where so many people live at the margin." (S029)
- **Sources:** 5 (S020, S021, S025, S029, S005).

## 7. The roots of a crisis: euphoria plus debt *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Crises start in booms, when money is easy and everyone extrapolates; they become crises when borrowers cannot pay.
- **Parts:** Is the market euphoric; are rates low; is the plan extrapolating recent growth; is there debt that must be paid on schedule?
- **When to use:** Raising or borrowing in a hot market, AI-driven valuations, expansion after a great year.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan survives a 30 percent fall in demand without breaching debt. 3 if it needs the good times to last a year. 1 if it assumes the boom continues.
- **Quote:** "the roots of a crisis always come from excess euphoria but also supported by things like very loose monetary policy uh very loose supervision" (S005) "A debt crisis is what creates a crisis." (S005) "It is at such times that banks make mistakes. They extrapolate past growth and performance to the future." (S026)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S026, S028).

## 8. Buffer if short, adjust if long *(unnamed, 2026)*
- **What it is:** Cushion a shock only while it looks temporary. If it lasts, pass costs through steadily and target help to the weakest. Then build strategic resilience.
- **Parts:** Is this transitory; how long can buffers last; steady price pass-through, not one jerk; targeted support; diversify critical inputs without trying to make everything at home.
- **When to use:** Input cost spikes (fuel, tariffs, rupee falls), supply disruptions, whether to raise prices.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder passes costs through in steps and has diversified critical inputs. 3 if absorbing costs with a deadline. 1 if absorbing indefinitely and hoping it passes.
- **Quote:** "buffer to the extent it's short, uh allow adjustment into prices to the extent it's longer because you simply cannot subsidize your way out of this." (S008) "You can't sort of wait and hope and pray that the the straight opens up" (S007) "we don't try and produce everything. We simply cannot do that, but we are much more strategic about what is key, what is what is a real source of vulnerability" (S008)
- **Sources:** 2 (S007, S008).

## 9. Own the two ends of the value chain *(Breaking the Mould)*
- **What it is:** Value sits in design and IP at the start and marketing and services at the end, not in assembly. Do not fetishise manufacturing.
- **Parts:** Where in the chain is the margin; who owns the customer and the IP?
- **When to use:** Manufacturing versus services, contract manufacturing, D2C brands.
- **How to score:** 5 if the business owns design, IP, or the customer. 3 if it assembles but is moving up. 1 if it is pure low-margin assembly on a subsidy.
- **Quote:** "if you want to think about growth you want to try and occupy those two ends the beginning and the end uh not so much the middle" (S012) "Apple is worth 60 times foxcon even though foxcon does all its manufacturing for Apple" (S010) "what I'm against is fetishizing manufacturing" (S010)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S010, S012, S013, S015).

## 10. Feel the stones: enabling regulation and the sandbox *(his phrase, borrowed from China)*
- **What it is:** Regulators should allow innovation unless it is a clear and present danger, watch it as it grows, and regulate once it is material. Experiment inside a stable broad policy.
- **Parts:** Is this a clear and present danger; is it small enough to experiment; who carries the risk as it scales?
- **When to use:** Fintech, lending, payments, crypto, any business in a grey regulatory zone.
- **How to score:** 5 if the product is safe for the unsophisticated user and could survive tighter rules at scale. 3 if it relies on a sandbox phase. 1 if the model depends on regulators not noticing.
- **Quote:** "our philosophy is to allow innovation in institutions, instruments and practices so long as they do not present a clear and present danger." (S021) "We are more open to experimentation at the early stages of a product or method of service" (S027) "you can theorize all you want but the theory has to hit the ground" (S010)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S010, S021, S027, S028).

## 11. Weigh the balance of risks; choose the least bad option *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Decide under uncertainty by comparing the cost of doing nothing with the worst cost of acting. With no good options, take the least bad and own it.
- **Parts:** Cost of inaction; worst-case cost of action; probability; reversibility.
- **When to use:** Crisis decisions, big bets with no clean answer.
- **How to score:** 5 if both costs are sized and the worst case is survivable. 3 if sized roughly. 1 if the decision ignores the cost of doing nothing or the worst case.
- **Quote:** "Policy making is about deciding in the face of uncertainty, after weighing the alternatives as best as one can." (S029) "On the day I was to take over, with no good options on the table, I had to choose the least bad one." (S029) "here are the costs if I take this uh action here are the costs if I take that action where are the costs lower where where are the benefits higher" (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S014, S029).

## 12. Insiders versus outsiders: arm's-length over relationships *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** As India grows, newcomers need contracts, auctions, and fair courts rather than connections. A business that depends on access will lose as rules level the field.
- **Parts:** Does the model depend on relationships or on contracts; would it survive a level playing field?
- **When to use:** Government contracts, licences, businesses built on connections.
- **How to score:** 5 if the business wins on merit in open contests. 3 if relationships help but are not essential. 1 if it depends on privileged access.
- **Quote:** "Newcomers and outsiders rely, not on relationships to get things done, but on arm’s length auctions and contracts, enforced quickly by an impartial bureaucracy and judiciary." (S021) "Competition is only unfair if it is not on the same playing field." (S021)
- **Sources:** 3 (S019, S021, S023).

## 13. Public rails, private products *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The state builds infrastructure (UPI, Aadhaar, NEFT, TReDS); private players build the products people want on top. Technology cuts the cost of small-ticket transactions.
- **Parts:** What public rail can you build on; does the product serve small tickets at high volume; is it secure for the actual customer?
- **When to use:** Fintech, payments, MSME credit, anything on India's digital public infrastructure.
- **How to score:** 5 if it rides public rails to serve small-ticket users cheaply and safely. 3 if it uses the rails but serves only the top. 1 if it depends on a monopoly or on users being careful.
- **Quote:** "What we need are non-governmental players to utilize this infrastructure to provide the products and access that people want." (S017) "There is no point creating security for the ideal customer, instead the system should incorporate the possibility of mistakes by the actual customer that we have." (S027)
- **Sources:** 3 (S017, S021, S027).

## 14. Predictability beats tinkering *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Constant changes in tax rates, tariffs, and rules stop people planning and invite lobbying. Moderate and stable beats clever and shifting.
- **Parts:** How exposed is the plan to policy flips; is it betting on a rule staying favourable?
- **When to use:** Businesses exposed to GST rates, import duties, licences, retrospective tax risk.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan works under any reasonable rule. 3 if a policy change hurts but is survivable. 1 if one notification could kill it.
- **Quote:** "if you constantly cut cut and ra raise GST rates then people don't have a ability to plan and then you have the whole lot of lobbying cut my rate keep his rate high" (S004) "have consistent reasonable policies don't keep going back and forth" (S010) "the problem with Indian policy um you know for a long time had been unpredictability unpredictability" (S015)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S009, S010, S015, S007).
