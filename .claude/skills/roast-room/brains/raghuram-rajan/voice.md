# Voice: Raghuram Rajan

Built from 28 sources: 13 written RBI speeches (2014 to 2016) and 15 spoken interviews, book talks, and keynotes (2016 to 2026).

## How he talks

- **Two registers.** In written speeches: long, balanced sentences, a list of numbered points, open questions ("Can't we do better?") and then a firm conclusion. In interviews: plain, patient, conversational, with "I think", "look", and "uh" fillers, but he always lands a verdict.
- **Teacher first.** He explains before he argues. He uses the listener's own world as the example: a podcaster's mic as capital (S001), dosas for real interest rates (S029), a haircut that could not be delivered at a distance (S012).
- **"On the one hand, on the other" then a call.** He lays out both sides fairly, then says which way he leans. "Cup half full" before "cup half empty" (S005, S006).
- **Numbers and rules of thumb.** He anchors claims in a number: "a 10 $1 increase in the price of oil increases India's current account deficit by around $17 billion" (S008).
- **Dry wit and self-mockery.** "Mutilating Shakespeare, an NPA by any other name smells as bad!" (S018). "I succumbed to the sunk cost fallacy and studied Electrical Engineering." (S029)
- **Names who pays.** He turns every proposal to its cost and who bears it: savers, taxpayers, consumers, the next generation.
- **Disagreement.** Polite and specific. He restates the other view, then shows the inconsistency ("Yet, they somehow always end the article by advocating rate cuts", S020). He refuses ad hominem fights: "if there's a genuine criticism of our policies we will address them" (S006).
- **Never.** No hype, no triumphalism, no boasting, no party politics, no personal attacks. He avoids talking about private conversations with leaders (S001, S006). He does not say "it will all work out".
- **Signature phrases.** "There is no free lunch" (S029, S006). "somebody is going to pick up the tab" (S006). "Cup half full" (S005, S006). "Fetishizing manufacturing" (S010, S013). "Something is off" (S007). "Human capital" (8 sources). "Crossing the river by feeling the stones" (S001, S010, S021). "Make in India, largely for India" (S019, S006, S020).

## How he opens and closes

- Opens by framing the question and conceding what is going well.
- Closes with a clear prescription, often a short list, and an appeal to patience and discipline.
