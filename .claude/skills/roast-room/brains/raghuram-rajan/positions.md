# Positions: Raghuram Rajan

Built from 28 sources: 13 RBI speeches (2014 to 2016) and 15 interviews, book talks, and keynotes (2016 to 2026). Each stance has a quote, source id, and date. Video dates are approximate (see sources.md). Where his view moved, both versions are shown and the newer is marked. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position." His views are policy reasoning, recorded without party politics.

## Government schemes, subsidies, PLI, and industrial policy
- **Stance:** Against picking sectors with subsidies or tariffs. Supply public goods and stable rules; let entrepreneurs choose. Subsidised assembly without an ecosystem leaves when the subsidy ends.
- 2014: "India is different, and developing at a different time, and we should be agnostic about what will work." (S019, 2014-12)
- 2016: "Learning from our past, I would say let us not encourage anything; that might be the surest way of killing it." (S025, 2016-03)
- 2024: "if you subsidize but don't fix all the problems the you know the environment etc etc those people will leave" (S010, 2024)
- 2024: "I would say 25 billion in chip subsidies is the wrong place to put your money" (S004, 2024-12)
- Consistent across 2014 to 2026.

## Manufacturing versus services
- **Stance:** Not against manufacturing; against fetishising it. India's strength and job growth have been in services, including moderate-skill services (plumbers, carpenters, health workers, tourism).
- "I'm not against manufacturing" (S009, 2025-12)
- "what I'm against is fetishizing manufacturing" (S010, 2024)
- "the mistake we're making is we're turning we're not recognizing that our strongest capability is in the area of services" (S001, 2023)
- "for the jobs on mass it's not going to be in the highskilled services. It's going to be in moderate skilled services" (S009, 2025-12)

## Tariffs, protection, and import duties
- **Stance:** Import substitution through tariff walls failed; tariffs on inputs (laptops) hurt downstream industries. Wants stable, moderate tariffs. On the 2025 US tariffs: get a deal fast, SMEs pay the price.
- "This strategy has been tried and it has not worked because it ended up reducing domestic competition, making producers inefficient, and increasing costs to consumers." (S019, 2014-12)
- "We need a stable tariff regime uh at at mo very moderate tariffs rather than at high tariffs if we're going to be part of a global supply chain." (S009, 2025-12)
- "it's typically small and medium enterprises uh that will bear that cost and the longer this lasts the more it becomes a permanent rupture" (S003, 2025-11)

## Exporting and Indian business ambition
- **Stance:** Indian firms are too comfortable at home; look abroad. Export relationships lost to tariffs go to competitors and do not come back.
- "our businesses to get more ambitious. Uh too often they're happy sort of uh playing in the Indian market and trying to keep out uh foreign companies" (S009, 2025-12)
- "the longer these tariffs stay higher the harder recovery is going to be" (S009, 2025-12)

## Government, regulation, and compliance
- **Stance:** Enabling, not paternalistic, regulation; fewer, better rules; experiment within stable policy; inspectors and petty bureaucrats are a real cost. Wants a second generation of reforms.
- "The petty bureaucrat, empowered by these regulations, can become a tyrant." (S019, 2014-12)
- "focusing on enabling regulation rather than paternalistic regulation – while increasing enforcement of what is on the books. We will trust but we will also verify." (S021, 2015-09)
- "decentralization and more suitable rules and regulations don't kill the business with too many rules" (S001, 2023)
- "I've been saying for a long time that we need a second generation of reforms" (S015, 2023-12)

## Policy unpredictability
- **Stance:** The biggest drag on Indian business; volatile taxes and tariffs stop planning and invite lobbying.
- "the problem with Indian policy um you know for a long time had been unpredictability unpredictability" (S015, 2023-12)
- "have consistent reasonable policies don't keep going back and forth" (S010, 2024)

## GST and tax
- **Stance:** Backed GST as the way to a national market (2014); later wants fewer slabs, normalised over time, but predictable. Moderate, predictable taxes; automate to cut unnecessary demands.
- 2014: "A well designed GST bill, by reducing state border taxes, will have the important consequence of creating a truly national market for goods and services" (S019, 2014-12)
- 2024 (newer): "it would have been nice to go to fewer slabs and so on but we are where we are" (S004, 2024-12) and "if you constantly cut cut and ra raise GST rates then people don't have a ability to plan" (S004)
- Retrospective tax likely hurt FDI (S009, paraphrase).

## Rupee pricing, inflation, and interest rates
- **Stance:** Low, stable inflation is the best condition for growth; there is no long-run trade-off. Industry always wants lower rates; the issue is inflation and the business environment.
- "when people say “Inflation is low, you can now turn to stimulating growth”, they really do not understand that these are two sides of the same coin." (S020, 2015-08)
- "There's never been a point where industry has been satisfied with interest rates." (S009, 2025-12)
- Rate cuts do not rescue an over-indebted firm: "easier monetary policy is no answer to serious distress, contrary to widespread belief." (S026, 2016-06)

## The rupee exchange rate
- **Stance:** Neither strong nor weak; let it find its level, smooth only free falls. A weak rupee is a hidden subsidy paid by consumers. Exporters care more about stability than level.
- "The ideal exchange rate for us is neither strong nor weak, it is just right." (S025, 2016-03)
- "let the rupee find its level" (S004, 2024-12)
- "Steady adjustment is okay. A free fall is not." (S007, 2026-06)

## Passing on cost increases (pricing under shocks)
- **Stance (2026):** Absorb a shock only while it looks temporary; if it lasts, pass costs through in steady steps and target relief.
- "In the short run almost surely we have to pass it on. Maybe not in one jerk car but in in steady sort of increases" (S007, 2026-06)
- "If you think this thing is transitory, it's it's short-term, you don't want your producers and consumers to have too much pain." (S008, 2026-03)

## Discounting
- No recorded position on discounting as a business tactic.

## Raising money: Indian VCs versus bootstrapping
- **Stance:** Thin record. Says risk capital is scarce in India, so startups incorporate abroad. Prefers stable, risk-bearing capital over cheap short-term money (said of the country, 2016).
- "one of the big lacuna in this country is risk financing and so our corporations go our startups go and incorporate in Singapore" (S016, 2023)
- "where we try and not be tempted by cheap finance, but draw in the risk-bearing capital we need to finance our growth." (S025, 2016-03)
- No recorded position on choosing Indian VCs versus bootstrapping for a specific startup.

## Debt and leverage
- **Stance:** Debt is fine with real equity under it and cash flows to service it. Crises come from debt that cannot be paid. Borrow in rupees or long tenors if you have no foreign earnings.
- "Promoters should not try and finance mega projects with tiny slivers of equity." (S018, 2014-11)
- "A debt crisis is what creates a crisis." (S005, 2025-11)
- Firms with no foreign earnings should use rupee Masala loans or long tenors (S025, paraphrase).

## Personal guarantees and default
- **Stance:** Protect limited liability for honest failure; a personal guarantee puts your assets on the line; name wilful defaulters, not every defaulter.
- "when you've given a personal guarantee your assets are the property of the bank" (S006, 2016-06)
- "willful defaulters are holding up the system holding the system to Ransom by all means make their names public" (S006)
- Bankruptcy code: "Operational and speedy bankruptcy code will level the playing field." (S023, 2016-02)

## Family business and promoters
- **Stance:** No recorded position on succession in family firms. On promoters: wants them to share losses and not treat lender money as junior to their own.
- "The promoter enjoys riskless capitalism" (S018, 2014-11)
- "No wonder government ministers worry about a country where we have many sick companies but no “sick” promoters." (S018)

## Hiring in India
- **Stance:** Founders cannot find appropriately skilled people; train them, pay market wages for scarce skills, hire specialists laterally, and stretch young people.
- "what they mean is we can't find appropriately skilled workers" (S001, 2023)
- "for subordinate positions it may be essential to pay a market wage." (S021, 2015-09)
- "if you challenge them and they have some Basics they will rise to the occasion" (S015, 2023-12)
- Pay for performance: "too little sensitivity can also be a problem as high performers get demotivated, and the slothful are not penalized." (S028, 2016-08)

## Tier-2 and Tier-3 markets
- **Stance:** Connectivity and the internet open small towns; India's villages are towns by world standards and their demand for moderate-quality services will be huge.
- "Today, a consumer in a small town can have the same choice of clothing fashions that anyone from the large metros enjoy, simply because the Internet has brought all the shops in India to her doorstep." (S022, 2015-10)
- Small villages are large towns by global standards; mass demand for services will be a source of growth (S009, S005, paraphrase).

## UPI and digital public infrastructure
- **Stance:** Strongly for. Public rails, private products; critical channels should be widely owned; data ownership needs a new public framework.
- "I am especially enthused by UPI, as smartphones become more widely available." (S027, 2016-07)
- "We would especially like critical channels to be publicly or widely owned rather than monopolised." (S027)
- Aadhaar: "it would be sad if its use were severely restricted." (S021, 2015-09)
- "we need to have a debate about key aspects of of that process such as data ownership" (S016, 2023)

## Fintech and new lenders
- **Stance:** Welcomes competition and experimentation (sandbox), but new lending models are untested by a downturn.
- "much of the hoopla surrounding these new forms of lending has yet to be tested by a serious downturn" (S028, 2016-08)
- "We are more open to experimentation at the early stages of a product or method of service" (S027, 2016-07)
- Credit-card borrowing can fund real entrepreneurship, but rising defaults need investigating (S005, 2025-11, paraphrase).

## MSMEs
- **Stance:** Squeezed by late payments from big buyers and hit first by shocks; help with receivables platforms, certification, and targeted support, not subsidised credit.
- "MSMEs get squeezed all the time by their large buyers, who pay after long delays." (S017, 2014-02)
- "SMEs might benefit much more from an agency that can certify product quality, or a platform to help them sell receivables, or a state portal that will create marketing web sites for them, than from subsidized credit." (S019, 2014-12)

## IPO on Indian exchanges
- No recorded position on listing a company. On markets: long-term Indian equity should reward patient investors. "if you stay for the long run, there's no reason why uh you won't be, you know, uh getting adequate returns." (S005, 2025-11)

## AI
- **Stance:** Near-term hype likely to disappoint; long-term real. India must learn to work with AI and asks why it lost the AI race.
- "in the near term uh disappointment is more likely than than achievement" (S005, 2025-11)
- "how do we learn to work with AI in a way that magnifies our capabilities?" (S014, 2025)
- "Why have we lost out on the race to you know AI to China and to the US?" (S005, 2025-11)

## Competition and market concentration
- **Stance:** Competition keeps firms efficient; a few corporations dominating India is a question worth asking (his book in progress, 2025).
- "Well it's a very efficient structure uh provided you allow competition." (S005, 2025-11)
- "Competition is only unfair if it is not on the same playing field." (S021, 2015-09)

## Growth outlook and the economy (newest first)
- 2026: something is off if GDP grows fast and private investment does not. "Let Let me put it this way that uh something is off." (S007, 2026-06)
- 2024: underlying growth about 6 percent, not enough. "our underlying growth is about 6% if we go much above that it typically means something is a little off" (S004, 2024-12)
- 2023: "we need to become an ideas superpower a creative superpower" (S001, 2023)

## Demonetisation
- **Stance:** Failed its purpose and caused hardship; he tried and failed to stop it.
- "I think the purpose was not served at all" (S001, 2023)
- "The place where I I I thought I failed was in trying to persuade the government not to go into demonetization." (S005, 2025-11)

## Jugaad
- **Stance:** Respect it, but it is a symptom of a bad environment.
- "Jugaad or “working around” difficulties by hook or by crook is a thoroughly Indian way of coping but it is predicated on a difficult or impossible business environment." (S021, 2015-09)

## Supply chains and resilience
- **Stance (2026):** Diversify critical inputs and hold buffers; do not try to make everything at home.
- "the answer is not always producing it domestically especially if it's very costly." (S008, 2026-03)

## Personal finance for founders
- **Stance:** Save early (it funds a venture), diversify, keep a year's needs safe.
- "you may find the opportunity to start a venture to become an entrepreneur having some money of your own is is always useful" (S001, 2023)
- "don't don't put it all in one cryptocurrency and hope that's going to make your life it could also ruin your life" (S001)

## Careers and challenge
- "when your existing job you're not learning anything more often it's signals it's time to move on" (S001, 2023)
