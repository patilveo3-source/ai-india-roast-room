# Frameworks: Raj Shamani

Built from 18 sources (2018 to 2026): three TEDx talks, a solo rules episode, talks with Q&A at Masters' Union and a personal branding event, long interviews where he is the guest (South Asian Trailblazers 2026, Siddharth Rajsekar, Humans of Bombay, Prakhar Gupta, Moneycontrol, Nikhil Kamath clip, CNN-News18), his own lines on one hosted episode, and four articles.

16 frameworks, most-used first, with the audience-to-revenue ones lifted to the top because they answer this seat's question. "Sources" counts distinct sources. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated rules and numbers; they are not quotes. Many of his transcripts are auto captions of Hinglish speech, so quotes keep their rough grammar.

## 1. Build your own TV channel *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Ads on Star Plus used to win because what is seen more sells more. The channels changed, the rule did not. Every business can now run its own media channel for the exact people who buy, talk about their life rather than the product, and sell in between.
- **Steps:** Name the buyer; make daily content about their world (for a household brand, the content Grihshobha used to carry); watching daily builds engagement, engagement makes them take your side, siding builds trust, trust leads to buying.
- **When to use:** Any founder who asks how to get customers without a big ad budget, or who plans to "run ads" as the whole marketing plan.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has a named buyer and a plan to publish for that buyer at least weekly for six months. 3 if content is planned but it is product promotion. 1 if marketing is ads only, or "we will do content later". Exception he names: pure trading businesses that win on buy-low, sell-high gaps.
- **Quote:** "Today, every person in this room, everyone who has a business, has an opportunity to build their own TV channel." (S006) "When they start taking your side, then trust will be built, and when trust is built, they will buy from you." (S006)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S009, S014, S019).

## 2. Buyer, decision maker, sharer *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Make content for three people: the one who buys, the one who decides or pays (the husband whose month decides the jewelry purchase), and the one who will forward it on WhatsApp because it makes them look smart.
- **When to use:** Deciding what to post; checking a content plan that only talks about the product.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan names all three and has content for each. 3 if it covers only the buyer. 1 if it is about the product category itself ("jewelry is my niche").
- **Quote:** "So every time you make content. You think about who will buy it, and along with who will buy it, who is the one influencing it." (S006) "So jewelry, jewelry is my niche. That's a pathetic way to do it." (S006)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S009 on sharing to look smart).

## 3. ECG: Evergreen, Controversial, Growth
- **What it is:** His mix for every 10 pieces of content. Evergreen pieces stay relevant for years; controversial pieces ride today's news and pull in new people; growth pieces serve the small loyal niche and build trust even if they get fewer views.
- **The mix (it moved):** about 2022: five evergreen, three controversial, two growth (S014). About 2025: five evergreen, two growth, three controversial (S006). Same split, the labels of the 2 and 3 swapped between talks.
- **When to use:** Building a content calendar; diagnosing a channel that grows but does not sell (too much controversial) or sells but does not grow (no controversial).
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan has all three in roughly that ratio. 3 if it is all evergreen. 1 if it is all trend chasing.
- **Quote:** "I follow a format. ECG: Evergreen, Controversial, Growth." (S006) "So five is evergreen content, three are controversial and two is growth content." (S014)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S014).

## 4. Micro deposits of trust
- **What it is:** In a low-trust society like India, distribution comes from trust, not from a narrow niche. Every right act is a deposit in a public bank; every wrong act is a withdrawal. Trusted names (Tata) can sell salt and steel.
- **When to use:** Any decision that trades trust for money: a paid promotion, a sensational clip, a product the creator would not use, a price trick.
- **How to score:** 5 if the move is a clear deposit (keeps a promise, refuses a bad sponsor). 3 if neutral. 1 if it withdraws trust for short-term revenue.
- **Quote:** "It's a micro deposit of trust that you're making in a public bank. So every right thing that you do, it's a deposit. Every wrong thing that you do, it's a withdrawal." (S007) "in India infact almost everything works on trust" (S019)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S008, S017, S019).

## 5. Values over views (say no to what works)
- **What it is:** Write down on day one what you will never do for views and stick to it. His office sign. Figuring Out skips the five biggest view categories in India (horror, religion, astrology, sex, entertainment), records about 220 to 230 episodes a year and publishes about 150, and has shelved clips worth tens of millions of views.
- **When to use:** A founder or creator tempted by a sensational format, a gossip clip, or a paid guest who wants promotion.
- **How to score:** 5 if non-negotiables are written and the plan respects them. 3 if unwritten but respected. 1 if the plan depends on sensationalism or on someone else's secrets.
- **Quote:** "values over views sense over sensationalism teachings over trends" (S007) "We say no to everything that works because we are very clear what to do." (S007) "make it clear what are the things you will never do, and then just stick to it" (S006)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S006, S007, S010).

## 6. 10 year framework
- **What it is:** Before entering any field ask two things: can I do this for 10 years, and am I okay to be underestimated, look like a loser, and look stupid for those 10 years. If yes, jump in. It answers the Gen Z stamina problem.
- **When to use:** Starting a channel, a brand, or a career switch; any founder judging an idea by month-three results.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder would keep going for 10 years with no early win. 3 if they would give it two years. 1 if the plan needs fast validation to survive.
- **Quote:** "I call it 10 year framework" (S007) "I'm okay if you underestimate me for 10 years because I know after 10 years I'm going to win." (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004 infinite game, S007, S017, S019).

## 7. Consistency as moat
- **What it is:** Every creator needs a moat (a CA's degree, a designer's tool mastery). With no credential, his was output: three videos a day, 700 to 800 in six months during the pandemic, then daily with no missed day since June 2020. Outwork everyone until a skill or pattern emerges.
- **When to use:** A founder with no credential or capital asking how to stand out.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a daily or near-daily publishing plan the founder can sustain. 3 if weekly. 1 if "when we have time".
- **Quote:** "until i come up with some kind of skill or expertise in content all i'm gonna do is i'm gonna outwork everybody" (S009) "if you are consistent for couple of years you will end up winning regardless of talent, competition, time, money etc." (S019)
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S014, S019, S006).

## 8. Content from questions *(unnamed; his production system)*
- **What it is:** Never "come up with content". Decide the outcome, then have the team ask you first-principles questions for about an hour a week on camera; editors chop it into a month of posts. Later, he replaced his three daily reading hours with recording podcasts and banked 12 months of content.
- **Team (2022):** the face, a script writer who studies what is working all day, a videographer who acts as "prompter" asking questions, and a data and upload person.
- **When to use:** A founder who says they have no time for content.
- **How to score:** 5 if one recording session a week or month feeds the calendar. 3 if the founder writes every post. 1 if content waits for inspiration.
- **Quote:** "you can definitely create content once a month for four to five hours" (S006) "Our 12 months of content is shot, edited, and ready in advance." (S006)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S009, S010, S014).

## 9. You are your niche, then expand
- **What it is:** For a starter: ask who am I, my age, my city, my dream, my biggest challenge; millions are like you. Or pick a narrow niche (D2C home care, not "entrepreneurship"), become everything for it, expand vertically, go broad after 100k and again at 500k. He revisits the five persona questions every three months.
- **How his view changed:** 2022 to 2025 for starters: niche down (S014, S006). 2026, for distribution at scale in India: niche-and-depth is "outdated"; trust lets you talk about anything (S007). Read both: niche to start, trust to scale.
- **How to score:** 5 if a starter has a narrow audience they belong to. 3 if broad but relatable. 1 if broad and generic with no personal angle.
- **Quote:** "You are your niche, and that is very powerful." (S006) "you become everything for someone not something for everyone" (S014)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S007, S014, S017).

## 10. Influence over follower count
- **What it is:** Revenue follows depth of trust, not reach. A postpartum yoga creator with about 50,000 followers earns about Rs 8 crore a year; an 80,000-subscriber sneaker channel charges Rs 10 lakh a video. High-paying niches beat big generic audiences.
- **When to use:** A founder who thinks they need a million followers before selling.
- **How to score:** 5 if a small audience has a clear paid product for a high-value need. 3 if audience is big but unmonetized. 1 if the plan is "grow first, sell someday".
- **Quote:** "doesn't matter how big numbers you have it matters that what is your influence over people" (S008) "If you have a niche channel which is dealing with high paying customers, you end up making more than average" (S014)
- **Sources:** 3 (S008, S014, S018).

## 11. Product first, then creator (House of X)
- **What it is:** His creator-commerce method: find white spaces in Amazon, quick commerce, MSME, distributor, DMart, and import-export data; build a differentiated product (5 to 6 months); then find the creator whose audience and story match (about a month); launch pre-orders in 7 days. Creators do story; the company does sourcing, ops, marketing.
- **Metrics he uses:** repeat rate (Blanko above 30% vs about 15% fragrance norm), returns (under 10%), and patience: 18 months before judging.
- **How to score:** 5 if the product is differentiated and the creator's story fits it. 3 if the creator is big but the product is generic. 1 if it is merch slapped with a face.
- **Quote:** "we are product first not Creator first so we identify white spaces" (S008) "I believe it takes at least 18 months for you to even test customers" (S008)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S010, S014, S018).

## 12. Topic first, then relationship
- **What it is:** Topic-based content brings people for the topic; relationship-based content brings them for you, and lasts across platforms. Start with topics in your market, slowly add personality. Top creators everywhere are podcasters, streamers, vloggers because of relationship.
- **Quote:** "you start with topics you build it up and slowly start putting your personality into it" (S009)
- **Sources:** 2 (S009, S014).

## 13. Respect the platform
- **What it is:** Do what the platform is promoting this quarter. Read Meta, Google, and Snap annual reports. Twitter threads took him from 20,000 to 200,000 in six months; Facebook 0 to 60,000 in 25 days. Instagram: hook in 3 to 6 seconds, juice first. YouTube: tell them what is coming, then build. Twitter: people share what makes them look smart.
- **Quote:** "whatever the platform is promoting in the next quarter do that" (S014) "you give the juice first and then the build up" (S009)
- **Sources:** 3 (S009, S013, S014).

## 14. Trend latching
- **What it is:** Take today's viral story and build a bridge to your niche (what jewelry Diljit wears; the bag Putin's bodyguard carries). Do it daily for six months. This is the controversial slice of ECG.
- **Quote:** "So, this is trending. This is my niche. I will just try to build a bridge between them ." (S006)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S014).

## 15. Micro geography and premium to villages *(from his detergent years)*
- **What it is:** With a small budget, saturate one colony instead of a city: an MNC cannot outspend you on 10 houses. Do not fight giants where they dominate; he sold a Rs 75 a kg dishwash gel against sub-Rs 50 brands by calling it "expensive detergent for your expensive clothes", built 200 distributors village by village, and painted autorickshaws for free in exchange for branding.
- **Quote:** "does he have more money to Market to these 10 houses in my Colony probably not" (S012) "you don't stand a chance in front of un lier or PNG in the place where they anyway dominate" (S012)
- **Sources:** 1 (S012). Low count, but it is his only detailed offline go-to-market record.

## 16. Give value first (human network)
- **What it is:** Reach people by offering them something they want, never by asking. One message a day, seven a week, 365 a year; before writing, check your intent, why they should care, and that you can deliver.
- **Quote:** "if you want someone to value you give them value first because if you won't give them value why would even someone talk to you" (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S010 name-place-activity).
