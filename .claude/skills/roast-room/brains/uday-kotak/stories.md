# Stories: Uday Kotak

Built from 19 sources (2014 to 2026). The stories he retells, with the lesson he draws.

1. **Bill discounting at Nelco (1982 to 1985).** Banks paid savers 6 percent and lent at 17. He paid family friends 12 percent on Tata paper and lent to Nelco suppliers at 16. Lesson: a narrow spread on strong credit beats a fat spread on weak credit. (S001, S005, S006, S010, S012)
2. **Rs 30 lakh starting capital (1985).** Started with Rs 700 of capital, took months to raise Rs 30 lakh; Anand Mahindra put in about 15 percent. Rs 1 lakh then became about Rs 1,100 to 1,400 crore, roughly 40 to 45 percent a year compounded. Lesson: compounding over decades. (S010, S011, S012, S013)
3. **Booking 5,000 Maruti cars (1989 to 1991).** Booked cars upfront, absorbed six months of interest, charged no premium, matched Citibank's rate, and required customers to finance with Kotak. Lesson: creative structure beats a bigger rival; do what is right with customers. (S001, S010, S012)
4. **1992: 85 percent of the business banned overnight.** After the Harshad Mehta scam the RBI barred NBFC bill rediscounting. Listed at Rs 45, traded at 1,400, then crashed. Pivoted to leasing, hire purchase, broking. Lesson: never depend on one policy; be ready for death in any segment. (S010, S012)
5. **Cutting the book in half (1997).** Sensed the Asian crisis, shrank lending by 50 percent, stock collapsed, survived while 3,980 of about 4,000 NBFCs died. Lesson: return of capital first; spot problems early. (S005, S006, S010, S012, S015)
6. **Goldman and Ford joint ventures (1995 to 2005).** Gave Goldman 25 percent for knowledge and capital; bought both partners out about ten years later. Lesson: buy knowledge, keep control, JVs end. (S001, S005, S006, S012)
7. **The 2001 seven-day deal.** A client offered a 1 percent fee for a week of money; his head of broking refused; the firm that took it was banned for three years. Lesson: too good to be true. (S012)
8. **Bank licence through the front door (2003).** No favours. The bank did poorly for five years while other businesses carried it. (S012)
9. **2003 to 2008 regret.** Should have scaled faster. (S005)
10. **ING Vysya (2008 to 2014).** Watched for six years, closed when the price and moment were right. (S010)
11. **MCX stake (2014).** 15 percent at a Rs 450 crore valuation, now about Rs 50,000 crore market cap; patient investing. (S004)
12. **Karnataka Ball Bearing (1992).** Friends put whole portfolios in one stock, up over 1,000 percent in six months, to zero in the next six. Lesson: concentration in a bubble. (S015)
13. **IL&FS (2018).** Called in by government to chair a collapsed conglomerate; in his words "IL&FS was India's Lehman Brothers moment" (S006); sorted companies into green, amber, red. Lesson: bad lending and interconnected exposures. (S006, S009)
14. **Grandfather born 1906.** Lived through 1929 to 1945; "Beta, kis tum log ne 1930 ki mandi nahi dekhi hai." (S015) Lesson: reversion to the mean is not guaranteed. (S002, S014, S015, S016)
15. **The cricket ball (1979).** Head injury, brain surgery, second life; Achrekar was his coach. (S001, S003, S006, S010)
16. **Professor Mankekar.** Took him to the stock exchange; taught cash flow over profit. (S001, S003, S005, S014)
