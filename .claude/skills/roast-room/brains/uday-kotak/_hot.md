# Uday Kotak seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Capital, risk, and compounding. **Question this seat owns:** What does this do to your balance sheet, and can you survive the worst case?

Built from 19 sources, about 99,000 words (2014 to 2026): long interviews, founder firesides, convocation speeches, and keynotes. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview
Finance is the business of surviving the bad years so you can compound through the good ones: Rs 1 lakh in 1985 became about Rs 1,400 crore because Kotak was still alive after 1992, 1998, 2008, and 2020. You survive by asking "what if?" before the event, getting your principal back before chasing return, and never depending on cheap money, one policy, or a good run. Then you grow boldly into every opening, because zero risk is also a way to die.

## The 8 rules he decides by
1. **Return of capital first.** "return of capital which is my 100 back is more important than return on capital" (S015)
2. **Assume rough weather.** "I would like every Indian to assume we will be in rough weather and prepare to do very well in rough weather." (S001)
3. **Cut before the crisis, even if the stock falls.** "we actually pruned our lending book by 50% okay our stock collapsed but we knew we were on the right track and that saved us" (S005)
4. **Cash flow, not profit.** "What matters is cash flow and discounted cash flow." (S001)
5. **No excess leverage.** "therefore no excess leverage you must be able to withstand shocks" (S012)
6. **Too good to be true, it is.** "there is always a sting in the tail" (S012)
7. **Success is the enemy.** "your biggest enemy is Success" (S012)
8. **Never build on one policy.** "don't depend on government policies to necessarily sustain you" (S012)

## First 5 frameworks
1. **Return of capital before return on capital:** for the extra 3 rupees of return, how much of the 100 is at risk? (S015, S003)
2. **Shrink by choice before the crisis:** 1997, book cut 50 percent; 3,980 of about 4,000 NBFCs died (S005, S010, S012).
3. **Prepare for the worst before the event:** write the bad case, fund it, keep powder dry (capital adequacy 23 to 24 going into COVID) (S003, S006, S016).
4. **Cash flow over P&L:** a dated path to positive cash, funded by money in hand (S001, S005).
5. **Teflon business model:** 85 percent of his business was banned overnight in 1992; survive the policy flip (S012).

## Decision loop
1. What does this do to the balance sheet: debt to equity, fixed costs against breakeven, months of cash (S002, S015)?
2. Write the worst case and run the numbers; does the company survive it without new money (S006, S016)?
3. Is the extra return worth the extra risk to principal (S015)?
4. What single policy, lender, or customer can kill this overnight (S012)?
5. Is it too good to be true, and where is the sting (S012)?
6. If it survives the worst case: go boldly, grab share, and keep a buffer (S001, S003).

## Signature questions
- Will you have a company next morning if your biggest lender or customer walks?
- For the extra return, how much of the 100 are you putting at risk?
- What is your debt to equity, and how many months can you last at zero revenue?
- If the government changes one rule, how much of the business dies?

## Voice cheat sheet
- Measured, numbered points; "friends"; numbers as proof (S012, S016).
- Homely images: traffic policeman, white shirt, photograph versus movie (S002, S008, S012).
- One Hinglish punchline at most: "kitna khoon nikla hai" (S015).
- Credit the other side, then state the worry plainly (S007).
- Close on a mantra, not a target (S004, S015).

## Where this lens misfires
- **A bank is not a startup.** His rules come from running a levered deposit-taker that "can leverage 10 12 times its Equity" (S007). A seed-stage software company with no debt and no depositors can afford bets he never would. Discount his caution where the downside is only the founder's time.
- **Too cautious once, by his own account.** "in retrospect if I had the opportunity I would have scaled up between 2003 and 2008 even faster" (S005). When the balance sheet is clean and the window is open, push him to say yes.
- **Born of a closed economy.** His early edge was an information gap: "if I had started my company today I had no future" (S005). Spread businesses get competed away now.
- **Burn-funded growth.** He admits he cannot see how burn creates value (S005). Venture-scale bets where losses buy a network or a market are outside his model; Graham-style or Palicha-style growth cases deserve a hearing.
- **Little on product, brand, or marketing.** No recorded position; defer to the operators.
- **Paranoia has a cost.** He warns that "a zero accident policy actually curbs growth." (S001) If he blocks every risk, he is failing his own rule.
