# Frameworks: Uday Kotak

Built from 19 sources (2014 to 2026): 17 long interviews, keynotes, and convocation speeches on YouTube and one news report of a 2025 speech. Quick depth. Most of his frameworks are unnamed patterns he repeats; names in *(unnamed)* are ours. Most-used first, with the balance-sheet and survival frameworks lifted to the top because they answer this seat's question. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance built from his stated rules, not quotes. Dates for S009 to S018 are inferred from content.

---

## 1. Return of capital before return on capital
- **What it is:** Before chasing a higher return, ask what extra risk you are taking on the principal itself. Getting your 100 back matters more than whether it becomes 107 or 110. He credits it with Kotak surviving the 1998 to 2003 NBFC wipeout.
- **Parts:** State the base return (the safe option). State the higher return on offer. Ask how much more of the 100 is at risk to earn the extra. Price the chance of zero. Keep a liquidity buffer so you never sell under pressure.
- **When to use:** Any bet that trades safety of principal for a few extra points: a riskier customer segment, a bigger loan, an illiquid investment, putting the cash buffer to work.
- **How to score:** 5 if the downside to principal is sized and survivable and the extra return clearly pays for it. 3 if the upside is real but nobody has sized what is lost if it fails. 1 if the extra return is small and the bet can take the 100 to zero.
- **Quote:** "return of capital which is my 100 back is more important than return on capital" (S015) "for incremental three rupees which I'm going to get between seven and 10 how much is the incremental risk I'm taking about on my 100 itself?" (S015)
- **Sources:** 3 (S015, S003 on 100 rupees becoming zero, S005 on survival). Inferred link to the 1990s survival from S015.

## 2. Shrink by choice before the crisis *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When he sensed trouble in 1997, Kotak cut its lending book by 50 percent while competitors kept growing. The stock fell. 3,980 of about 4,000 NBFCs died; Kotak lived. Growth you give up early is cheap; capital you lose late is fatal.
- **Parts:** Spot the problem before others do; assume no bailout is coming; cut exposure hard, early; accept the market punishing you; keep capital strong (the Goldman and Ford joint ventures had added capital); grow again when others are dead.
- **When to use:** A founder seeing early signs of a downturn, a stressed customer segment, or rising defaults, and wondering whether to keep growing.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan cuts exposure before the stress shows in numbers and the founder will live with lower growth. 3 if cuts are planned only after defaults rise. 1 if the plan is to grow through the warning signs.
- **Quote:** "we actually pruned our lending book by 50% okay our stock collapsed but we knew we were on the right track and that saved us" (S005) "contrary to all the other non-bank finance companies we actually shrunk our business by choice and reduced our lending book by 50 percent" (S010)
- **Sources:** 5 (S005, S006, S010, S012, S015).

## 3. Prepare for the worst before the event (paranoia) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Assume rough weather. Ask "what if?" every time. The question that keeps him awake: will I have a bank next morning? Success breeds complacency, and complacency starts the failure cycle.
- **Parts:** Write the bad scenario down, even at 5 to 20 percent odds; ask what happens if things do not go to plan; check theft and fraud risk and disruption risk; prepare now, while it is calm; do not let a good run convince you it is safe.
- **When to use:** Every decision. Especially after a strong year, or when the plan assumes the good times continue.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan names the worst case and the business survives it. 3 if the worst case is named but not funded. 1 if the plan only works in the base case.
- **Quote:** "I would like every Indian to assume we will be in rough weather and prepare to do very well in rough weather." (S001) "always keep in mind to ask the question, what if? What if things did not go as planned? What would happen?" (S006) "a sense of paranoia is the core to building sustainable business." (S004)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008, S011, S014, S016).

## 4. Cash flow over P&L
- **What it is:** Learned in his first corporate finance class from Professor Mankekar: profit is an opinion, cash flow decides. A funded company may burn for years, but it must have a plan to reach positive cash flow. He cannot see how burning more cash makes a company worth more.
- **Parts:** Look at cash generation, not reported profit; map the years of negative cash flow; name the year cash turns positive; ask if you have enough money in the bank to do what you need.
- **When to use:** Fundraising plans, burn decisions, growth bought with discounts, any plan where profit and cash diverge.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a dated path to positive cash flow funded by money already in hand. 3 if the path depends on the next round. 1 if burn is the strategy and there is no cash plan.
- **Quote:** "P&Ls don't matter. Profit and loss account doesn't matter. What matters is cash flow and discounted cash flow." (S001) "if you burn more cash you're more valuable but I I can't figure that out" (S005)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S003, S005, S014).

## 5. Too good to be true, it is
- **What it is:** The first lesson his firm internalized after 1992 and 2001. When an offer looks too easy, there is a sting in the tail, and it shows later. In 2001 his head of broking refused a 1 percent fee for seven days of money; a firm that took it was banned for three years.
- **Parts:** Notice when returns look far above the risk; go into detail; keep asking what can go wrong; be most careful when easy money is on the table.
- **When to use:** Unusually cheap money, a client offering premium terms, a partner promising outsized returns, a hot sector.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has found and priced the catch. 1 if the founder cannot explain why the deal is so good.
- **Quote:** "when something is too good to be true it is" (S012) "there is always a sting in the tail" (S012) "keep on asking the question what can go wrong" (S012)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S012, S015).

## 6. Deleverage while the window is open *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** In a crisis, the haves and have-nots are split by access to equity capital. If debt to equity is high and markets are open, raise equity now and pay down debt. Treat it as insurance.
- **Parts:** Check debt to equity and fixed costs against breakeven; raise from private equity, venture, or public markets while they are willing; cut fixed overhead; accept dilution as the premium.
- **When to use:** A leveraged company in a sensitive sector, or any founder offered capital in good times.
- **How to score:** 5 if leverage is low or being cut with fresh equity. 3 if leverage is high but covered by cash flow in a downturn. 1 if leverage is high and the plan adds more.
- **Quote:** "if you have excessive leverage that is your debt to equity ratio is high please go out there the capital markets are in good shape please raise capital and D leverage your businesses" (S002) "if you have access to capital particularly equity capital you have a much greater chance of surviving" (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S003, S012 on no excess leverage).

## 7. Keep powder dry *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Enter a crisis light, with spare capital, and treat the crisis as the time to grow. Kotak went into COVID at a capital adequacy of about 23 to 24 percent. Every crisis is an opportunity for the one still standing.
- **Parts:** Hold more capital than required; spot problems early; make fewer mistakes than everyone else; buy or grow when others are forced sellers.
- **When to use:** Deciding how much cash to hold, whether to spend the buffer, or whether to buy during a downturn.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder keeps a buffer that funds the next crisis and a move on it. 1 if every rupee is deployed.
- **Quote:** "we had kept a lot of our powder dry we are today at a capital adequacy of about 23 24" (S003) "number one is your ability to spot a problem before others do and number two is to make sure that in general you make less mistakes than everybody else" (S005)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S005, S015).

## 8. Teflon business model
- **What it is:** Never build a business that only works because of a favourable policy. In 1992 more than 85 percent of his business (bill discounting) was banned overnight by the RBI. Be ready for death in any segment.
- **Parts:** List what the business depends on that a regulator or government can change; estimate the share of revenue at risk; build a fallback line; use a policy tailwind but do not rely on it.
- **When to use:** Businesses that depend on a subsidy, a tax rule, an RBI or SEBI permission, GST treatment, a PLI scheme, or a single regulatory reading.
- **How to score:** 5 if the core still works if the policy flips. 3 if a single policy change cuts revenue by under a third. 1 if one notification can shut most of the business.
- **Quote:** "your ability to build a business model which is Teflon Beyond government policies is also extremely important" (S012) "be ready for death in any of your businesses any of your segments and don't depend on government policies to necessarily sustain you" (S012)
- **Sources:** 3 (S010, S012, S001 on staying within the law).

## 9. Professional entrepreneur
- **What it is:** Creativity starts a company; professionalism scales it. Of 100 players, two or three make it, because the rest take shortcuts or falter on execution. Once the concept works, add governance, process, and compliance fast.
- **Parts:** Keep the owner's mindset; add professional managers and process; stay within the law, and follow its spirit when it is grey; put governance in early, not after a scandal.
- **When to use:** A startup past product-market fit, a founder running everything personally, any plan that is "fast and loose" on regulation.
- **How to score:** 5 if governance and process grow with the business. 1 if growth runs ahead of controls.
- **Quote:** "the key to success is how quickly an entrepreneur can also embrace what i call as professionalism" (S003) "There is an entrepreneur in every professional and there should be a professional in every entrepreneur" (S014) "entrepreneurship needs the backbone of professionalism and the two together make it work" (S010)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S004, S010, S014).

## 10. Acquisition as a cheque
- **What it is:** Price every acquisition as if you were paying cash. Do not use a high share price as a free currency; investors will stop valuing it.
- **Parts:** Would you write this cheque? Can you earn your return on it over time? Then culture and people: treat the other side well.
- **When to use:** Acquisitions, especially paid in stock or with fresh funding.
- **How to score:** 5 if the price works as a cash cheque. 1 if it only works because the stock is expensive.
- **Quote:** "always think about an acquisition as if you're going to write a check and if you think at that check you can make your returns over time then it's a good acquisition" (S010)
- **Sources:** 2 (S010, S008 on no stupid deals).

## 11. Joint ventures are not forever
- **What it is:** Partner for a defined goal and time; know when to exit. Keep control (Goldman got 25 percent, Kotak 75). He bought back Goldman and Ford after about ten years.
- **When to use:** Strategic partnerships, giving equity to a big partner, co-ownership.
- **How to score:** 5 if control, purpose, and exit terms are clear. 1 if 50:50 with no exit.
- **Quote:** "joint ventures are not forever very difficult joint ventures forever" (S010) "Which is why they were 25% and we were 75. And Goldman was betting on us rather than us betting on Goldman." (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S005, S010, S012).

## 12. Never as good, never as bad (equanimity)
- **What it is:** Do not get carried away in boom or despair in bust. Biggest mistakes come from excess and hubris.
- **Quote:** "it is Never As Good As it looks it's never as bad as it looks" (S012) "when things are good, don't get too exuberant. And when things are bad, don't get too despondent." (S006)
- **How to score:** Mark down plans built on peak numbers or on panic.
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S012, S015).

## 13. Simplicity, prudence, humility
- **What it is:** His Davos 2009 answer on what a good bank looks like. Prudence means no excess leverage; simplicity means products a customer understands; humility means bankers are not masters of the universe.
- **Quote:** "a quality of prudence particularly in the risk management business and therefore no excess leverage you must be able to withstand shocks" (S012) "Bankers are not masters of the universe." (S006)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S012).

## 14. Spend on what builds the business, nothing else *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Ruthless on overhead (he cut office tables to fit more people), generous on what wins business (a good hotel so the counterparty took him seriously).
- **How to score:** 5 if costs map to revenue. 1 if the plan carries prestige overhead.
- **Quote:** "for anything which is a wasteful overhead and which is not adding to your business or your Top Line don't waste money but anything which is critical for your business don't save money" (S005)
- **Sources:** 1 (S005). Low confidence, but vivid.

## 15. Minimize accidents, not zero accidents
- **What it is:** A zero-accident policy kills growth. Aim to minimize losses, not to eliminate all risk. The counterweight to his paranoia.
- **Quote:** "A zero accident policy is a high risk to growth. Minimizing accidents is important, but a zero accident policy actually curbs growth." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S007).

## 16. Greedy, but not too greedy; measure the blood
- **What it is:** Buy when others panic, but size how deep the damage is. Concentrate enough to make real money, keep a small pocket for fear.
- **Quote:** "be greedy, but not too greedy." (S015) "Money is made when blood is flowing on the street. Okay? But you got to figure out kitna khoon nikla hai." (S015) "in your chase for returns, keep a small pocket for fear of risk." (S015)
- **Sources:** 1 (S015).

## 17. Five Cs
- **What it is:** Character, conviction, commitment, creativity, capability, plus culture. Test conviction against foolhardiness before committing.
- **Quote:** "always question yourself between conviction and foolhardiness before being sure it is conviction" (S013) "culture eats strategy for breakfast every single day" (S013)
- **Sources:** 1 (S013).

## 18. Roti: return on time invested
- **What it is:** Discounted cash flow applied to a life: measure the return on each hour, financial or not.
- **Quote:** "It's discounted cash flow of a lifespan." (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001).
