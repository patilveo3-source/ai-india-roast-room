# Frameworks: Anupam Mittal

Built from 17 sources (10 long videos and clips, 2021 to 2026, and 7 articles quoting him, 2015 to 2025). Quick depth. Most-used first. He names few frameworks, so most are *(unnamed)*. How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated thresholds; they are not quotes.

---

## 1. Index two-thirds on the founder *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** At the early stage there are no proof points and the plan will change, so most of the call is the founder.
- **Parts:** (1) evidence of exceptionalism at one thing (execution, selling, product, vision); (2) business acumen: margins, profit pools, cost structure, tested by handing the founder a marker at the whiteboard; (3) a non-obvious truth about the space that gives the right to play; plus sincerity, integrity, perseverance, and chemistry for a 10-year ride.
- **When to use:** Any seed or angel decision; any "should I back this founder" question.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder is the best you have met at one thing, can draw the unit economics unprompted, and holds a non-obvious insight. 3 if two of three. 1 if the pitch rests on market size or pedigree alone.
- **Quote:** "twothirds of the indexing has to be done on founder." (S001) "what's giving you the right to play?" (S001)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S006, S009, S012, S013, S016, S019).

## 2. Valuation is supply and demand at the early stage *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** In theory, valuation is discounted future cash flow. With no history, a startup's price is set by how many investors want in and how many similar deals exist. Hot markets push it up, down markets crash it.
- **Parts:** risk-free rate, then about 10 to 13% for listed equity, then about 30% IRR for private equity and 50% for early-stage investors like him; the ask means nothing, the market sets the price; do not pick an offer by the colour of the money.
- **When to use:** A founder anchoring on a number, or choosing between term sheets.
- **How to score:** 5 if the valuation matches what competing investors would pay and the founder can grow into it. 3 if a stretch with real competing interest. 1 if the price is self-set with no buyers, or so high that the next round must be a down round.
- **Quote:** "valuation becomes a game of supply and demand not of future cash flow." (S001) "So market determines your valuation." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S012 on seed valuations falling from $15 million to $5 million in the correction).

## 3. Raising equity is a life choice *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Equity is the most expensive capital. It commits the founder to about 10 years of very high growth (100% a year for five years, then 50%) to return 30 to 50% IRR, more rounds, and partners at the table.
- **When to use:** Before any first raise.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has said what life they want and the business needs equity to win. 1 if they raise because others raise or for headlines.
- **Quote:** "the choice of raising  capital is a choice of what type of life you want." (S001) "early stage investors  like me expect 50%." (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S009, S011).

## 4. The capital ladder, cheapest first *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Customer money first (no interest, no equity, real validation), then friends, family and fools (patient), then government schemes and grants (CGTMSE, schemes for women founders), then bank, NBFC or revenue-based debt for working capital, and equity last.
- **When to use:** A founder who thinks equity is the only money available.
- **How to score:** 5 if customers or cheap debt fund the next step. 1 if equity funds what customers would have paid for.
- **Quote:** "It's the cheapest form of capital because you give away no interest.  You give away no equity." (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S008, S012).

## 5. Anti-dilution math on a high valuation *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Raise too high, miss the plan, raise again lower, and anti-dilution makes the old investor whole at the new price. Do it twice and the founder is nearly out.
- **When to use:** A founder choosing the highest of several offers.
- **How to score:** 5 if the price leaves room to raise the next round higher. 1 if the plan has to be perfect to avoid a down round.
- **Quote:** "So that's the danger of raising money at high  valuations." (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001).

## 6. Team, TAM, timing (the three Ts)
- **What it is:** His three early-stage filters. Team first; a large market because "large markets are very forgiving"; and timing (D2C works now because logistics and UPI exist).
- **How to score:** 5 if all three line up; 3 if team and market are strong but timing is early; 1 if the market is small.
- **Quote:** "the first two T's the third T timing also has to be equally important" (S009) "large markets are very forgiving." (S016)
- **Sources:** 3 (S009, S016, S006 on timing).

## 7. Power law portfolio and angel discipline *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A few companies return the whole portfolio, so make many small bets, learn through angel groups, and keep a template. No FOMO.
- **Parts:** start with a 20 lakh corpus at 1 lakh each; Rs 25 to 50 lakh is his usual ticket and Rs 50 lakh to a crore his ceiling per company; startup investing only a slice of wealth already made; expect 50 to 60% to fail.
- **Quote:** "the one thing that you don't do is for more and the other thing that you always do is discipline" (S006) "I believe if 50 to 60 percent of your companies are not failing, then you are not taking enough risk" (S012)
- **Sources:** 5 (S005, S006, S007, S012, S015).

## 8. Paper wealth is not wealth *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Angels cannot force outcomes, sit last in the preference stack, and depend on rare exit windows in India. Count only cash returned.
- **Quote:** "paper wealth is not wealth" (S002) "cash on cash return is the most important metric" (S002)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001 on thin Indian M&A, S002, S014, S015).

## 9. Vote with the wallet *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Validate cheaply: a WhatsApp group of a few hundred strangers, AI images, then a fake storefront that takes real orders and refunds them.
- **Quote:** "the real validation comes  when people vote with their wallet." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S008 on Shaadi doubling down once people paid).

## 10. Play to your fitrat, margins from desirability
- **What it is:** Build the business that suits your nature. His nature is desirability and brand, so he wants high gross margins and negative working capital, not operations.
- **Quote:** "Aapko apni fitrat samajhna hai." (S008) "I believe that margins come from desirability." (S008)
- **Sources:** 1 (S008).

## 11. Why startups fail *(unnamed)*
- **Parts:** co-founder chemistry and world view; founder-investor fit; weak opinions and no go-to-market the founder will fight for; emotional attachment that stops a timely pivot.
- **Quote:** "first and foremost is founder chemistry and world views" (S006)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S008).

## 12. Engage, then educate (pitching)
- **What it is:** Win attention in five minutes, then teach the investor the business; the cheque is a by-product.
- **Quote:** "Before I can sell you, I need your engagement." (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001).

## 13. Tailwind times gap *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Find a gap (India is unbranded in most categories) and a tailwind (social media, AI, a multipolar world, defence) that multiply.
- **Quote:** "India is highly unbranded in every category" (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S005, S009).

## 14. Senior hiring: meals and unnamed reference checks
- **What it is:** One office meeting and two meals outside, 90 minutes each, plus reference checks with people the candidate did not name, ideally before the interview. Ask "would you hire him again".
- **Quote:** "the best question to ask is would you hire him again that forces honesty" (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001).
