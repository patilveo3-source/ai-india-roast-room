# Stories: Anupam Mittal

Built from 17 sources (10 long videos and clips, 2021 to 2026, and 7 articles quoting him, 2015 to 2025). 12 stories, most-used first, with the lesson.

1. **Millions in his twenties, wiped out in the dot-com crash.** At MicroStrategy in the US he became a multi-millionaire on stock options; the stock fell from 666 dollars to about 3 after an accounting scandal, and he had bought other dot-com stocks on margin. "in my early 20s, I had made something big and lost it all." (S016) "we became Devils of Wall Street from Darlings overnight" (S002) Lesson: paper wealth and leverage vanish; fear and failure build character (S002, S004, S007, S016).

2. **Shaadi.com built on customer money.** Three designers and five engineers in India for about $1,000 a month, 10 to 20 sites and IT services, then a double-down once people paid. "Oh, people are going to pay for this. So, let's stop the IT services. Let's double down on this." (S008) Lesson: customers are the cheapest capital and the real validation (S001, S008).

3. **Buying shaadi.com for $25,000 after the crash.** All the cash they had, for a domain nobody valued. "Very instinctive decision. Screw it. Let's do it." (S008) He now spends about half what rivals spend on marketing (S007). Lesson: pay for the asset that makes you the category (S007, S008).

4. **The first angel cheque, Interactive Avenues (2007).** About a million dollars to four agency employees he met at the airport; became the largest shareholder; sold for several hundred crores, all cash. "i looked at it more as backing a bunch of guys who i knew would win" (S006) Lesson: back the team in a market that must exist; cash exits are the best returns (S006, S009, S013, S015).

5. **Ola: the founder who answered his own phone (2011).** No taxis in Delhi; he called Ola's call centre and Bhavish picked up. Invested "when it was illegal" (S008), at a valuation "that will make you laugh and then cry" (S013). Lesson: evidence of extraordinary drive beats benchmarks (S002, S006, S008, S009, S013).

6. **Lehman, 2008: the round that never closed.** Signed to raise about $25 million at a $120 million valuation; the money never came. "for the first time we had to let people go and we had to wind down businesses" (S007) Lesson: never plan expansion on money not in the bank (inferred from S007).

7. **Makaan.com, self-funded to an exit.** Investors would not fund it; he spun it out, financed it for 12 to 18 months from his Interactive Avenues money, read the consolidation coming, and sold in 2015. "my investors didn't want to fund it anymore so we spun it out" (S009) Lesson: back your own conviction when the market is consolidating (S004, S009).

8. **Up and down the US for money, then Sequoia (2005 to 2006).** "Pura US up and down karlia par kuch hua nahi" (S018) The first cheque came from Sequoia, SVB and WestBridge. Lesson: persistence; India had almost no VCs then (S018).

9. **Animall from a cattle fair.** At the Pushkar fair he saw a broken market with many small sellers and buyers, came back to Bombay and backed Animall (S001). Lesson: theses can come from keeping eyes open.

10. **The mobility thesis (2011 to 2012).** "mobility in India is broken in every segment" (S001) First investor in Ola, Rapido, Zingbus, Porter; exited Chalo well (S001, S002, S013). Lesson: a researched thesis across segments.

11. **The candidate everyone loved.** Four interviewers praised her communication; he failed her for saying the same thing five ways. "People with good communication skills get to the point." (S001) Lesson: articulate means brief.

12. **Business at 13.** A book-rental library, a sports club at 16, five or six businesses that failed, all about selling (S001). "I failed 10 times, but I still kept feeling it's somebody else's fault" (S008) Lesson: sell early; ego gets you through the early failures (S001, S008).
