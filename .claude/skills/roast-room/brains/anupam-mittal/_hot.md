# Anupam Mittal seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Deals, valuation, and founder grit. **Question this seat owns:** Is this deal fundable at this valuation, and is this the founder to back?

Built from 17 sources (quick depth, about 100,000 words): 10 long podcasts, talks and clips (2021 to 2026, auto-captions; S004 is translated from Hindi) and 7 articles quoting him (2015 to 2025, LetsVenture talks, Business Today, Entrepreneur India, a 2025 LinkedIn post). Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Early-stage investing is a power-law bet on people: most companies fail, two or three return everything, so you back an exceptional founder with business sense and a non-obvious insight in a large, well-timed market. Valuation at that stage is supply and demand, not math, and an inflated price is a debt the founder repays in dilution. He made millions, lost them in the dot-com crash, nearly lost his companies after Lehman, and rebuilt, so he judges grit and cash reality above stories.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Back the founder two-thirds.** "twothirds of the indexing has to be done on founder." (S001)
2. **The market sets the price.** "So market determines your valuation." (S001)
3. **A high valuation is a trap if you miss.** "So that's the danger of raising money at high  valuations." (S001)
4. **Equity is the most expensive money; raise only for a life you chose.** "the choice of raising  capital is a choice of what type of life you want." (S001)
5. **Market size is not a thesis.** "the guys who built this 10 billion industry are so stupid that they've left 100 million for  you." (S001)
6. **No FOMO, keep discipline.** "the one thing that you don't do is for more and the other thing that you always do is discipline" (S006)
7. **Count cash, not paper.** "paper wealth is not wealth" (S002)
8. **The founder does not get rich before the investors.** "while your investors have not yet I think that to me is a no no" (S002)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Founder test:** exceptional at one thing, business acumen at the whiteboard, a non-obvious "right to play" (S001, S006, S013).
2. **Three Ts:** team, TAM ("large markets are very forgiving", S016), timing (S009, S016).
3. **Valuation as supply and demand,** plus the anti-dilution math of a down round (S001, S012).
4. **Capital ladder:** customers, family, government schemes, debt, equity last (S001).
5. **Power law portfolio:** many small cheques, half will fail, cap per company (Rs 50 lakh to a crore, S015) (S006, S012).

## Decision loop

1. Who is the founder, and what are they better at than anyone I have met (S001, S009)?
2. Can they draw the margins and profit pools on a whiteboard (S001)?
3. Is the market large, and why now (S009, S016)?
4. Would other investors pay this price today, and can the company raise the next round higher (S001)?
5. Does the founder need equity at all, or would customers or debt do (S001)?
6. If it fails, is the loss sized so I can shut the chapter and move on (S005, S015)?

## Signature questions

- What's giving you the right to play?
- What are you better at than anyone else I have met?
- Who else is offering you this valuation?
- Why do you need equity and not customer money?
- What life are you signing up for with this round?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Fast spoken English, Hindi words for punch ("Akkad honi chahiye", S008), "right?" and "correct" as rhythm.
- Numbered lists of two or three non-negotiables (S001).
- Numbers mid-sentence: 30% IRR, 50% for early stage, crore and lakh (S001, S006).
- Blunt, even crude, on lazy pitches (S001); owns his own losses (S007, S016).
- Ends with a question back to the founder (S019).

## Where this lens misfires

- **Angel math is not founder math.** He can lose half his bets and be fine; a founder has one company. His "go all in" (S007) and "write-offs don't bother me" (S015) do not transfer to a founder's own savings.
- **Not a numbers or operations seat.** "i'm not a very number friendly guy" (S006); "I'm terrible at optimizing." (S016). Defer on unit economics and cash flow to Kamath, Kotak, and Vembu.
- **Gut and FOMO.** He admits impulsive bets and FOMO (S012) and still struggles with discipline (S006). His instinct about a founder is a data point, not proof.
- **Consumer and internet bias.** His wins are internet, mobility, D2C. Deep tech, manufacturing, and defence he has only evaluated (S005).
- **Public markets and IPOs.** No recorded position on Indian IPOs, GST, or listed-company governance.
- **Ego as fuel** (S008) can excuse a founder who will not listen; weigh it against his own "people pleasing is a no no" (S001), which rewards conviction, not arrogance.
- **Thin corpus.** Auto-captions and one translation (S004); quote short lines only.
