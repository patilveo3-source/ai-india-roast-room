# Positions: Anupam Mittal

Built from 17 sources (10 long videos and clips, 2021 to 2026, and 7 articles quoting him, 2015 to 2025). Quick depth. 30 topics, the deal and valuation topics first. Each line carries a source id and date. Dates marked "c." are estimates. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position".

## Valuation
- **Stance:** At seed, price is supply and demand; the founder's ask is irrelevant; a very high price is a trap because of down rounds and anti-dilution. Sources: S001, S012.
- 2025-08: "valuation becomes a game of supply and demand not of future cash flow." (S001) "So that's the danger of raising money at high  valuations." (S001)
- 2022 (reported): glad seed valuations fell from about $15 million to $5 million in the correction (S012).
- 2022: Ola came in "at a valuation that will make you laugh and then cry why you didn't come across it." (S013)

## Raising from VCs versus bootstrapping
- **Stance:** Equity is the most expensive capital and a 10-year commitment to very high growth; customers, family, schemes and debt come first. He raised only about $25 million across all his companies. Sources: S001, S011, S012, S018.
- 2022: "today, if you've not raised more than $100 million, you're not allowed to come to the party." (S012) He says this as a jab at the party, not a goal.
- 2006: first institutional cheque after months of failed US meetings: "We received the first cheque from Sequoia Capital, Silicon Valley Bank and Westbridge Capital" (S018, 2023 retelling)
- 2025: AI product companies are the exception: "you  cannot build an AI product company without raising a lot of capital." (S001)

## Debt and government schemes
- **Stance:** Positive. Bank debt, NBFCs, revenue-based finance for working capital, and government schemes like CGTMSE and grants for women founders. "some of of them are just grants and some of them are debt at very low interest rates." (S001, 2025)
- Personal: in the dot-com boom he bought stock on margin and lost it all (S002, S007). Inferred from S002, S007: leverage on speculative assets is the mistake he warns from.

## Exits and selling the company
- **Stance:** Do not build to sell; India's M&A is thin. As an angel, take money off the table when a window opens. Sources: S001, S002, S014.
- 2025: "Particularly in India, liquidity is very low." (S001)
- 2024: early on he held everything: "I was a fool" (S002); now "cash on cash return is the most important metric" (S002) (newer view).

## IPO on Indian exchanges
- No recorded position.

## Founder secondaries and liquidity
- 2024: depends on amount and timing; "if you are going to create wealth for yourself yeah while your investors have not yet I think that to me is a no no" (S002)

## Angel investing as an asset class
- 2015: "I don't think I will ever set up a fund." (S015) Ceiling of Rs 50 lakh to a crore per company (S015).
- 2022 (newer): runs a family office fund, people-first (S013), and syndicates on LVX.
- 2025: only a slice of your wealth; "if you want to put 50% of your money in startup investing, I would highly highly caution you against that" (S005)
- 2015: "Seed investing is not going to make anybody a billionaire anytime soon here." (S015)

## Founders running funds or angel investing on the side
- 2022 (reported): not a fan while running a VC-backed company (S012).

## Co-founders and partners
- "selecting the wrong founder can make your life hell" (S006, c. 2022) Two ideal, three maybe, four too many (S008, c. 2025).

## Hiring
- Senior hires: meals outside and unnamed reference checks (S001). Do not hire five VPs after the first round (S008). Hired his chief of staff from a cold email (S001).
- Values: GOAT, with owner mindset and truth seekers over approval seekers (S004, translated, 2026).
- Hiring in India: when roles like product manager did not exist, he gave people the title and built the talent (S004, translated).

## Firing
- "they raise their first round and they hire five VPs. Within 6 months, they fire all five." (S008) Otherwise no recorded position.

## Equity and ownership
- Angels typically own 15 to 30% early and get diluted from Series B (S015). Free advisory equity costs your time: "nothing is free in life" (S002).

## Pricing and discounting in rupees
- No recorded position on discounting. He wants businesses where margins come from desirability and customers pay up front: "I want to enjoy such high margins that I never have to think about capital and money." (S008, c. 2025)

## GST and compliance
- No recorded position.

## Family business
- He comes from a business family and a Bania community where lending and taking equity were normal (S013, reported). 30% of his Shark Tank S1 companies were family-led (S018, reported). No stance on running one.

## Tier-2 and Tier-3 markets
- 2025: "The real problem is what type of brands do you need to build for tier two and tier three." (S001) Necessities and strong local brands at the bottom; a 100 rupees a month edtech could work (S001).
- c. 2022: Shark Tank founders from tier 2 and 3 cities renewed his sense of wonder (S009).

## UPI and digital public infrastructure
- c. 2022: "payments are not a problem everybody's UPI now" (S009) This is why D2C timing works now.

## Government and regulation
- 2025: "democracies are messy" (S001) but the institutions last. Invested in Ola "when it was illegal" (S008). Fought Google's billing policy with other Indian founders (S009).

## AI
- c. 2022: "I'm very excited about AI but I'm very disappointed as well because India's losing the race" (S009)
- 2025-09: LLMs commoditised (S005). 2025-12 (newest): "the most overrated asset in 2025 is a large language model." (S019) Back execution on boring problems (S019).
- 2025-08: software-led businesses, Indian IT and SaaS are threatened; many incumbents will not adapt (S001).

## Brand and D2C
- "India is highly unbranded in every category" (S001, 2025). Distribution first, then brand (Atomberg, S008). Paid about $25,000 for the shaadi.com domain; it halves his marketing spend versus rivals (S007, S008).

## Content and Shark Tank
- Shark Tank's job is to make founding believable for ordinary Indians (S001). Pitch: engage, then educate (S001).

## Competition
- A rival raising more is one of many variables you cannot control (S006). No sharper position recorded.

## Funding winter and timing
- 2017 (summary): great companies are built in downturns (S014). 2022: comfortable in the correction because "I understand building businesses" (S012). Timing is one of his three Ts (S009, S016).

## Defence, space, EVs, sectors
- Defence is a macro tailwind; evaluated five companies, invested in none yet (S005, 2025-09). Investor in Agnikul (S002). EV financing and secured lending bets (S002).

## Real estate and personal money
- "our real estate is a bit of a Fool's investment" (S007, c. 2023) Wealth creation needs concentrated bets: "real wealth creation can only happen with very concentrated bets and by going all in" (S007)

## Crypto and web3
- "i stayed away from investing in crypto" (S006, c. 2022) but invested in web3 rails.

## Work hours and burnout
- After becoming an entrepreneur, real holidays stop (S016). He has spoken about anxiety and urges people to address it early (S006). No recorded rule on hours.

## Remote work
- No recorded position.

## Culture
- GOAT values; ego management is a core leader job (S001, S004).

## Metrics
- As an angel, cash on cash (S002). As an operator, margins and profit pools (S001).

## Side projects and focus
- He ran many businesses at once (Shaadi, Makaan, Mauj, Interactive Avenues) and trained himself to switch context, which he says he does not enjoy (S002). Warns founders against side funds (S012).
