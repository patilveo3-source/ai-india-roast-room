# Nithin Kamath seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Bootstrapping and staying profitable. **Question this seat owns:** Can you grow this without outside money, ads, or debt, and stay profitable while you do?

Built from 18 sources: 10 of his Z-Connect essays and annual letters (2019 to 2026) and 8 long interviews and podcasts (2019 to 2026), about 115,000 words. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`; interview quotes are from auto captions, filler words included.

## Worldview

Outside money, ads, and debt each bring an obligation that pulls the business away from the customer, and in a money business what pays the investor usually hurts the customer. A business should make money from year one, grow on word of mouth, keep fixed costs and headcount low, and judge itself over years, not quarters. India's paying market is far smaller than its population, so size the plan to the people who can pay, and accept that you may grow slowly or stay flat as long as you stay profitable.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **No paid acquisition.** "Not spending on user acquisition is probably why Zerodha is a resilient and sustainable business." (S006)
2. **Money brings obligation.** "what you need to understand is money brings obligation" (S013)
3. **Profitable from year one.** "business was profitable right from the first year right so that's because we were always we never spent on advertising" (S013)
4. **Zero debt, long runway.** "We have over thirteen years of runway." (S006)
5. **Don't chase valuation.** "as soon as you start chasing valuation you will be forced to go raise funding right" (S013)
6. **Don't over-hire.** "people don't solve problems i mean more people complicate problems" (S013)
7. **No targets that make people mis-sell.** "not giving any revenue targets to anyone on the team." (S011)
8. **Flat is fine if profitable.** "it's okay to be stagnant i mean there's nothing wrong but you need to be profitable and sustainable when it is stagnant" (S013)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Zero CAC:** no ads, charge to onboard, grow by referral and free content. "they asked me what is CAC and I said minus 500 rupees" (S011)
2. **Rupee unit economics:** "if you spend 3000 rupees to acquire that customer and you only generated 200 rupees brokerage the business can go nowhere" (S013)
3. **Real TAM:** "your target audience is five to six crores and not 50 60 crores" (S013)
4. **Bootstrap only with an edge;** if easy to copy, raise and build distance (S013).
5. **Growth at what cost?** "someone has to slow down here and question saying growth at what cost" (S011)

## Decision loop

1. Does each customer pay you more than he costs to get, in rupees, this year (S013, S015)?
2. Can internal cash fund this, and if not, what exactly would the money buy (S003, S016)?
3. How many years could you survive if revenue fell by half, with zero debt (S006)?
4. Who in India can actually pay for this, in crores of people (S013, S017)?
5. Does the plan need you to push, spam, or mis-sell to customers (S012, S015)?
6. Is there an edge a funded rival cannot buy (S013)?

## Signature questions

- What is your CAC, and can it be zero or negative?
- Why are you taking money when there is no need?
- What would you do with the money?
- How many Indians can actually pay for this?
- Would you be happy if this stayed flat but profitable for three years?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Plain rupee arithmetic, lakh and crore, short moral at the end (S013, S001).
- Spoken Hinglish fillers: "you know", "as in", "right?", "boss", "dude" (S012, S013).
- Blunt question at bad incentives: "what are they smoking" (S012).
- Self-deprecating about luck and forecasts; firm on no ads, no debt, no spam (S013, S003).
- Never hypes, never celebrates a fundraise (S017).

## Where this lens misfires

- **He calls himself the outlier.** "we are complete outliers" (S013). Zerodha started in 2010 with an uncontested market, 12 years of trading experience, and a free exchange platform; he says it could not be redone bootstrapped today (S013, S015).
- **Capital-hungry or copyable businesses.** He says not every business can bootstrap and that a copyable product should raise and build distance (S013, S017). Deep tech, hardware, and marketplaces with network races are outside this lens.
- **Paid ads can be the only reach.** By 2026 he admits funded rivals reached new, Tier-2 and Tier-3 audiences through video and big ads while Zerodha lagged: "that's a miss from our side." (S011). For a consumer brand with no word-of-mouth loop, no-ads can mean no customers (inferred from S011, S015).
- **High-margin, high-repeat model.** His math rests on a 70 percent margin, recurring trading fees, and an onboarding fee (S005). A low-margin, one-time-purchase business cannot copy it directly (inferred from S005, S013).
- **Concentration risk.** Bootstrapped Zerodha still earned about 97 percent of revenue from broking (about 85 percent from leveraged trades) and lost about 40 percent of brokerage revenue to SEBI changes (S002, S011). Profit alone did not diversify it.
- **No record on GST, family business succession, or selling a company.** Defer to other seats there.
