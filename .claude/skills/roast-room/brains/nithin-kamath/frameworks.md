# Frameworks: Nithin Kamath

Built from 18 sources: 10 of his own Z-Connect essays and annual letters (2019 to 2026) and 8 long interviews and podcasts (2019 to 2026), about 115,000 words. Quick depth.

14 frameworks. He names few of them, so most are *(unnamed)* patterns read across sources. Most-used first. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated rules and numbers; they are not quotes.

---

## 1. Zero CAC: grow on word of mouth, never on ads *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Do not pay to acquire customers. Ads and paid acquisition wire the business to recover that cost from the customer, which leads to pushing, spamming, and mis-selling. Charge something up front if you can, so acquisition cost turns negative.
- **Parts:** No ads, no paid campaigns; charge an onboarding fee that covers the real cost; let happy customers refer; share a small cut with referrers; publish openly (blog, forum, free education) as the substitute for marketing.
- **When to use:** Any plan that leans on paid ads, cashbacks, or freebies to grow.
- **How to score:** 5 if growth comes from referrals and organic content and acquisition cost is near zero or negative. 3 if some paid spend but payback inside a few months from real revenue. 1 if CAC is several times what a customer earns you in a year.
- **Quote:** "they asked me what is CAC and I said minus 500 rupees" (S011) "if you spend 3000 rupees to acquire that customer and you only generated 200 rupees brokerage the business can go nowhere" (S013)
- **Sources:** 10 (S005, S006, S007, S008, S011, S012, S013, S014, S015, S017).

## 2. Money brings obligation *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Outside money is not free. VC is a loan with a liquidation preference; it brings a story you must keep selling, a horizon you must hit, and a person you must answer. Raise only if there is a concrete use that the business cannot fund itself.
- **Parts:** Ask: what will I do with the money; can I predict revenue well enough to promise anything; would I lose the freedom to say no to customers' bad requests and investors' growth asks.
- **When to use:** Any decision to raise equity, take debt, or list.
- **How to score:** 5 if the money funds a specific thing internal cash cannot and the founder can predict returns. 3 if the money speeds a business that already works. 1 if the raise is for PR, peer pressure, or valuation.
- **Quote:** "what you need to understand is money brings obligation" (S013) "I think venture capital is is is as I think it's a glorified loan" (S017)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S012, S013, S014, S016, S017, S018, S002).

## 3. Growth at what cost? *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** The question every founder should put to any growth plan. Resilience and profit come before growth; capitalism pushes the other way, so someone has to slow down.
- **When to use:** A plan to grow faster than profits fund, or to use dark patterns, notifications, or leverage to lift numbers.
- **How to score:** 5 if growth is funded from profit and does not harm the customer. 1 if growth needs pushing customers into products that hurt them.
- **Quote:** "someone has to slow down here and question saying growth at what cost" (S011) "a business that can ask itself “growth at what cost?”" (S008)
- **Sources:** 5 (S005, S008, S011, S012, S018).

## 4. The investor and customer conflict *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** In money businesses, what pays the investor usually hurts the customer (more trades, more loans, more spam). Not having investors is what lets you do right by the customer, and that becomes the moat.
- **Quote:** "See in the business of money what is right for the investor is hardly ever right for the customer." (S012) "One of the biggest moats we have is the ability, based on our conviction, to say and to do things that are hard for others to do." (S005)
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can name a customer-friendly practice that funded rivals cannot copy. 1 if the plan copies what funded rivals do.
- **Sources:** 6 (S005, S012, S015, S017, S002, S001).

## 5. Long customer life over big take *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Earn less per customer over more years. A customer who survives keeps paying and refers others; one squeezed in a month leaves.
- **Quote:** "if the customer is going to survive 10 years I rather make 300 rupees a month over 10 years" (S014) "instead of making 1,000 rupees in one year you'll make 1,000 rupees over five years" (S012)
- **How to score:** 5 if pricing and product keep customers for years. 1 if revenue depends on short-lived, high-churn customers.
- **Sources:** 4 (S012, S014, S015, S010).

## 6. Real TAM, not population *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** India is not 140 crore buyers. Count people with discretionary income: about 5 to 6 crore for an online business, at most 10 to 20 crore. Size the business, hiring, and fundraising to that number.
- **Quote:** "your target audience is five to six crores and not 50 60 crores" (S013) "in India you know there is there are not more than 10 cr people who make more than 2 lakh rupees a year." (S012)
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan sizes to paying Indians. 1 if the plan multiplies population by a hoped-for penetration rate.
- **Sources:** 5 (S012, S013, S015, S017, S009).

## 7. Bootstrap only with an edge *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Bootstrapping works when you have an edge rivals cannot buy (years of credibility, a hard tech problem solved, an uncontested market). If the product is easy to copy, raise money and build distance. Zerodha was lucky and is an outlier.
- **Quote:** "you can i think be bootstrapped when you have a edge over the competition in some form right" (S013) "if it is very easily copyable boss raise money and put some distance on your competition" (S013)
- **How to score:** 5 if the edge is real and hard to buy. 3 if the edge is cost or founder skill. 1 if the only edge is being first.
- **Sources:** 4 (S013, S015, S016, S017).

## 8. Runway and net worth as safety *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Measure safety as years you could survive at zero revenue, and net worth against what you owe customers. Zero debt. Keep fixed costs low because "luxuries become necessities."
- **Quote:** "We have over thirteen years of runway." (S006) "We have been a zero-debt business from the start." (S006)
- **How to score:** 5 if the business can survive two-plus years with revenue falling sharply and has no debt. 1 if one bad quarter needs a raise or a loan.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S006, S018).

## 9. Lean team: people complicate problems *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Do not throw people at problems. Hire slowly for culture; own your tech for economies of scale; grow customers without growing headcount.
- **Quote:** "people don't solve problems i mean more people complicate problems" (S013) "Fewer than 100 people across tech, product, business, and operations run everything." (S001)
- **How to score:** 5 if revenue per head rises as you grow. 1 if the plan is to hire ahead of revenue.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S013, S017, S018, S011).

## 10. No targets: no sales team, no revenue targets *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Targets make people mis-sell. Remove them, and judge the business on long trends.
- **Quote:** "if I put a sales Target someone's gonna sell something to someone there's no other way humans will take the easy way out" (S015) "not giving any revenue targets to anyone on the team." (S011)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S013, S015, S017).

## 11. 5-year moving average *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Judge revenue on a five-year moving average, not quarter on quarter. Only possible without outside investors.
- **Quote:** "for many years, I have been looking at a 5-year moving average to see if the business is trending up or not" (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S005).

## 12. The tax arbitrage *(his name)*
- **What it is:** Indian tax makes it cheaper to sell stake (LTCG about 21 percent unlisted, 12 percent listed) than to take profit as dividends (about 52 percent on PBT). This is why founders and VCs chase valuation over profit. Know the incentive before you copy the playbook.
- **Quote:** "A tax arbitrage definitely exists between trying to earn by chasing growth and selling a stake in the business over trying to earn profits and taking out dividends." (S008)
- **Sources:** 2 (S008, S012).

## 13. Angels before VCs, and undersell *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** If you must raise: build the MVP cheaply, take the minimum from angels in your industry on a capped note with a discount, pay yourself the bare minimum, and undersell the story so expectations stay reachable. Then choose slow or fast.
- **Quote:** "raise the minimum amount of money that you can right go to angels who want to invest in you because they are from your industry" (S013) "a good good uh good idea is to actually underell Right?" (S017)
- **Sources:** 3 (S013, S014, S017).

## 14. Is the user growth real? *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** In a $2k per capita country, freebies buy "users" cheaply. Check whether users generate real revenue. Users who add no value slow you down.
- **Quote:** "If anything, users who don't add any value to the business only slow the business down, especially in a risky and regulatorily complex environment like finance." (S009)
- **Sources:** 3 (S009, S012, S017).
