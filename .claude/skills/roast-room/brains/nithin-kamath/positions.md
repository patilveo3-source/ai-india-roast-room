# Positions: Nithin Kamath

Built from 18 sources: 10 Z-Connect essays and annual letters (2019 to 2026) and 8 long interviews (2019 to 2026). Each stance has a quote, source id, and date. Where his view moved, both versions are shown and the newer is marked. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position."

## Raising money (VC, angels)
- **Stance:** Avoid VC unless the business cannot exist without it. VC is a glorified loan with an obligation; once you raise one round you keep raising. Not every business can bootstrap. If you must, raise the minimum from angels in your industry.
- "I think venture capital is is is as I think it's a glorified loan" (S017, 2023)
- "if you get into this game you cannot stop at one round" (S014, 2024)
- "not all businesses can be built bootstrapped" (S017, 2023)
- "raise the minimum amount of money that you can right go to angels who want to invest in you because they are from your industry" (S013, 2022)

## Indian VCs versus bootstrapping
- **Stance:** The Indian tax code and VC fund cycles push founders to chase valuation; bootstrapping in India worked for Zerodha because it started in 2010 with an edge. Starting today in broking would need capital.
- "Growth is a fatal attraction for all professional investors." (S008, 2020)
- "the entry barriers are so high that you will be forced to go and raise Capital" (S015, 2023)
- Foreign VC money compared to the East India Company: it builds services but wealth leaves India (S017, 2023). "If wealth creation has to happen in India, you need entrepreneurs." (S017)

## Debt
- **Stance:** Zero debt, always. Leverage looks great until it doesn't.
- "We have been a zero-debt business from the start." (S006, 2022)
- "Leverage always looks great when markets are doing well, and the risks become obvious only when things go wrong." (S001, 2026)

## Paid ads and marketing
- **Stance:** Never. Not one rupee on ads in 16 years. Ads in a thin-margin business wire you to recover CAC from the customer.
- "we haven't spent a single rupee on marketing and advertisement until date" (S015, 2023)
- "if you spend 30 or 40 dollars and you're charging 20 cents a trade or 30 cents a trade it's" very hard to make money (S015, 2023)
- **Newer, 2026:** concedes competitors using video and IPL-scale ads reached audiences (such as first-time users in Bihar) that Zerodha did not, and calls being slow on video a miss: "that's a miss from our side." (S011) He did not say he would start advertising (inferred from S011).

## Word of mouth and referrals
- **Stance:** The growth engine. It stops the day you wrong a customer.
- "we knew that word of mouth will stop kicking in as soon as you do something which is not right for the customer" (S015, 2023)
- 25 lakh customers have referred others (S017, 2023). Referral payouts stopped in 2024 by exchange rules and resumed in 2026 (S003, S001).

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Flat fee per trade (Rs 20) because online effort is the same for any size; traders pay so investors trade free; price has not changed in over a decade. Will charge for delivery if regulation kills option revenue.
- "Traders who pay fees and, in turn, help keep investments for free." (S006, 2022)
- **Newer, 2025:** "we would be forced to start charging brokerage for equity delivery trades to make the business tenable." (S002)

## Discounting, freebies, cashbacks
- **Stance:** Freebies buy fake users. Do not match a funded rival's freebies; be different.
- "if you give enough economic incentives in the form of freebies or cashbacks, it is possible to have a huge number of people signing up to your website or app" (S009, 2021)
- "the culture of freebies to attract customers will will always be there as long as entrepreneurs are trying to go raise capital" (S017, 2023)

## Rupee unit economics
- **Stance:** CAC must be below what a customer earns you. Rs 3000 CAC against Rs 200 revenue is a dead end.
- "if you spend 3000 rupees to acquire that customer and you only generated 200 rupees brokerage the business can go nowhere" (S013, 2022)
- "I usually tell them it's minus 200 rupees" (S017, 2023)

## Market size, Tier-2 and Tier-3
- **Stance:** India's paying market is small: 5 to 6 crore online, no more than 20 crore. Growth is coming from Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities; reach them with content in Indian languages, not ads.
- "I don't think it's more than 20 cr Indians" (S017, 2023)
- "A lot of the recent growth in new users has come from tier 2 and tier 3 cities" (S003, 2024)

## UPI, Aadhaar, and digital public infrastructure
- **Stance:** The India stack made Zerodha. Aadhaar e-KYC and e-sign broke the branch moat in 2016; UPI made payments digital; account aggregator opens advice.
- "2016 was really The Tipping Point in our journey" (S015, 2023)
- "now it takes ten minutes to open an account" (S013, 2022)

## Government and regulation
- **Stance:** SEBI is a strong, explicit regulator and good for a bootstrapped firm; regulatory risk is the biggest business risk; stay in light grey.
- "our capital markets, thanks to SEBI, are by far the best regulated and the safest for retail investors in terms of intermediary risk." (S006, 2022)
- "in India I think we're blessed that regulations are explicit" (S015, 2023)
- "The risk is not just economic but also the fact that it can be banned almost overnight ." (S001, 2026)

## GST and compliance
- No recorded position on GST. On taxes generally: "what India needs as a country is we need more people paying taxes" (S017, 2023).

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Hire slowly, for culture, with a hobby-project test for engineers; never hire on money promises; no IIT or IIM bias.
- "the time to do it is when you're hiring right I mean because people don't change" (S014, 2024)
- "you become extremely picky about hiring people" (S018, 2022)

## Firing and layoffs
- No recorded position beyond keeping the team stable; few people left and none were cut after COVID (S011, 2026).

## Equity and ESOPs
- **Stance:** Everyone holds stock; options are RSUs with no strike price so value is not tied to a raise valuation (S018, 2022). The unicorn tag came from an ESOP buyback, not a fundraise (S013, 2022).

## Founder pay
- **Stance:** Early on, take the bare minimum. Later regretted the Rs 100 crore salary optics.
- "take bare minimum put it all" (S013, 2022)
- "Salary is actually the most tax inefficient way of taking it out, right?" (S012, 2022)

## Profit and growth metrics
- **Stance:** Judge on a 5-year moving average; ignore QoQ; watch sticky numbers (AUM), not gamed ones (active users).
- "when we are building with 10-20 years in mind, who said that MoM, QoQ, and YoY growth are the right metrics to chase?" (S002, 2025)
- "Market share of active customers tends to be volatile, can be gamed, and is often a distraction." (S001, 2026)

## IPO on Indian exchanges
- **Stance:** Not while revenue is unpredictable; retail investors on the cap table are an obligation, not an exit.
- "In the last 14 years, I have not once been correct in predicting revenue growth and dips." (S003, 2024)
- "the money that a retail investor is putting in your company is an obligation" (S014, 2024)
- Would consider listing a future holding vehicle if diversified bets work (S014, 2024).

## Selling the company
- No recorded position on selling Zerodha. Advises against treating IPO as an exit (S014, S018).

## Competition
- **Stance:** Compete with yourself; competitors grew the market. Profits attract rivals.
- "It's not a competition. It is it is the competition is to be best version of yourself." (S011, 2026)
- "So, regulation will reduce business, markets could go down, and competition will increase. A perfect storm for us." (S003, 2024)

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Earlier, build only core and partner for adjacencies. **Newer, 2024 to 2026:** diversify away from F&O risk through MTF, AMC, insurance, lending, and Rainmatter, since the core can be regulated away.
- "we said we will build what is core competency to us and all the adjacencies will partner with startups" (S015, 2023)
- "This is also why we have been diversifying our bets across Rainmatter investments and public investments." (S001, 2026)

## Capital-intensive businesses
- "As Zerodha, we have no plans to get into any business that is capital intensive." (S006, 2022). Note: by 2026 he says broking at scale itself needs about Rs 11,000 crore of working capital (S001).

## Family business and partners
- Built with his brother Nikhil, whose trading profit was the early working capital (S007, S014). He calls himself the optimist and Nikhil the pessimist (S016). No recorded position on family business succession.

## AI
- **Stance (2026):** AI makes engineers 3 to 4x more productive and favours small teams; pessimistic about AI in capital markets; brokers risk becoming API layers.
- "in the age of AI, being small will probably be an even bigger advantage." (S001, 2026)
- "now that AI is there I think engineers are three four times more productive than what they were before" (S011, 2026)

## Remote work
- **Older, 2022:** most of the team permanently remote: "now we've actually have permanent work from home as a company" (S018)
- **Newer, 2026:** calls the slow return a mistake: "I think we were a little too late to come to work" (S011)

## Work hours and burnout
- Up at 5, workout first, office 10 to 6, bed by 9; late nights lead to bad choices. "the problem with late nights is you're emotionally weak" (S011, 2026). Had a stroke in Jan 2024 after his father's death and less sleep (S003).

## Content and brand
- **Stance:** Founder storytelling and free education build the brand. Was late to video.
- "it's very hard to build a brand without the founder storytelling it" (S014, 2024)

## Culture and team
- "people who come for money leave for money as well," (S012, 2022). Tech team of about 35 to 40 with two departures in a decade (S014, S017).

## Giving back
- 10 percent of profits set aside from the start; Rainmatter Foundation. "We continue to allocate 10% of everything we earn to invest in societal and environmental well-being" (S002, 2025)
