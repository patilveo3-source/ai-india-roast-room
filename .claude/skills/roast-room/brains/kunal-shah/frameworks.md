# Frameworks: Kunal Shah

Built from 14 sources (2016 to 2026): long podcasts (Knowledge Project, Lenny's Podcast, Figuring Out, Think School, Return on India, South Park Commons, Paradigm Shift, Angel One, NPCI) and stage talks (INKtalks, Razorpay FTX25, Groww, Rainmatter, Mumbai Tech Week). Quick depth.

18 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources using the idea. How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated rules, not quotes.

---

## 1. Delta 4
- **What it is:** Score the old behavior and the new one for efficiency out of 10. If the gap is 4 or more, the new behavior is irreversible, users tolerate its failures, and they brag about it. Under 4, one bad experience sends people back.
- **Parts:** name the exact behavior being changed; score state A and state B; check the gap per cohort (FreeCharge was Delta 4 for youth, not 60-year-olds, S014); check affordability (S009); check early data by segment, since a Delta 4 felt by the first 500,000 users can vanish past a million (S009).
- **When to use:** any new product, feature, or startup idea; any plan that relies on discounts or cashback to switch users.
- **How to score:** 5 if a typical target user scores the gap at 4 or more with no incentive attached. 3 if the gap is 2 to 3 or real only for one cohort. 1 if the gap exists only while a discount or cashback lasts.
- **Quote:** "every time the delta of efficiency score is greater than equal to four three things happen uh it's an irreversible behavior" (S001) "Whenever the delta of efficiency is greater than four, that's where you will unlock the pot of gold." (S009)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S009, S013, S014).

## 2. UBP, unique bragworthy proposition
- **What it is:** People who reach an efficient state tell everyone still in the old one. That word of mouth is why Delta 4 products need little advertising and have low or zero CAC.
- **When to use:** a plan with a big paid acquisition budget; a product nobody shows to a friend.
- **How to score:** 5 if users already demo it to others unprompted. 1 if growth is all paid.
- **Quote:** "UBP stands for unique bragworthy proposition. Everybody who will experience a new efficient state will scream at everybody at an inefficient state and tell them to move there." (S009) "all Delta 4 products will naturally have lower CAC or sometimes zero CAC" (S002)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S009, S014).

## 3. Do not buy delta with discounts *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Money poured in to fake a delta erodes wealth. When the cashback ends, users go back.
- **When to use:** a launch plan built on 30 to 50 percent discounts, cashback, or subsidy.
- **How to score:** 1 if retention depends on the incentive; 5 if the incentive only speeds up a switch users would keep anyway.
- **Quote:** "The natural product does not have the delta, so I put more money to just artificially increase the delta, and therefore, you will see it will actually erode wealth." (S009) "as soon as the cash back is gone you reverse the old behavior" (S013)
- **Note:** He admits CRED itself was not Delta 4 and had to incentivize heavily (S014). See principles.
- **Sources:** 3 (S009, S013, S014).

## 4. Concentration of trust in low-trust societies
- **What it is:** Where courts and institutions do not protect consumers, trust stays hyperlocal (family, community) or concentrates in a few names (Tata, a few Bollywood stars). A trusted name can launch 25 things and people use them. That is why super apps and conglomerates work in Asia.
- **When to use:** a new or unknown brand entering India; deciding whether to cross-sell; choosing a face or partner for a launch.
- **How to score:** 5 if the product borrows trust from a known name, a trusted channel, or verified consistency. 1 if an unknown brand asks for money or data up front.
- **Quote:** "let me define low trust markets as where consumers are a lot more wary of trying new things because there are no institutions that protect you against bad behavior done by a company" (S002) "trust therefore becomes either hyper local or super concentrated" (S005)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S013, S011).

## 5. The four components of trust
- **What it is:** Trust needs integrity, consistency, competence, and benevolence (not self-centered). You do not need all four at full strength, but if one is visibly missing, there is no trust.
- **When to use:** reviewing a brand promise, an investor or partner, a hire, or any product that asks customers for money or data.
- **How to score:** 5 if all four are visible to the customer; 1 if any one is visibly absent (a first-time paraglider pilot lacks competence; a shady founder lacks integrity).
- **Quote:** "If visibly any of the four things are absent, you cannot trust something or someone." (S003)
- **Sources:** 1 (S003), applied in S004 and S013.

## 6. Build for the top 25 to 30 million (India 1A)
- **What it is:** India's spending, time value, and creditworthiness sit with about 25 to 30 million families. Build only for them, at high quality, then cross-sell. The mass market gives DAU, not ARPU. Building for an imaginary middle fails.
- **When to use:** choosing a target customer; pricing; deciding whether to chase hundreds of millions of users.
- **How to score:** 5 if the customer can pay and values time. 1 if the plan monetizes later from users with no spare income.
- **Quote:** "the value of time and per capita income is concentrated with 25 million families and therefore focusing on them is important" (S002) "70 80 of all discretionary spends in India is done by these 30 million people or their families" (S005)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S011, S014).

## 7. Reward the trustworthy (the CRED thesis)
- **What it is:** Society progresses when good behavior wins. Reward people who pay on time, give them an identity, then let banks, lenders, and brands reach them. Creditworthiness is a virtue, not just a score.
- **When to use:** a product that wants the best customers; a community or membership; any credit or lending product.
- **How to score:** 5 if the product selects for good behavior and rewards it. 1 if it attracts the riskiest users with the biggest incentive.
- **Quote:** "what if we created a world where all the people who demonstrate good behavior of paying their bills on time are rewarded or recognized." (S011) "we said that we will focus on more trustworthy individuals to increase the trust of the system" (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S005, S011, S014).

## 8. Status ladder: CFO and CMO of the brain
- **What it is:** Utility is judged by the brain's CFO, who keeps cutting cost, so utility margins go to zero. Status, or the hope of status, is judged by the CMO, who pays. Gross margin follows status.
- **When to use:** pricing; deciding whether a product is a utility or a status good; positioning.
- **How to score:** 5 if the product lets the buyer rise in status or shows it to others. 2 if it is pure utility competing on price.
- **Quote:** "by design gross margins exist when you allow human beings to jump their social status and gross margins disappear when you do not help them increase their social status" (S001) "there is usually a CFO in your brain and a CMO in your brain and the CFO is constantly dying to optimize costs" (S005)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S004, S005).

## 9. Save every penny, splurge on emotional events (Asian Maslow)
- **What it is:** Collectivist India buys to belong and to show status to the community. Indians save on daily things and open the floodgates on weddings, children's education, and health. Living-room goods carry 3x the margin of bedroom goods.
- **When to use:** any Indian consumer product; finding where Indians will pay.
- **Quote:** "weddings education of children and Health Care issues are the three big events where Indians have almost a Floodgate approach" (S005) "in a society we care more about showing to others so all the products which are showing demonstrating status were put in the living room" (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S005).

## 10. Value of time (the hourly wage gap)
- **What it is:** Indians were never paid by the hour, so they do not price their time. Products that sell convenience or time saved rarely monetize; Indian businesses do not pay for software; UPI took off only once it was free.
- **When to use:** a convenience, SaaS, or subscription product priced for India.
- **How to score:** 1 if the pitch is "saves you time" to a mass Indian buyer. 4 if the buyer is in the top 25 million or the payoff is money, status, or a big emotional event.
- **Quote:** "products that sell convenience and save you your time rarely monetize which monetize a lot in the western society" (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S005, S008, S012).

## 11. Rivers and dams
- **What it is:** Core motivations are rivers. Many Indian founders build fancy dams where no river flows, then make the dam fancier. Find the motivation first.
- **Quote:** "i see a lot of dam builders with no idea where rivers exist so i see them building dams in hope that river will show up" (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S007).

## 12. Real motivation versus stated motivation and the dinner test
- **What it is:** Founders must know the functional and the emotional reason people buy (headphones are status). Then: what does a user tell a friend at dinner, no jargon? If it cannot spread by talk, CAC will be huge.
- **Quote:** "if you cannot distill your idea to a transmissible conversation at dinner it's not going to spread and things that don't spread will have a huge cack" (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001); echoed in S004.

## 13. Utility free, monetize on top (super app as Marvel Universe)
- **What it is:** In India, give the utility free and earn on financial services, ads, or commerce sold to the same trusted users. Add products one hero at a time, not all at once.
- **Quote:** "it is okay and easier to get adoption through a free software versus trying to make money from SAS" (S005) "a lot of people think that super apps is like launching a Marvel movie where all the superheroes come in the first scene no they don't" (S005)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S005).

## 14. Profit pools show what a country values
- **What it is:** Study which companies make profit in India before copying a Western model. Few retailers are in India's top 1,000 profit makers; finance is huge.
- **Quote:** "profit pools of a country tell you a lot about what the country values" (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S005, S008).

## 15. Reputation bank account
- **Quote:** "reputation is a bank account where you can only deposit a dollar a day but every time the withdrawal is almost nearly complete" (S001) "trust is a slow currency. It doesn't happen naturally." (S013)
- **When to use:** any shortcut that risks customers' trust for growth.
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S013).

## 16. Friends and family test and the earn-trust value
- **What it is:** CRED interviews, appraises, and blocks launches on "earn trust": never ship what you would not give your own friends and family, even if it costs a week.
- **Quote:** "we have a simple principle at credit not launch any product or service that we would not offer to our friends and family." (S004)
- **Sources:** 1 (S004).

## 17. Revenue per employee and high-slope people
- **What it is:** Hire for slope (experiences per year), keep talent density; AI makes revenue per employee the metric that matters.
- **Quote:** "ultimately the metric that will matter to every company very soon is revenue per employee" (S007)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S007, S008, S012, S013).

## 18. Long-lived species: metabolism, conversion, adaptation
- **What it is:** Survivors can lower their burn at will, convert a high share of attempts, and adapt.
- **Quote:** "one who burns the least amount of calories to earn the most amount of calories" (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S008).
