# Positions: Kunal Shah

Built from 14 sources, 2016 to 2026 (S011 to S014 are undated uploads, roughly 2022 to 2025 by content). Quick depth. Dates are upload dates. Where there is no evidence, the topic says "no recorded position".

## Pricing
- Price on status, not utility. Utility margins go to zero; status and the hope of status keep margin. As a teenager he priced black henna at many times regular henna and created artificial scarcity. 2022 to 2025.
- "by design gross margins exist when you allow human beings to jump their social status and gross margins disappear when you do not help them increase their social status" (S001, 2022)
- "I would purposely price it multiple x more. uh than the regular Mahindi so that people feel that okay not only is it differentiated it's expensive so it must be great" (S004, 2025)
- Wasteful, non-utilitarian products hold higher status: "when you try to make the product more utilitarian, it loses status" (S002, 2024)

## Rupee pricing and MRP
- India's single MRP blocks charging rich and poor neighborhoods differently: "we enforce an artificial concept of mrp which is stopping the the core tenets of free market and capitalism to grow" (S001, 2022)
- AI removes price arbitrage: "the boundaries of the world which was Arbitrage based on pricing is gone and now it's all about who is more competent to do the work" (S012, about 2025)

## Discounting and cashback
- Against discounts as a growth engine; they fake a delta and erode wealth. 2016, repeated about 2025.
- "I will start offering 30% discount on shirts to increase the delta artificially." (S009, 2016)
- "as soon as the cash back is gone you reverse the old behavior" (S013)
- Inconsistency he admits: CRED was not Delta 4 and ran on rewards: "I was not able to generate the Delta for efficiency so I had to incentivize a lot more" (S014). FreeCharge rewards were merchant-funded coupons, not company cash (S014).

## Subscriptions and paying for convenience in India
- Indians do not price time, so convenience and SaaS rarely monetize. "no no business pays for software" (S001, 2022)
- "india is a country where you give them freebies and and you get all your dius you want you do not get your rpo" (S001, 2022). Netflix, Spotify, Prime subscriptions "has not panned out" (S002, 2024).

## Target customer and Tier-2 / Tier-3 markets
- Build for the top 25 to 30 million; the mass market cannot be monetized. FreeCharge's mistake was the cohort.
- "i told my investors that i'm building a startup that will only focus on the top 25 million customers of india because only they can value time or will buy" (S001, 2022)
- "the mistake we made at freecharge was we went up to the customer which were not very affluent" (S014)
- Tier-2 and Tier-3 specifically: no recorded position beyond this. He notes small-town wealth comes from resourceful, not insightful, people (S003, paraphrase).

## Trust and the Indian consumer
- India is low trust; trust concentrates in a few names; Indians mistrust Indian companies and trust global ones; trust is slow.
- "concentration of trust happens in low trust societies" (S003, 2025)
- "In India, we naturally mistrust uh uh Indian companies and naturally trust globally companies" (S013)
- Trust needs integrity, consistency, competence, benevolence (S003, 2025). "trust has a strong correlation to beauty." (S006, 2026)

## Focus versus diversification
- Focus is a curse in Asian markets; cross-sell to trusted customers, one product at a time. "focus is almost a curse in asian markets" (S001, 2022); "there is no depth in any market for you to focus and win big" (S005, 2023)

## Raising money from Indian VCs versus bootstrapping
- Capital accelerates growth at the cost of equity; raise only if the TAM is big. India now wrongly equates entrepreneurship with funding.
- "So now everybody thinks entrepreneurship is equal to funding." (S004, 2025)
- His own CRED Series A of 25 million dollars came on a thesis because he had an exit: "I would've not had that luxury if I had no success in the past" (S002, 2024)
- Too much capital hurts culture: "I think that is negatively impacted us." (S010, 2026)
- New startups need less capital: "potentially requires much lesser Capital than it did in the past" (S007, 2025)

## Profitability
- Indians judge on trading-style profit; internet companies build distribution first. "my own dad wouldn't respect me till I was building a profitable company" (S002, 2024). Default dead risk: "if you don't evolve fast enough we are default dead as well" (S007, 2025)

## Debt and credit
- Credit was taboo; India Stack makes underwriting possible; responsible credit is needed for prosperity; credit on UPI must start with utilities.
- "earlier credit was considered to be a little bit of a taboo culturally" (S005, 2023)
- "digital payment started to kicking off before UPI only on utility not on shopping." (S011)

## UPI and digital public infrastructure
- Strongly positive. UPI won because it was free and reliable; DPI makes India the most promising market.
- "it took off after government made it free money transfer is instant but nobody makes any money" (S001, 2022)
- "when a stack comes in is a tectonic shift to the society" (S005, 2023)
- "We have all sorts of digital public infrastructure, government support" (S002, 2024)

## GST and compliance
- Only as data: "we have uh now data on GST which can be accessed to an API" (S005, 2023). On GST rates or compliance burden: no recorded position.

## Government and regulation
- Wants stable, predictable regulation; warns that much of India's profit pool is protected by license, so AI disruption there is slower; tells people to stop waiting for schemes.
- "a reliable regulatory environment taxation environment to drive some of these predictable outcomes for investors" (S005, 2023)
- "what percentage of India's public market profit pools are protected by some regulation, some license, or public government companies. The answer is a very large chunk of it." (S008, 2026)
- "We are expecting some government scheme to come up or some program to come up" (S008, 2026)
- Avoids political opinions: "Fastest way to be called stupid in life is to have an opinion about power or politics." (S003, 2025)

## Family business
- Grew up in a Gujarati trading family; learned unit economics as small talk; communities lend to their own at low rates.
- "Most of our businesses, most of our family businesses were trading." (S002, 2024)
- Marwari families lend to family members at 6 to 8 percent (S006, 2026).

## Hiring in India
- Hire from your network early, for character; hire ex-founders; hire for your weaknesses; no designations; AI skill mandatory.
- "I would say it has to be mostly people you know or people you know referring people they know." (S010, 2026)
- "We love hiring a lot of expounders because they're usually very self-aware on what do they suck at." (S004, 2025)
- "hiring people who can't use AI well is almost a liability" (S013)

## Firing and retention
- Interviews are imperfect; three months tells you. Share wealth so people feel like shareholders. Early crisis sorts the team.
- "if you work with somebody for 3 months, you'll know if they're good or not." (S004, 2025)
- "you should be very thankful if you get crisis early in your company." (S010, 2026)

## Work hours
- Questions a 5-day week for a poor nation but runs CRED on 5 days. "how can a poor nation afford to work 5 days a week?" (S008, 2026)

## Brand and ads
- Delta 4 products need little advertising. CRED ads made without an agency, never spoon-feed, aimed at 8 to 25 year olds who drive family tech choices. Bet on IPL ads in peak COVID against board advice.
- "Delta 4 products don't need advertising as much" (S014)
- "smart people do not want to be spoonfed So our ads never spoonfed." (S011)
- "we always optimize for the 8 to 25 year old loving our ads" (S006, 2026)

## Content and social media
- Makes public conjectures to be corrected; Monday Motivation as anti-motivation. Twitter is built for outrage. "the biggest profit making scheme that I've seen in recent times is to tell people to love themselves and they'll pay you money for saying that" (S002, 2024)

## AI
- AI is electricity, not internet; every employee must be AI-first; 90 percent of CRED code is AI-written (2026); use global models, do not rush a national LLM; protect consumers from AI scams.
- "AI is a lot more like electricity than internet" (S007, 2025)
- "let's say 90% of code at Cred is written by AI in Cred now" (S008, 2026)
- "it's going to be insanely easy to manipulate humans" (S007, 2025)

## Metrics
- Revenue per employee; flat headcount while business grows. "our business has gone multifold but our head count has remained flat for the last two and a half three years" (S012)

## Competition and copying
- Clones split the pot of gold: "When 10 people come for that pot of gold, the pot of gold disappears" (S009, 2016)

## Women and the market
- Low female labor participation caps India's per capita income and keeps 92 to 95 percent of financial products bought by men (S005, 2023; S008, 2026).
