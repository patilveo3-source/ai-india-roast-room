# Frameworks: Falguni Nayar

Built from 17 sources (2017 to 2026): long interviews and podcasts, IIMA, Praxis and ASCENT talks, the pre-IPO analyst call, the Q1 FY27 results call, and four short press pieces. Quick depth. She names few frameworks, so most are *(unnamed)* patterns read across sources. Most-used first. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance built from her stated rules, not quotes. Transcripts are auto captions, so quotes keep caption spellings (Nika for Nykaa).

---

## 1. Inventory where trust matters, marketplace where risk is high *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Beauty sits close to health, so authenticity and freshness are the product. She took inventory in beauty to guarantee genuine, fresh stock, and chose a marketplace for fashion, where styles change and SKUs explode.
- **Parts:** Is fake or expired product a real fear in this category? If yes, own the stock, run first-expiry-first-out, keep a short inventory cycle (40 days in 2017, S016). If SKUs and style risk are huge, go marketplace or inventory-light. Check the rules: inventory-led needed Indian-owned capital (S002).
- **When to use:** Any D2C or retail call on stocking, marketplace versus own stock, new category entry.
- **How to score:** 5 if the model matches the category's trust risk and the founder can fund the stock without strain. 3 if inventory is taken for margin only. 1 if a trust-sensitive category is run on an unchecked marketplace, or a fast-fashion style category is stocked heavy.
- **Quote:** "in fashion taking inventory risk is a bit too much so we do have some inventory but a lot of it is through our partnership with the brands" (S001) "in beauty we were of the opinion that it needs to be inventoryled business and if you wanted to do inventory-led uh then you couldn't take foreign money" (S002)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S004, S011, S012, S013, S016).

## 2. Authenticity brings trust *(unnamed; the Nykaa value system)*
- **What it is:** A young company cannot look like an old banyan tree, so it earns trust by being exactly what it says: genuine stock, honest discount claims, no gap between talk and action.
- **Parts:** Guarantee genuine product; make offer claims literally true (if "up to 30% off", at least 30% of the range is 30% off); say what you mean; let word of mouth do the marketing.
- **When to use:** Brand building, offer design, any claim to customers.
- **How to score:** 5 if every customer-facing claim is literally true and stock is verified genuine. 1 if the plan leans on inflated MRP, bait discounts, or unclear sourcing.
- **Quote:** "Nika believed in authenticity and authenticity brought us trust" (S001) "we just got viral ity out of being authentic" (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S004, S005, S009, S013).

## 3. No discount from your own pocket *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Discounts are brand-funded, planned in joint business plans, and never the way to buy customers. A customer bought with discount is the wrong customer.
- **Parts:** Pass on brand-funded offers only; fund only loyalty points; acquire through education, content and engagement; protect brand value so premium and luxury brands will list with you.
- **When to use:** Pricing, launch offers, sale events, customer acquisition plans.
- **How to score:** 5 if growth does not depend on self-funded discounts. 3 if discounts are occasional and brand-funded. 1 if the plan is to win share with deep discounts paid by the company.
- **Quote:** "a customer that comes with discount is not the right customer" (S002) "we do not believe that we should discount brands from our pocket because that is something that brands also don't appreciate they lose value" (S013)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S006, S010, S011, S013, S016, plus the Uday Kotak voice in S002).

## 4. Capture the larger value chain *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Margin comes from doing the heavy lifting yourself: inventory, warehouses, supply chain, and organic marketing. Hand each piece to a third party and margins shrink.
- **Parts:** Take inventory risk for better margin; run your own warehousing and supply chain; keep paid marketing low and avoid giving 10% to affiliates. Benchmark: her cost of doing business was 32% of revenue in 2018 against roughly 50% for typical online players (Biyani's figure, S011).
- **When to use:** Unit economics, outsourcing, channel choice.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder owns the costly, trust-critical steps and the cost of doing business is well under gross margin. 1 if every step is outsourced and margin depends on a future scale miracle.
- **Quote:** "if you're giving away components to different players and not capturing full value chain then obviously your margins Creek keep shrinking" (S011) "I always felt that we need to do a lot of heavy lifting on behalf of our consumers." (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S011, S016).

## 5. Unit economics, ROCE, and a growth rate you can afford *(unnamed; "basic principles of finance")*
- **What it is:** Her banker discipline: keep capital low, make unit economics work, earn a return on capital employed, and grow only as fast as you can fund.
- **Parts:** Current unit economics and how they progress; will it ever make money; capital employed versus return; new-customer share balanced against profit (she pledged about 30% new customers in 2018, S011); lifetime value over revenue.
- **When to use:** Any growth plan, fundraising, expansion.
- **How to score:** 5 if unit economics are positive now or have a dated path and ROCE rises. 3 if positive only at an assumed scale. 1 if GMV is bought at a loss with no accountability.
- **Quote:** "we all learned that return on Capital employed matters" (S009) "it's just a discipline to maintain a growth rate that you can afford" (S006) "a tight capital execution leading to higher return on capital employed" (S014)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S005, S006, S009, S010, S011, S014, S018).

## 6. Art of retailing: content in the context of commerce
- **What it is:** Beauty needs education. The retailer plays beauty adviser at scale through content, so customers visit far more often than they buy. Content must stay tied to the purchase.
- **Parts:** Hire content early (one of her first three hires was a chief content officer); advise, do not push; measure visits against purchases; keep content about the category, not generic entertainment.
- **When to use:** Marketing plans, new categories that need explaining, CAC problems.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan educates customers into the category and ties content to purchase. 1 if it relies on paid ads alone in a category customers do not understand.
- **Quote:** "the art of retailing, which is to intertwine advice with sales, not just push sales" (S016) "our customers visit us 50 times a year when they buy only four times a year" (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S007, S010, S013, S016).

## 7. Right shade at full price *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Sell the right product at full price rather than the wrong one cheap. Consumer pull, not brand push, and never a clearance platform.
- **When to use:** Assortment, recommendations, sale strategy.
- **How to score:** 5 if the offer helps the customer pick right and repeat. 1 if it moves stock the customer will regret.
- **Quote:** "it's important to send the sell the right color of the lipstick to the customer at full price then sell the wrong color of the lipstick to consumers uh at a at a low price" (S010)
- **Sources:** 2 (S010, S016).

## 8. Sustainable brand strength test *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A D2C brand lasts only if customers come back, it meets a real need, and it has product others cannot simply source.
- **Parts:** Repeat cohort; real customer need; unique innovation; a point of view per brand. For own brands, start from a gap you see, never a copy of a bestseller.
- **When to use:** Launching or backing a D2C brand or a private label.
- **How to score:** 5 if repeat data and a unique product exist. 3 if repeat is unknown. 1 if trial is bought and the product is generic.
- **Quote:** "if this customers don't come back then eventually that music will stop" (S010) "the starting point very often for a retailer is a gap that they identify and they want to service it well" (S002)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S007, S009, S010).

## 9. Sunrise category, compounding, decades *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A founder without deep pockets should pick an early, underpenetrated category, grow big before conglomerates arrive, and stay for 10 to 20 years.
- **Parts:** Size the category (she checked L'Oreal and Lakme India turnover, S005); compare per capita spend with the US and Asia (India about $16 vs US $300, S001); check the US ratio between categories (fashion five times beauty, S002); commit for decades.
- **When to use:** Choosing a category or market entry.
- **How to score:** 5 if the category is early, large in mature markets, and the founder will stay 10+ years. 1 if crowded and the founder wants a quick exit.
- **Quote:** "early days of industry allows you freedom to build a solution in a very capital efficient manner" (S001) "I believe in power of compounding" (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S005, S007, S012, S015).

## 10. Consumer-first omnichannel *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Stores where the consumer needs touch and advice (shade, smell, foundation); e-commerce for endless aisle and reach. Stores are capital-inefficient but build trust and convert well.
- **Parts:** Go where consumers are, even expensive malls; treat part of store cost as brand building; a store must hit revenue so costs fall in place.
- **When to use:** Opening stores, offline expansion, Tier 2 and 3 reach.
- **How to score:** 5 if offline serves a clear trial need with a revenue model. 1 if stores are opened for vanity with no unit model.
- **Quote:** "what we call it is consumer led consumer first approach where you give them physical retail to the extent they need" (S010) "it is clearly more capital inefficient compared to e-commerce" (S011)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S007, S010, S011, S013, S014).

## 11. Magic of the multibrand retailer
- **What it is:** Under one roof the customer feels safe choosing the best, buys two or three when she came for one, and converts higher than at single-brand stores.
- **Quote:** "customer comes looking for one but they may buy two or three" (S002) "single Brands always push too many of their products whereas in a multibrand environment customer feels safe that they're choosing from what best is out there" (S007)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S007, S016).

## 12. Plan becomes budget; govern by data
- **What it is:** What is promised to investors becomes the budget. Detailed annual operating plans; daily to quarterly KPI reports; leaders who know their numbers.
- **Quote:** "whatever we put in our business plan to investors we actually convert it to our budgets and work towards it and achieve it" (S007) "my issue normally arises when someone who works for me doesn't know the numbers" (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S004, S007, S009).

## 13. Invest ahead of the curve, within discipline *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** She unlearned banker conservatism: when growth runs 50 to 100% a year, warehouses, inventory and spend must come ahead of demand. She balances this with profit, never with losses she cannot sustain.
- **Quote:** "in a technology-led world your assumption can limit you" (S008) "We would be wrong to give up on long-term growth for short-term profitability" (S018)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S008, S018, S011).

## 14. Expand by vertical integration, not diversification
- **What it is:** Extend to adjacent categories with the same customer (beauty to fashion to wellness, eB2B in beauty), never for the sake of a channel trend.
- **Quote:** "it's more of a vertical integration than diversification" (S010) "we will not get into additional categories for the sake of quick commerce" (S017)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S010, S017).
