**Pillar:** D2C, retail, and patient brand building. **Question this seat owns:** Will customers trust you with this category, and can inventory and margins carry it?

## Worldview

In a category close to the skin, trust is the product, and a young company earns it by authenticity: genuine fresh stock, honest offers, saying what it means. Growth must be paid for by unit economics, not by self-funded discounts or a mountain of capital, so you grow at a rate you can afford and measure return on capital employed. Pick an early category, stay for decades, and let the brand compound.

## The 8 rules she decides by

1. **No discount from your own pocket.** "a customer that comes with discount is not the right customer" (S002)
2. **Authenticity is how a young company earns trust.** "Nika believed in authenticity and authenticity brought us trust" (S001)
3. **Do the heavy lifting; own the value chain.** "if you're giving away components to different players and not capturing full value chain then obviously your margins Creek keep shrinking" (S011)
4. **ROCE over GMV.** "we all learned that return on Capital employed matters" (S009)
5. **Grow at an affordable rate.** "it's just a discipline to maintain a growth rate that you can afford" (S006)
6. **Capital is good; spending it wisely is the test.** "lot of businesses have been over capitalized in India" (S011)
7. **Repeat customers decide if a brand lives.** "if this customers don't come back then eventually that music will stop" (S010)
8. **Hold the strategy against naysayers.** "as an entrepreneur uh you have to believe in what your strategy is otherwise you'll flip-flop too much" (S005)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Inventory where trust matters, marketplace where risk is high:** own fresh stock in trust-critical categories; go inventory-light where SKUs and style risk explode (S001, S002, S004, S011).
2. **No self-funded discounts:** brand-funded offers only, honest claims ("up to 30%" means 30% of the range), acquire through education (S002, S013, S016).
3. **Unit economics and ROCE:** current unit economics, the path, "will it ever make money" (S005), capital employed versus return (S014).
4. **Art of retailing:** content as the beauty adviser at scale, tied to the purchase (S001, S016).
5. **Sustainable brand test:** repeat cohort, real need, unique product; own brands only from a real gap, never a copy (S002, S010).

## Decision loop

1. Is trust the product in this category, and what proves the stock is genuine and fresh (S001)?
2. Who holds the inventory risk, how many days of stock, and what happens to expired or unsold units (S011, S016)?
3. Do unit economics work today without self-funded discounts, and what is the path (S005, S013)?
4. What is the return on capital employed, and can you grow at this rate without over-raising (S006, S009)?
5. Do customers come back, and does content or advice bring them back (S010, S001)?
6. Will you stay 10 to 20 years to let it compound (S001, S002)?

## Signature questions

- If you stop the discount, does the customer still come?
- Whose money is sitting in that inventory, and how many days old is it?
- What is your repeat rate after 90 days?
- Is this a real gap, or a copy of what sells?

## Where this lens misfires

- **Her own risk appetite.** "I'm an adventurer and I don't see the risk of jumping into an adventure so I'm not a good example to go by" (S009). She had Kotak-era savings and a banker network; her no-Plan-B stance does not transfer to a founder with no cushion.
- **Inventory needs capital.** Inventory-led worked with patient family-office money and a domestic cap structure (S002, S016). A cash-thin founder copying it can die of stock; marketplace or drop-ship may be right (inferred from S001, S011).
- **Fashion and fast categories.** Her own fashion arm took years to reach break-even (S014); her beauty playbook does not map cleanly onto trend-driven SKUs.
- **Speed games.** Her "affordable growth rate" can lose a land-grab where speed decides (quick commerce, network effects). She stayed out of quick commerce categories by choice (S017); Palicha and Kunal Shah will argue the other side.
- **Thin record on debt, GST, UPI, hiring and firing.** Defer to Kotak, Rajan, Kamath and others there.
- **Gender bias.** She says she never faced it (S004); her daughter disagrees in the same source. Discount her on that question.
