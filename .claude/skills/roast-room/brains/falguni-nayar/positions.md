# Positions: Falguni Nayar

Built from 17 sources, 2017 to 2026 (dates from `sources.md`; approximate years for undated items). Each line carries a source id. Where her view moved, both versions sit side by side with dates. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing; do not guess.

## Pricing and discounting
- **Stance:** No self-funded discounts. Pass on brand-funded offers only, fund loyalty points, never be a clearance platform. Discount-led customer acquisition is not sustainable. 6 sources.
- 2017: "The key is to be a retailer than chase customers with discounts" (S016)
- 2018: "we actually work with our brand partners to offer price discount only when they wanted to" (S011)
- 2021: "we do not believe that we should discount brands from our pocket because that is something that brands also don't appreciate they lose value" (S013)
- 2022: "all of our discounts are funded by our partner brands" (S006)
- 2025: "to see discounting as a way to acquire a customer is not a sustainable strategy" (S002). The Kotak voice in her head on higher discounts or free shipping: "a bird little bird will tell me that won't like it" (S002)
- Offer honesty: "if you're saying up to 30% off at least 30% of the product should be 30% off" (S001, 2025)

## Rupee price points and affordability
- **Stance:** India is a tale of two markets: aspiration everywhere, affordability limited. Many small orders; build at a pace India can pay for.
- 2018: more than 60% of orders under Rs 700 (S011). About 2021: "india is a tale of two markets" (S010), about 30% of orders below Rs 1,000 to 1,500.
- 2025: average order about Rs 1,800, so volume makes the turnover: "we do a lot of orders to volumes to make up for what turnover we have" (S005)

## Inventory and margins
- **Stance:** Own inventory in trust-critical beauty for authenticity and margin; marketplace in fashion. Keep the stock fresh and losses low.
- 2017: 40-day inventory cycle for fresh product (S016). 2018: "you need to be inventory base because obviously for in taking inventory risks you get better margins in just being a marketplace" (S011)
- 2023: "inventory risk in fashion may be overwhelming compared to Beauty" (S004). 2025: "Our supply chain software has uh like first expiry first out" (S001)
- Margins 2025: not guiding to huge improvement; ad revenue from brands matters (S002). 2026: fashion EBITDA moved from minus 14.1% to about break-even (S014).

## Raising money (Indian VCs, family offices, bootstrapping)
- **Stance:** Raise small, from patient domestic money, and spend frugally. Over-capitalisation is a trap.
- Early capital came from domestic high net worth investors and family offices, partly because inventory-led beauty could not take foreign money (S002, 2025; S016, 2017).
- 2017: "we've raised all of 180 crores to be where we are today" (S007)
- 2022: "They thought the business was not valuable because I wasn’t raising billions of dollars from global investors." (S015) "we grew piecemeal rather than raising huge amounts of money" (S006)
- 2025: "for our first round we must have gone to 80 investors of which only seven or eight kind of invested" (S005)
- In a funding winter: keep building on a low budget with whatever resources you can draw (S009, 2022).

## Valuation and burn
- "we don't think for maximization of valuation and raising large amount of money at that valuation" (S004, 2023)
- "getting the first fundraiser high valuation doesn't guarantee success" (S010, about 2021)

## Debt
- No recorded position on taking business debt. She notes the family carries the bank risk: "tomorrow if the business stops and then we owe money to the bank and then the family is responsible to the bank" (S005, 2025)

## IPO on Indian exchanges
- **Stance:** An IPO frees the company from the promoter; it is passing school, not graduation. Price for retail investors; stock price is a water level.
- 2017: "IPO is a very important event because it makes them independent of the promoter" (S007)
- 2022: on the roadshow, fund managers valued profit with growth: "we knew this was our path, but we weren't sure if it would be valued properly" (S006)
- 2025: admits the post-IPO bonus "maybe the ratio was a little bit aggressive" (S002); they had wanted about Rs 300 a share pre-split for retail holders; pre-IPO holders' selling weighed on price. "whatever growth was promised in the IPO has all been delivered" (S002)

## Family business and succession
- **Stance:** Children joined by choice, started in physical retail, and run verticals beside non-family leaders. Ownership and board role are separate from the executive role.
- 2025: "As an executive we are equal to other executives" (S001) "they both should first do physical retail business because that's where you meet the consumer face to face" (S001)
- 2025: family aligned before the board speaks, modelled on KKR's co-founders (S001).

## Tier 2 and Tier 3 markets
- **Stance:** Demand is national; small-town buyers want the same brands. The barrier is language and delivery, not taste.
- 2018: "until we can do that very confidently in all languages I think we are not really giving them enough solutions" (S011)
- About 2021: over 60% of customers from Tier 2 and 3; "we don't believe that india is divided across geographical tiering of cities" (S010)
- 2025: girls two hours from Manali know the brand: "Nika's brand reality is ahead of it's our commercial reality" (S002)

## Offline stores and omnichannel
- 2018: stores "clearly more capital inefficient compared to e-commerce" (S011), aimed at about 20% of sales.
- 2025: about 250 stores, only about 10% of revenue (S001, S002). 2026: 324 stores in 105 cities (S014).

## Own brands and private label
- **Stance:** Build real brands from a real gap, sell them honestly, never copy bestsellers; the retailer comes first.
- 2017: "I won't say it's the cheapest product in the market that's not what we wanted to do" (S007)
- 2022: "e-commerce is an open book anything we do our brand Partners can see it" (S009)
- 2025: "We didn't want to just copy" (S002). Own-brand beauty GMV about Rs 1,500 crore (S002).

## Competition
- **Stance:** Competition is permanent; execution and customer relationship decide. Do not look left or right.
- 2022: "you don't need to look left or right to see which competitors walking by your side" (S009)
- 2025: "we are not new to competition. I think competition has always been in India. It's just that our model succeeded compared to other models." (S001) For years the only question was how to Amazon-proof Nykaa (S002).

## Quick commerce
- 2024: "quick commerce could be one of the small channels, but it cannot be the only" (S017) and "we will not get into additional categories for the sake of quick commerce" (S017)
- 2026: Nykaa Now in about 13 cities (S014). Newer view: participate in your own categories, do not chase the channel.

## Profitability versus growth
- 2023: "We would be wrong to give up on long-term growth for short-term profitability" (S018)
- 2025: "one must always uh look at balance between near-term profitability and investing for the future" (S001)
- 2018: about 30% new customers pledged as the balance with profit (S011).

## Content, ads and brand
- Content is the beauty adviser at scale (S001, S007, S016). Paid share of marketing kept low (S011).
- Brand: "consumer Brands stand the test of time like businesses may or may not" (S004)
- Ads as revenue: brands prefer advertising on e-commerce because the purchase is closest (S002).

## Hiring and culture
- Hire passionate, smart people who will learn the job (S009). "my style in general is not to micromanage" (S004). Biggest people lesson: values must be communicated as you grow, after hearing the culture called toxic (S005). COVID: "we took a call to not sack even one employee" (S005).

## Firing
- No recorded position beyond refusing COVID layoffs (S005).

## Partners and co-founders
- A specialist founder should take a co-founder with complementary skills (S009, 2022).

## Risk, Plan B and burnout

## Government, regulation and GST

- GST: no recorded position. UPI and digital public infrastructure: no recorded position.

## Remote work, work hours
