# Positions: Sridhar Vembu

Built from the extraction notes on 21 of 23 sources (2010 to 2026). Each topic has his stance, a verbatim quote with source id, and a date. Where his situation changed, the newer view is marked. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position".

## Raising money from VCs vs bootstrapping
- Avoided VC from the start because VC forces an exit, by sale or IPO, and both end independence (2023). "I felt that if we took venture capital [funds], we would have to provide an exit eventually." (S024)
- Not dogmatic: money is fine when needed; ask what it buys. "definitely when you need money you need money but where is the money going" (S001, 2024)
- It is a timeline choice, not a market-timing choice (2022). "you accept the limitations of not taking money slower growth all that maybe you should go for it" (S003)
- Indian VC record (2025): 30 years, no major global player, because India copied Silicon Valley. "We have not produced a single major global player yet in 30 years." (S016)

## Valuation and unicorns
- Valuations are not meaningful; valuation obsession distorts decisions and ends in layoffs (2023). "This severely distorts your decision-making [since] you’re not running the company to benefit customers or even employees." (S024)

## IPO on Indian exchanges
- 2010: "As long as I have anything I can say about it, we are not going to go public." (S022)
- 2022: "not as long as i am in charge of the company so no plans" (S003)
- 2023: "No, I prefer to stay private because even public companies are subject to quarter to quarter pressure." (S024)
- **Newer, 2025** (now chief scientist, not CEO): not his decision, but he is glad to be private. "I'm very glad I don't have to worry about stock prices or the index." (S004) No recorded view specific to NSE or BSE listings versus foreign listings.

## Selling the company
- 1999: turned down a 25 million dollar offer; the team agreed never to sell. "shall we make it a forever we won't sell" (S001, 2024 telling)
- Exit means death; he will work as long as he can (2025). (S007)

## Debt
- Zoho has no debt (2024). "completely bootstrapped, still private, no outside money, no debt" (S005)
- Against debt for young people; Zoho Schools pays a stipend (2024). "societies that trap their young people in debt are doing a massive injustice to their own young people" (S005)
- Dollar loans are risky when the Fed tightens (2022, inferred from S003).

## Pricing in rupees and discounting
- Price below the incumbent with a better product (2010): Zoho CRM at 12 dollars a user vs Salesforce at about 65. "I could create a better product and sell it at a lower price." (S022)
- Never charge more than the value delivered; leave money on the table (2024). "We never take money without delivering at least an equal amount of value, preferably more, to them than we take from them." (S005)
- But know your value: an early customer said he would have paid ten times more. "Make sure you charge the next customer a better price, he told me." (S010, 2025)
- Will raise prices when costs force it (2022). "we had to raise prices because of that" (S020)
- No big AI surcharges (2025, 2026). "We don't like massive price increases." (S004)
- Gross margin on every sale; worked example in rupees: buy at 40, sell at 50 (2021). "are you earning a profit on every sale" (S021)
- Discount campaigns, freemium mechanics for Indian SMBs, coupon wars: no recorded position beyond the above. Zoho runs a free tier subsidized by paying users (S022, 2010).

## GST and compliance
- Early approvals and contradictory rules were hard; get a good chartered accountant (2025). "compliance with various laws and regulations sometimes contradictory right" (S002)
- Accounting and GST filing software is essential for every business. "GST, all of that, right? essential." (S002) No recorded position on GST rates or reform.

## Hiring in India
- Talent creation over acquisition; ignore degrees; train for months; pay while training (2023 to 2026). "we are a talent creation business, we are a talent nurturing business, not merely a talent acquisition business" (S024)
- Rural freshers learn fast. "in every place in India there's talent like this. Everywhere." (S009, 2026)
- First sales hire came after he learned he could not sell; that hire tripled sales (S010, S022).
- AI in interviews: against. "I'm extremely uncomfortable with this whole AI interviewing people idea." (S005, 2024)
- Moonlighting: use common sense (2023). "Our policy in Zoho is to use your common sense." (S024)

## Firing and layoffs
- Avoid layoffs; laid off 10 to 12 salespeople after 2001 and decided never again (S001). Promised no layoffs in the 2020 crash (S020). "If we expect people to be an extended family, how can we just simply, you know, randomly lay off people" (S010, 2025)
- Many tech layoffs blamed on AI are really cost pressure (2025, 2026). "The layoffs are often related to rising cost pressures. We experience those pressures too" (S012)
- Firing individuals for performance: no recorded position.

## Equity, ESOPs, and pay
- Zoho has no ESOPs; employees share through bonuses (2022). "as we make money the employees share in the prosperity of the company" (S003)
- ESOPs in funded startups often cannot be sold at inflated prices because of lock-ins (2023, S024).

## Partners and co-founders
- Trust comes before business (2010). "before there is business, there has to be trust" (S022)
- Fights over strategy, never money (2024, S001). Co-founders and brothers left in 2003 to start their own companies (S022).

## Family business
- Zoho was built with siblings (Kumar, Radha, Shekhar, Mani) and Tony Thomas; differences were about strategy, not money (S001, S005). Brother Kumar's company was later bought by Zoho (S001).
- Children: shield them from wealth; they must work for a living (2022, S003). No recorded position on succession inside promoter families beyond "somebody who already works here" (S001).

## Tier-2, Tier-3, and rural markets
- Go where talent is born; small towns and villages, with a leader on the ground (2023 to 2026). "you drop a pin on the map in a random part of India, draw a 20 30 km radius, you're going to find about 3 to 5 lakh people easily" (S011)
- Build products for rural Bharat by understanding how rural people think (2020). "Connect back to your roots and that will allow you to build the right products for India." (S025)
- India is Zoho's fastest-growing market, about 10 percent of revenue (2025, S011).

## UPI and digital public infrastructure
- Proof India can do what the US called impossible (2026). "every adult Indian is using UPI once a day. At least." (S009)
- Defends UPI's tiny fee against big tech's rising charges (2026). "While we fight about UPI imposing a very tiny fee" (S018)

## Government and regulation
- Government cannot build products; the private sector must invest in R&D (2020). "The government alone can’t build products. The corporate sector can invest in long-term R&D." (S025)
- Government should encourage dispersal to rural areas (2022, S003); IP created in India should be registered and taxed in India (2025, S004).
- Selling to government: earned through audits and a competitive bake-off; government revenue is small for Zoho (2025). "Our percentage of government revenue is actually small." (S011)

## Competing with giants and foreign players
- Giants do not kill you; companies kill themselves (2010). "So companies kill themselves." (S022)
- Win by tough evaluation against Microsoft and others (2024, S001); integrated suite beats single-point "category leaders" (S001).

## Focus vs diversification
- Diversify products and geographies after the 2001 bust (S001, S020). But in a crunch, narrow focus: "this is not the time to expand our focus right now" (S011, 2025)

## Marketing, ads, and brand
- Word of mouth from a great product; search ads worked for ManageEngine; banner ads did not drive signups (2010, S022).
- Against heavy sales and marketing spend relative to R&D (2024). "they'll spend 70% sometimes 80% in sales and marketing they'll spend like 10% in R" (S001)
- Admits critics say Zoho markets poorly (S001). No ads inside work apps (S022).

## Content and personal brand
- No recorded position on founder content strategy. He is very active on X but ignores praise and criticism (2025, S008).

## AI
- Use small, cheap, task-tuned models and edge AI; AI must not become a tax on customers (2025, 2026). "if we have to do this a billion times a day, it better be damn cheap" (S009)
- AI coding augments good programmers; not safe yet for production code (2025). "I wouldn't say it replaces a really good programmer. It augments them." (S004)
- AI bubble: most funded AI companies may not survive (S001, 2024); software priced for scarcity may crash like mangoes (S019, 2025).

## Remote work
- 2022: default to office, hybrid case by case; new hires need face-to-face to form bonds. "how do you create that culture and bonds in the first place" (S003)

## Hours, burnout, and stress
- Enough youth will work 70 hours a week (2024, S001). But the CEO job and staff stress must not be permanent (2024). "I want my job to be as stress-free as possible" (S005)
- Burnout comes from the split between extracting at work and serving on weekends (S005).

## Culture and metrics
- No chief culture officer; culture spreads by example (S023, S005). Against metricizing people. "metrics are for widgets, for software, for factories" (S005)

## Timing and downturns
- "it's always raining somewhere so you just have to catch the rain" (S003, 2022)
- Warned staff of a downturn in 2022 and kept months of cushion (S003, S020).

## Data centers and infrastructure
- Owns 18 data centers; 50 to 70 percent cheaper than public cloud (2026, S009). Cautious about foreign AI data centers in India because of power prices (2026, S012).

## Side projects
- No recorded position for founders. For employees: common sense on moonlighting (S024).
