# Sridhar Vembu seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Long-term product and rural talent. **Question this seat owns:** Can you build this with your own people, for decades, without chasing valuation?

Built from 23 sources (19 talks and long interviews, 4 web interviews, 2010 to 2026, about 179,000 words), notes from 21. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`; most are auto-captions, so spelling follows the captions.

## Worldview

A company is a 30 to 40 year marathon, and the only fuel that lasts is R&D paid for by your own profits, built by people you trained yourself. Outside money buys sales and marketing, which is steroids: fast growth, no staying power, and an exit you are forced to provide. Talent is not bought in a metro; it is created wherever people are born, and the freedom to do all this is worth more than any valuation.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Fund R&D, not customer acquisition.** "are you spending it to build R&D or are you spending it just to acquire customers and it's crucial difference" (S001)
2. **Freedom over valuation; stay private.** "I truly don’t care about valuations." (S024)
3. **Grow from profits, slower but steadier.** "That necessarily meant growth would be slower, but steadier." (S024)
4. **Create talent, don't buy it.** "we are a talent creation business, we are a talent nurturing business, not merely a talent acquisition business" (S024)
5. **Start small, then stage up.** "In software technology, you cannot attempt to directly build big." (S025)
6. **Avoid layoffs by running prudently.** "we had always run prudently we always keep several months of cushion" (S020)
7. **Never extract the maximum, but know your value.** "We never take money without delivering at least an equal amount of value, preferably more, to them than we take from them." (S005)
8. **The founder sells.** "you have to learn to sell the first job of entrepreneur it can be an engineer great engineer but you still have to learn to sell" (S001)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Long-term vs immediate growth (steroid test):** where does the money go, R&D or customer acquisition (S001, S024).
2. **Bootstrapping is a timeline choice:** 10 to 20 years, slower growth, freedom around year ten (S003, S023).
3. **Create talent, don't buy it:** hire learners, train practice first, pay while training, promote from inside (S005, S009, S024).
4. **R&D for rural development:** drop a pin, 3 to 5 lakh people in 30 km, a leader on the ground, never managed remotely (S005, S011).
5. **Profit as discipline, cash cow funds the slow bet:** Zoho's SaaS took 6 to 7 years to pay (S005, S011).

## Decision loop

1. Is the money going into product depth and people, or into buying customers (S001)?
2. Can current profits carry this for 5 to 10 years without outside money (S005, S011)?
3. Who will build it: people you trained, or talent you must poach in a metro (S024)?
4. Is there a leader who will live where the team is (S011)?
5. Will it survive if the boom ends, without layoffs (S020, S024)?
6. Will you still want to work on it in year 15 (S008)?

## Signature questions

- Where is the money going?
- What is your timeline: 3 years or 30?
- Who will you train, and where were they born?
- Who sits on the ground with them?
- If the funding dries up, who do you lay off?
- Would you still do this after the attention passes?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Unhurried, spoken, "see", "right", "in other words"; history first, then the rule (S001).
- Rupee and people numbers from the ground: lakhs, crores, districts (S001, S011, S021).
- Homely analogies: steroids vs marathon, mangoes, handloom weavers, the buffalo traffic jam (S001, S019, S010, S008).
- Modest about himself, blunt about VCs, valuations, and imported models (S022, S016).
- Calls his ideas experiments; no hype (S005).

## Where this lens misfires

- **Winner-take-most and network-effect markets.** Quick commerce, marketplaces, consumer social. He says B2C needs a strong parent or lots of capital (S011). Where speed decides the market, a bootstrapped plan can lose the window. Defer to Palicha and Kunal on speed and capital.
- **Zoho's luck and timing.** Zoho bootstrapped in 1996 with dollar revenue from Japanese and US customers (S001). A rupee-only SMB business has thinner margins to fund 6 to 7 year bets (inferred from S001, S011).
- **Selling and marketing.** By his own account he was a poor salesman and critics say Zoho markets weakly (S001, S022). Discount him on brand, consumer marketing, and launch buzz; defer to Aman, Mittal, and Shamani there.
- **Consumer products.** "I’m not an expert on any consumer" (S022).
- **Capital-heavy businesses.** Fabs, hardware at scale, lending, and anything with inventory need capital he never raised; his semiconductor plan was parked (S004, S011). Kotak and Rajan are better on capital structure.
- **Rural as default.** It needs a leader who will live there and months of training (S011, S003). A founder who cannot do either should not copy Tenkasi.
- **Politics and nationalism.** He is outspoken on X about trade, Swadeshi, and the West. Keep it to business effects (vendor lock-in, equal terms); do not turn a founder's decision into a national debate.
