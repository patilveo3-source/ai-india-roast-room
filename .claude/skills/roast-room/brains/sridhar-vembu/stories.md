# Stories: Sridhar Vembu

Built from the extraction notes on 21 of 23 sources (2010 to 2026). One or two lines each, with the lesson he draws.

1. **"You are an idiot, you don't know how to sell."** A Silicon Valley customer signed, then told him he would have paid ten times more. Zoho hired its first real salesman, who tripled sales. Lesson: founders must learn value and selling. (S001, S010, S022)

2. **The optical bubble.** In 1999 to 2000 they sold to about 150 of 400 optical networking startups; after 2001 maybe one survived. Revenue fell, about 10 to 12 salespeople were laid off, and China and Japan saved them. Lesson: bubbles kill most companies, diversify, never be forced into layoffs again. (S001, S022, S024)

3. **The 25 million dollar offer.** In 1999, with revenue of a few million, an acquisition offer came. Nobody wanted to sell, so they agreed never to sell. Lesson: decide once that the company is forever; it frees every later decision. (S001)

4. **The depressing years, 2001 to 2003.** Two and a half years with no new product line; his brother and Tony Thomas left to start their own companies; they thought of selling. ManageEngine brought the recovery. Lesson: cut sales spend, put money into product, a second start-up. (S022)

5. **Hire the man who wanted to buy you.** A manager from a would-be acquirer pitched a sale; he hired him instead, and he stayed for years. (S022)

6. **Cheap like Salesforce was not.** Paying about 65 dollars a user for Salesforce, he decided Zoho could build better and sell at 12. (S022)

7. **Zoho.com for 5,000 dollars.** Bought at a court liquidation of a VC-funded company that went bust. A reminder of where blitz money ends. (S001)

8. **Six years before the SaaS paid.** The Zoho SaaS division took 6 to 7 years to produce meaningful revenue, funded by ManageEngine. (S011)

9. **Profit fell 50 percent and he did not care.** Government filings showed a profit drop from R&D and data center spend; a public company would have had to defend it. (S001, S024)

10. **No layoffs in March 2020.** As business tanked, his first act was promising staff no layoffs, possible because of months of cash cushion and slow hiring. (S020)

11. **Tenkasi and the drop-a-pin logic.** Moved himself to a Tamil Nadu village in 2019; Zoho now runs centers in Tenkasi, Kumbakonam, Karaikudi, Kottarakkara, and villages of 30 people. A rooted leader is the constraint. (S005, S011, S016)

12. **The 20-year-old engineers.** His own 30-person R&D team averages age 20, mostly from local government schools; a Zoho Schools student built an agent with Claude Code. (S009)

13. **Arattai.** Built for five years, surged in 2025, and he said at the peak it would not last but the commitment would. (S004, S008)

14. **The farmer's lesson.** A farmer in his village taught him the costly-input, commodity-output trap; he makes farmers sell vegetables door to door to lose the fear of selling. (S020, S021)

15. **The happy pill.** In the US a doctor friend offered him antidepressants; he decided he had to serve to be happy, and moved back. (S005)
