# Frameworks: Sridhar Vembu

Built from 23 sources (19 talks and long interviews, 4 web interviews, 2010 to 2026), with notes extracted from 21 of them. Most are auto-captioned video, so quotes keep the caption spelling. He rarely names his frameworks, so most are marked *(unnamed)*. Order is most-used first. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated rules; they are not quotes.

---

## 1. Long-term growth vs immediate growth *(the steroid test)*
- **What it is:** Outside money is spent on sales and marketing, which buys immediate growth. R&D buys long-term growth and staying power. Typical funded SaaS spends 70 to 80 percent of revenue on sales and marketing and about 10 percent on R&D. He calls that steroids: you win the race, you damage the body, and business is a 30 to 40 year marathon.
- **Parts:** Where is the money going, R&D or customer acquisition? What share of spend builds product depth? Will the culture become month-to-month and quarter-to-quarter? Can the company still be relevant in year 30?
- **When to use:** Any plan to raise money, any growth plan led by paid acquisition, any "category leader" pitch.
- **How to score:** 5 if new money mainly deepens product and capability, and the founder can grow from profits. 3 if the money is for a real but slow-paying investment. 1 if the money mostly buys customers at a loss to hit a valuation.
- **Quote:** "if you don't invest enough in R&D, you won't have long-term growth" (S024) "it's exactly like steroid I mean you steroid you you will do well you will do well but what are you doing to your body" (S001) "are you spending it to build R&D or are you spending it just to acquire customers and it's crucial difference" (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S020, S022, S023, S024, S025).

## 2. Bootstrapping is a timeline choice
- **What it is:** Bootstrapping is not easier or harder in a given year. VC existed 30 years ago too. The question is your personal time horizon and whether you accept slower growth in return for freedom.
- **Parts:** Can you commit to a strategy for 10 to 20 years? Do you accept no fast growth early? Is the sacrifice upfront acceptable, knowing the freedom shows around year ten?
- **When to use:** "Should I raise or bootstrap?"
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder wants a 10 to 20 year company and can live with slower growth. 3 if they want speed but the market does not demand it. 1 if they want both freedom and blitz growth.
- **Quote:** "it's a question of what is your personal timeline preference" (S003) "While there’s significant sacrifice upfront, about 10 years down the line, you begin to truly value the autonomy it provides." (S023)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S011, S015, S023).

## 3. Start small, idli positive, then stage up *(multi-stage booster rocket)*
- **What it is:** You cannot build big directly. A small first product that puts food on the plate keeps you alive; each stage funds and trains you for the next. Even start at "year minus five" building knowledge and relationships.
- **Parts:** One small product that sells; profits fund the next product; years of learning before the big launch; Zoho One came in many steps.
- **When to use:** A founder planning a big platform from day one, or a pre-revenue moonshot.
- **How to score:** 5 if a small sellable first product funds the next stage. 3 if the first stage is small but not yet paying. 1 if success needs the whole platform at once.
- **Quote:** "In software technology, you cannot attempt to directly build big." (S025) "Our first product was also something that was small and put food on our plates. It kept us alive." (S025) "think of bootstrapping like a multi-stage booster rocket, where each stage propels and prepares you for the next" (S023)
- **Sources:** 4 (S015, S022, S023, S025).

## 4. Create talent, don't buy it *(talent creation business)*
- **What it is:** Never assume ready-made talent. Recruit learners (rural, no degree, unrelated backgrounds), train them in practice, pay them while training, and they stay. What comes easily leaves easily.
- **Parts:** Hire for learnability; train practice first, theory later; pay a stipend so nobody takes on debt; promote from inside; someone passionate sits on the ground with them.
- **When to use:** Hiring plans, poaching wars, "we can't find people", expanding to a new town.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan builds its own pipeline and the founder will invest months of training. 3 if it hires laterally but trains. 1 if it depends on poaching expensive ready-made talent in a metro.
- **Quote:** "we are a talent creation business, we are a talent nurturing business, not merely a talent acquisition business" (S024) "what comes easily can leave just as easily" (S023) "don't inflict theory. Teach them practice." (S009)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S005, S009, S011, S023, S024, S025).

## 5. Research and development for rural development *(R&D and RD)*
- **What it is:** R&D creates capabilities; putting them in small towns keeps the income there. One or two percent of a rural population in high-value jobs can drive the local economy. Go to where the talent is born.
- **Parts:** Drop a pin anywhere in India: 3 to 5 lakh people within 20 to 30 km. Start with 5 to 10 people. A rooted leader must live there. Cheap rent is a 5 to 10 year efficiency play.
- **When to use:** Where to locate a team or office; whether to stay in Bengaluru.
- **How to score:** 5 if the work can be done by trained local talent with a leader on the ground. 3 if it needs some metro roles. 1 if it needs a remote manager or a ready-made senior team.
- **Quote:** "research and development and rural development are two sides of the same coin" (S005) "One thing we don't accept is you can remotely manage this." (S011) "it is a long-term efficiency play" (S023)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S005, S008, S009, S011, S023, S025).

## 6. Profit as the discipline *(grounding)*
- **What it is:** With no outside money, you must be profitable to grow. That constraint stops you from expanding too fast or betting the house, and it reality-checks every idea. Free money lets you chase flights of fancy.
- **Parts:** Balance the books and make a little extra; fund experiments from profit; let customers decide if the product is good.
- **When to use:** Big bets, new divisions, any plan that only works if more money arrives later.
- **How to score:** 5 if current profits can carry the bet. 3 if it needs a short bridge. 1 if it only works with fresh capital.
- **Quote:** "This has operated to discipline us from our own tendencies to expand too rapidly or bet the house." (S005) "if you raise money, you’re forced to spend it, and you’ll lose profitability" (S024)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S020, S022, S024).

## 7. Fund the slow bet from the cash cow
- **What it is:** Zoho's SaaS division took 6 to 7 years to make meaningful revenue, carried by ManageEngine. Long R&D bets run 10 to 15 years before profit.
- **When to use:** A new product line, a new market, deep tech.
- **How to score:** 5 if an existing profitable line carries the new bet for years. 3 if the runway is 2 to 3 years. 1 if the new bet must pay for itself in a year.
- **Quote:** "that division, the Zoho division, actually took about 6 years, 7 years to even produce meaningful revenue" (S011) "We have sustained investment in technologies for ten years, 15 years, sometimes before we see a profit." (S005)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S011, S024, S025).

## 8. Bubbles: right premise, wrong companies
- **What it is:** He sold to about 150 of 400 optical startups; after 2001 maybe one survived. The technology revolution was real, but most funded companies died. Predict the future from past bubbles.
- **When to use:** A hot sector (AI, quick commerce), a valuation-led plan, hiring sprees.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan survives the money drying up. 3 if it survives with cuts. 1 if it needs the boom to last.
- **Quote:** "the premise of the technology revolution was correct. Yet the companies that were betting on it, the venture capital that went into all this, eventually went bust." (S024) "you could have in 10 years everything could be AI but most of the AI companies funded may not survive" (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S004, S012, S022, S024).

## 9. Diversify markets and products
- **What it is:** After the telecom bust he decided never to be exposed to one market. Many products, many geographies (US now about 40 percent), slow hiring, months of cash cushion.
- **How to score:** 5 if no single customer, segment, or country can sink the company. 1 if one market is everything.
- **Quote:** "you have to have a diversified mix of business that is something that was clear you cannot be exposed with all eggs in one basket" (S001) "we had always run prudently we always keep several months of cushion" (S020)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S011, S020, S022).

## 10. Serve the overlooked customer, compound up-market
- **What it is:** Do not attack the incumbent's biggest accounts. Serve the customers who cannot afford the big players, at a lower price with a better product, and over a decade the bigger ones come.
- **Quote:** "I focus on the overlooked, marginalized customers who often find the products of big players unaffordable" (S023) "I could create a better product and sell it at a lower price." (S022)
- **Sources:** 3 (S022, S023, S001).

## 11. No wallet-fracking; leave money on the table
- **What it is:** Never take more than the value you deliver. Contentment in pricing means the customer is paying enough. No big AI surcharges. But know your value: underpricing out of ignorance is a mistake, not a virtue.
- **How to score:** 5 if the price is clearly below the value delivered and sustainable. 3 if fair but aggressive. 1 if the model depends on hooks, lock-in, or extracting the most.
- **Quote:** "We never take money without delivering at least an equal amount of value, preferably more, to them than we take from them." (S005) "Contentment means that this customer is paying enough. Let's not keep asking for more." (S005) "Make sure you charge the next customer a better price, he told me." (S010)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S004, S005, S008, S009, S022).

## 12. Costly inputs, commodity output (trap)
- **What it is:** A business sinks when input costs rise and it cannot raise its price. Ask whether you earn a gross margin on every sale.
- **Quote:** "do I have costly inputs and do I have a commodity output" (S020) "are you earning a profit on every sale" (S021)
- **Sources:** 2 (S020, S021).

## 13. Own the full stack and make your own tools
- **What it is:** Own data centers, own tools, own the know-how. Control guarantees reliability and cuts cost (50 to 70 percent below public cloud). The Japanese lens makers build their own polishing machines.
- **Quote:** "If you don't control the full stack, you cannot really guarantee those things." (S009) "if you don't make the tools yourselves, you won't be very good engineer in whatever we do" (S009)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S009, S025).

## 14. The 15th-year test
- **What it is:** Hustle lasts a year or two. Ask whether you will still work on this in year 15 or 20, after the attention is gone. The only exit is death.
- **Quote:** "would you be doing this after all of the attention passed" (S008) "the success is not about that instant you know one year thing that it is about what happens in 10 15 years" (S008)
- **Sources:** 3 (S007, S008, S004).

## 15. Trade on equal terms *(self-reliance, not self-sufficiency)*
- **What it is:** Build critical technology; buy what you do not build, but only on terms that cannot be weaponized. Applied to a company: avoid dependence on one vendor, platform, or foreign capital source that can dictate terms.
- **Quote:** "What we don't build, we should be able to buy, but on equal terms." (S004) "I draw a contrast between self-sufficiency versus self-reliance" (S011)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S004, S009, S011).
