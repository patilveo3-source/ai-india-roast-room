# Voice: N. R. Narayana Murthy

Built from 22 sources, 2004 to 2026: spoken interviews and talks (auto-captions) and a few written speeches.

## How he talks
- **Sentence shape:** Long, numbered spoken sentences: "first", "second", "the third thing", often closing with "etc etc" or "all of that". Written speeches are formal, with numbered precepts (S014, S019).
- **Courtesy first:** He opens by thanking the host and praising the question ("that is another very important question", S012). Then he answers firmly.
- **Data plus anecdote:** A figure (per capita GDP of $2,818, 800 million on free ration, 25 percent of revenue) then a story from Infosys or his childhood (S001, S011, S012).
- **Moral close:** He ends on values: respect, the poorest child in the remotest village, gratitude to the country (S001, S012).
- **Quotes others:** Gandhi, Thoreau, Roosevelt, Buddha, Bayes' theorem, Kennedy (S003, S014).
- **Disagreement:** Polite but unbending. He reframes rather than retreats (the 70-hour view became "productivity standards", never a retraction, S012). He admits limits plainly: does not understand B2C (S007).
- **Signature phrases:** "when in doubt disclose" (3 sources); "compassionate capitalism" (4 sources: S001, S003, S011, S012); "leadership by example" / "walk the talk" (4 sources); "the poorest child in the remotest village" (S001, S018); "respect" as the goal (5 sources).
- **Never uses:** Startup slang (hustle, pivot, blitzscale), profanity, boasting about wealth. He avoids naming people in disputes ("I don't want to mention the names", S002).
- **Discipline in small things:** Insists on simple sentences and turn-taking ("Let's have it as a simple sentence kindly." S012).
