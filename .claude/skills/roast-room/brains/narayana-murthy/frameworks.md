# Frameworks: N. R. Narayana Murthy

Built from 22 sources (2004 to 2026): long interviews, two episodes of The Record with T.V. Mohandas Pai, fireside chats, convocation speeches, his 2004 AGM chairman's speech, a 2019 written interview, and a Computer History Museum oral history. Quick-depth build.

14 frameworks, most-used first. He names few of them, so most are *(unnamed)* patterns read across sources. Most video sources are auto-captions, so quotes keep caption spelling. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance built from his stated rules, not quotes.

---

## 1. When in doubt, disclose *(his slogan, with "the softest pillow is a clear conscience")*
- **What it is:** If you hesitate over whether to tell the board, investors, staff, or your family something, that hesitation is the answer: tell them. It is his front-page test in one line.
- **Parts:** Notice the doubt; disclose to the people with a stake; consult well-wishers before acting; accept the short-term cost.
- **When to use:** Related party deals, bad numbers, a founder's side business, a cash crunch, a values breach by a star performer.
- **How to score:** 5 if every material fact is already disclosed or will be this week. 3 if disclosure waits on some event. 1 if the plan works only while someone does not know.
- **Quote:** "The softest pillow is a clear conscience, and an equally satisfying one is, when in doubt, disclose." (S021) "when in doubt disclose that is the finest coroporate governance principle that you people can follow" (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S014, S021), 2004 to 2024.

## 2. Asymmetry of benefits *(his core governance test)*
- **What it is:** Misgovernance is when owner-managers or professional managers take benefits the other shareholders do not get. The board exists to shrink that gap.
- **Parts:** Three common forms: related party transactions that move value to a founder's private company; overpaying for an acquisition; paying top management above equally performing peers.
- **When to use:** Any deal touching a founder's other company, family, or friends; acquisitions; senior pay.
- **How to score:** 5 if no insider gains anything other shareholders do not. 3 if there is a gain but it is disclosed and approved by independent people. 1 if value flows to an insider and the minority would object if they knew.
- **Quote:** "the fundamental issue here is how do I reduce the asymmetry of benefits" (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S014, S020).

## 3. The most respected company *(his founding objective)*
- **What it is:** At Infosys's first meeting, colleagues proposed largest revenue, largest profit, or highest market cap. He chose respect, because respect from every stakeholder brings the money.
- **Parts:** Customers (deliver on time, on budget, above quality), employees (they recruit others), investors (follow governance), vendors (they extend terms in a crisis), government (fair policy), society (which supplies all of them).
- **When to use:** Any trade of long-term trust for short-term revenue.
- **How to score:** 5 if the move raises respect with every stakeholder. 3 if it gains one and costs another. 1 if it buys revenue with trust.
- **Quote:** "I want to be the most respected company in India" (S012) "Respect should be the main purpose of any company because if you are respected, revenues will come." (S012)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S007, S011, S012, S014).

## 4. Lead by example *(he calls it leadership by example)*
- **What it is:** The leader's conduct is the culture. People watch the founder every minute and copy him.
- **Parts:** Be first in (6:20 a.m. for 33 years); take less pay than others; live austerely; same conduct at home and in office.
- **When to use:** Any rule the founder wants others to follow; founder perks and pay.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder does first and most of what he asks. 1 if the founder exempts himself.
- **Quote:** "The best instrument a leader has to instill a good value system is leadership by example" (S020) "your actions speak louder than your words" (S003)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S012, S014, S020, S001).

## 5. Governance from day one *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Values are like breathing; you do not start them at a billion dollars. Build listed-company habits before you list.
- **Parts:** Quarterly statements before required; independent directors 2 years before the NASDAQ listing; transparency drills inside the team.
- **When to use:** A founder who says "we will fix governance after the round" or "it is my company, I own 90 percent".
- **How to score:** 5 if the company already reports as if listed. 1 if governance is deferred to a later stage.
- **Quote:** "it is very difficult for me to say that I will start good governance once I have become a 20 billion dollar company" (S003) "wherever there is more than one individual who is uh part of the equity pool then good corporate governance is necessary" (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S003, S007).

## 6. A board that can question you *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Invite independent directors with complementary skills who are not in awe of the founder or CEO.
- **Parts:** No executive on audit or remuneration committees; a one-week induction; pair each independent with an executive director; peer evaluation; term limits; pay at global benchmarks to attract real talent. The chairman's character decides everything.
- **When to use:** Forming a board, adding investors' nominees, a founder-chairman setup.
- **How to score:** 5 if at least the chair of audit can say no to the founder and has. 1 if the board is friends and family.
- **Quote:** "I have always tried to invite people on the board who could question me" (S002) "the day the CH becomes a victim of the charm of the Senior Management that's when the company starts going down" (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S020).

## 7. Never cut price below what funds quality *(unnamed; the 1995 decision)*
- **What it is:** Charge a premium for differentiated value. A discount that starves training, pay, and R&D hurts the customer too.
- **Parts:** Innovate in every function; charge 20 to 25 percent more; protect industry-leading margins; walk away from a buyer who squeezes, even a big one.
- **When to use:** A large customer demanding a cut; a plan to win on discounts.
- **How to score:** 5 if the price funds quality and margin holds. 3 if a cut is temporary and costed. 1 if the discount makes good people, training, or quality unaffordable.
- **Quote:** "if I were to reduce the prices for you, actually you will suffer because we will not be able to invest enough in attracting good employees" (S012) "charge 20% to 25% higher prices, and obtain industry-leading operating margins" (S020)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S008, S012, S020).

## 8. Entrepreneurial phase, then managerial phase
- **What it is:** Two stages with different needs.
- **Parts:** Phase one: one leader, quick decisions, courage, sacrifice, compete on the giants' weaknesses. Phase two: professional managers, processes, independent board, quarterly results.
- **When to use:** Deciding whether to add process, a board, or managers.
- **How to score:** 5 if the company's stage and its structure match. 1 if a scaling company still runs on one founder's head, or a 5-person startup runs committees.
- **Quote:** "in the entrepreneurial phas there is very little money you need lot of Courage you need to make lot of sacrifices" (S002) "every startup company must have only one leader there cannot be two leaders" (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S003).

## 9. Size the market first *(unnamed; the Softronics lesson)*
- **What it is:** His first company failed because there was no domestic software market. Check the market cheaply before you build.
- **Quote:** "The biggest mistake I committed was not ascertaining the size of the market" (S022) "the entrepreneur must conduct an inexpensive uh market survey whether there is a market in his or her milieu for his or her idea" (S011)
- **How to score:** 5 if paying customers are proven. 1 if the plan assumes demand.
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S011, S022).

## 10. Compete on their weakness *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A small firm cannot beat giants on their strengths. Find what they cannot answer and win there, usually with more homework.
- **Quote:** "the better thing is to compete on your strength and compete on their weaknesses" (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S003). The 1982 Data General pitch: a 20-minute talk ran eight hours.

## 11. One new culture at a time *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Never bring in an outside chairman and an outside CEO together. Someone in one seat must carry the old values.
- **Quote:** "only one person can come from a new culture either the chairman or the CEO but not both" (S002) "Unfortunately, it did not happen in Infosys." (S022)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S022).

## 12. Cash and capital discipline *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Keep cash; earn well above the cost of capital; buy only companies that fit.
- **Parts:** ROCE at least twice the cost of capital, ROIC three times; dividends capped at 20 percent of net income (2004); acquisitions need complementary revenue, respected management, a similar value system, and a path to your margins.
- **Quote:** "The real happiness in a company is cash in the bank." (S021) "it must have an enduring value system similar to Infosys" (S021)
- **Sources:** 2 (S007, S021).

## 13. Five to seven levers *(unnamed; his metrics)*
- **What it is:** Run the business on a handful of numbers: revenue growth, utilisation, staff pyramid, gross margin, per-capita revenue and after-tax dollars, cost per innovation, attrition, brand, compliance. Bottom line first.
- **Quote:** "Every business has about 5 to 7 levers that you can tweak to achieve your objectives." (S020) "for me bottom line is the most important entity" (S007)
- **Sources:** 2 (S007, S020).

## 14. Productivity standards, not hours (2026 version of the 70-hour view)
- **What it is:** Set output standards per role; a shorter week is fine if output rises 5 to 8 percent a year. For himself and leaders, the bar is still very long hours.
- **Quote:** "as long as outcomes are important rather than the time you spend I have no objection" (S012) "when I say hard work, it does not mean go and sit and sleep in the office" (S012)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S012).
