# Stories: N. R. Narayana Murthy

Built from 22 sources, 2004 to 2026. The stories he retells, with the lesson he draws.

1. **The common salt (school, Mysore).** His headmaster measured school salt carefully but offered a boy a jar of his own salt free. Lesson: community property is sacred; it is the root of his governance. (S003, S012)
2. **The Fortune 10 client, 1995.** The client played Indian vendors room against room at the Taj Residency for price cuts. Against all his colleagues, he declined and lost 25 percent of revenue two years after listing, then rebuilt it. "everybody told me that it was the most risky decision that I took" (S012) Lesson: never discount below what funds quality. (S003, S012)
3. **Most respected, not largest.** At the founding meeting, colleagues wanted top revenue, profit, or market cap; he chose respect. (S012)
4. **The values breach.** A senior executive's misconduct; investors urged him to keep the man for revenue and share price. He flew back from London and parted ways. Lesson: never be beaten by fear. (S002)
5. **Softronics.** His first company failed in under a year: no domestic software market. Lesson: size the market first. (S003, S022)
6. **The eight-hour pitch, 1982.** A 20-minute technical talk to win a data centre deal ran eight hours; rivals could not answer his questions. Lesson: compete on the giants' weaknesses. (S002, S003)
7. **6:20 a.m. for 33 years, and the toilets.** He never lectured on punctuality; he arrived first. He inspected toilets so janitors felt their work mattered. (S003, S012)
8. **The leaker.** A non-founder who leaked quarterly results was quietly kept out of the room, without losing face. (S003)
9. **Pizza after a bad day.** On the night of the 1995 decision he took his children out; parents do not panic in front of a sick child. (S003)
10. **The ATM and the janitor, 1995.** The machine treated the janitor and the engineer the same. Lesson: technology is a leveller. (S001)
11. **Campus mind-share, 1994 to 1999.** From zero to number one among engineering graduates through strategy and discipline. (S001)
12. **Licence Raj.** 30 to 50 trips to Delhi to import one computer; a government officer priced the IPO. (S001, S022)
13. **Satyam, 2009.** A founder inflated revenue and profits for years to hold the stock price. He uses it as the warning case. (S014)
14. **Outsider CEO.** After the co-founders, an outsider came in and the culture balance was not kept on the board. (S022)
15. **The cleaner's three questions.** Faster, cheaper, better tomorrow than today. (S009)
16. **Staff apartments.** Built homes for his cooks and drivers identical to his own. (S012)
