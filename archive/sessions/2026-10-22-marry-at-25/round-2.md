# Round 2: Debate

## Buffett

SEAT: Buffett
I DISAGREE MOST WITH: Bezos. His line: the talks get you past 70 percent in 3 months, so marry at month 6. My rebuttal: talks are projections, and I don't buy projections. I buy demonstrated earning power (S056, S063). A talk tells you what your partner plans to do in a bad month. Only a bad month tells you what they actually do. Moving the wedding from month 6 to month 9 costs zero dollars, and you save about $3,000 more along the way. Marrying the wrong person through a one-way door costs half the farm plus lawyers. You don't buy a farm because the owner described the harvest well. You walk the fields through one bad season.

I CONCEDE: Hormozi. A wedding under $10K, paid in cash, with 6 months of reserves left (about $24,000 at $4,000 a month) takes away my cash-ruin objection completely. What's left of my case is about information, not money. The weakest part of my case is my own rule: "if we can't make a decision in five minutes, we can't make it in five months." (S010) If they've been together 1 to 3 years, extra months may add less than I'm claiming.

ON THE CRUXES:
1. A cheap, cash-paid A beats a long engagement only if they have lived on one budget first. So I shorten B. I don't drop it.
2. Talking settles which questions matter. Time settles the answers. My minimum track record is two full quarters on one shared budget, including one month that goes wrong.
3. Write this rule down now. Each partner lists three deal-breakers. If any one shows up, the date moves back 90 days, and nobody pays a nonrefundable deposit over $1,000 until the checks pass. "The most important thing to do when you find yourself in a hole is to stop digging." (S063)

## Bezos

SEAT: Bezos

I DISAGREE MOST WITH: Buffett. His argument: watch how they behave over time, so you need 12 to 18 months of lived record before the merger. My rebuttal: they already have 1 to 3 years of record. What's missing is the talks and the numbers, and you can learn those in weeks. Fifteen more months on top of that is waiting for 90 percent: "If you wait for 90%, in most cases, you're probably being slow." (S064) Here is A at its strongest: the talks are written down by January 22, the wedding is in April, costs under $10K paid in cash, and six months of expenses are still in the bank. That is a bet with a capped downside on a person they have already chosen.

I CONCEDE: Buffett and Leila are right that a shared budget that survives a bad month is lived data, not talk data. My own frame also works against A. The 70 percent rule covers "most decisions." One-way doors "must be made methodically, carefully, slowly, with great deliberation and consultation." (S063) What this changes: I keep a minimum stretch of lived proof before the date. It is measured in months, though, not 18 of them.

ON THE CRUXES:
1. Strong A beats a padded B but loses to a tight B. Regret of omission doesn't favor A either: getting engaged now already commits to the person (S043). Three months of talks plus three months on a shared budget is too thin for a one-way door.
2. Talks settle the facts you can know, in 3 months. Six months on a shared budget, living together or close to it, shows how they actually behave. That is 9 to 12 months total, not 18.
3. Write the rule and sign it now. Any red flag pauses the date automatically. Red flags: undisclosed debt, a no on kids, or a budget missed 2 months out of 3. Either partner can pause without having to argue for it. Pay no nonrefundable deposit until every check passes, because deposits turn the engagement into a fake two-way door.

## Leila

SEAT: Leila

I DISAGREE MOST WITH: Hormozi. His line is "You don't need more time. You need the checks." That treats talks as proof. Talks are self-report. In hiring, values checked by self-report score 1 out of 5. "it's not asking a question do you have this value it's getting someone to show you if they behave in a way that exemplifies that value" (S002). A couple can agree on a budget in two conversations. Whether they actually keep it after a bad month only shows up over time. "if you give feedback to somebody the behavior doesn't change the feedback was not feedback" (S011). Months 4 to 9 are when the agreements get tested against real behavior.

I CONCEDE: My own Round 1 logic cuts against my 18 months. Once the talks are done and six months of shared budget and living together are observed, months 10 to 18 add time but no information. That's the same flaw I called out in C. And "the only wrong answer is doing nothing" (S029, the newer view). This changes my timeline. I'm shortening B to 9 to 12 months.

ON THE CRUXES:
1. A small cash wedding at month 6 beats an 18-month engagement. It does not beat a 9-month one, because month 6 is too early to have seen a full behavior record.
2. The talks settle stated expectations in 3 months. Only lived months show whether the agreements hold. You need about six months of that.
3. Written rule: no non-refundable deposit until every check passes. If a check fails, the date moves automatically. "if you don't agree, don't move forward." (S008)

## Hormozi

SEAT: Hormozi

I DISAGREE MOST WITH: Buffett. His line: "a year on one budget" before you sign. My rebuttal: time on its own isn't information. Tests are. A budget runs in monthly cycles, so three months on one budget gives you three cycles plus the four talks. And his ruin math depends on his own $25K wedding. Here's A at full strength on combined household numbers (my assumptions): $36K saved between them, $2K a month of new savings, $6K a month in shared expenses. At month 6 that's $48K. Pay $10K cash for the wedding and $38K is left, which is more than the 6-month reserve of $36K. Cut the price of the wedding and his ruin case goes away. Also, "Speed is one of the best all-time competitive advantages." (S115)

I CONCEDE: Buffett and Leila are right about the events that carry the most information: a bad month, a real fight, an overdraft. Those don't happen often, and three months may not contain a single one. My own rule cuts against me here: "what presents as volatility is typically a symptom of low volume." (S052). Ninety days of good months is a small sample. So A can't win on talks alone. It still needs a stress test.

ON THE CRUXES:
1. A small cash wedding at month 6 is as good as B if the gate passes. It doesn't clearly beat B, because B buys more volume.
2. Talks and numbers settle money, kids, and city in 3 months. How someone acts under stress needs more months, or a stress test you set up on purpose.
3. Put the rule in writing before the ring: no nonrefundable deposits until the gate passes. Any undisclosed debt, a disagreement on kids, or going over budget in 2 of 3 months moves the date automatically. Change the terms, not the commitment: "I never discount but I will change terms." (S046)
